> Hichkas’s 2006 debut album, Jangal-e asfalt (Asphalt Jungle) fused hip-hop rhythms with classical instruments such as the santour, giving the genre a distinctive local flavour.

> It featured the hit track Ekhtelaf (Disparity), summing up the dog-eat-dog world of the capital city with a lyricism worthy of the country’s centuries-old poetic heritage: “This is Tehran/A city that tempts you till it saps your soul/And makes you see you were always meant to be/Nothing more than dirt.”

> Unlike “wannabe” US gangsta rappers, Hichkas cultivated a home-grown style, dressing like any other guy on the city streets, drawing his themes from everyday life. His music and his punchy but profanity-free lyrics alluded to national pride and an old-fashioned code of honour.

> In the video for 2008′s Yeh mosht sarbaz (A bunch of soldiers) the rapper stands with his gang, rapping about solidarity and survival in the mean streets. “The flag is flying high,” he raps, beside Iranian flags flapping in the wind. He is ready to lay down his life for “God, nation, family and friends”.

> By 2010 Hichkas had fled the country, dropping the anthemic Yeh ruz-e khub miyad (A good day will come) as his flight took off from Mehrabad Airport. According to testimony in Siamdoust’s Soundtrack of the Revolution, Hichkas had been nudged to leave by the regime.
