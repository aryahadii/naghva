# Mohsen Sharifian

The Bushehr ensemble and its founder have separate identities. The group used the longer Persian name مروارید لیان in its own news archive. Tirgan documents the 1993 formation. Thomas Gennaro’s 2013 festival report records a seven-person stage lineup, with the nay-anban player pictured separately from the group in Kuching. The original group archive and Apple Music and Spotify were checked under both credited English names. [Group archive](https://morwaridlian.blogfa.com/) · [Tirgan](https://tirgan.ca/nowruz2021/artist-guest/mohsen-sharifian/) · [Concert report](https://asianitinerary.com/mohsen-sharifian-and-the-lian-band/)

The Iranian release chronology precedes the international digital dates for several albums. Payam Cassette’s first two editions and the Parvaz edition of Bandari have identifiable titles and years. Full original cassette track lists remain unverified. The Apple Music track pages, Spotify editions, Persian Navaak track lists and artist’s video channel were checked separately. Videos of a workshop, an instrument unveiling and events with uncertain performer credits are excluded. Identity of different filmed or recorded masters remains open where the issuing artist gives only a title. [Original issue list](https://morwaridlian.blogfa.com/1390/01) · [Domestic catalogue](https://navaak.com/artist/mohsen-sharifian) · [Video archive](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCbYbOYHJ_7rmiOTiZ-Rr3-g)

Mahoor identifies the field research, recording and compilation work separately from the musicians captured on tape. The recording and compiler credits apply to the documented discs. The original names of local performers on individual selections and the complete sequence for all four Ahl-e Matam discs remain unresolved.
