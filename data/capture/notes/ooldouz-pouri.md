# Ooldouz Pouri

Tabriz-born Azerbaijani-Iranian singer based in Toronto. The solo album and original artist channel establish an independent recording practice. The artist interview in Persian traces migration and singing in the family. The CrossPlay portrait is the artist photograph explicitly named by Across Oceans Arts. The Aga Khan Museum identifies her own band Ooldouzlar and the separate trio Mahni.

The catalogue reconciles the Norwegian label’s 2016 and 2018 editions, physical 2018 track list, Apple Music, Spotify, all 17 artist-issued videos and three distributor uploads. The two studio renditions each of “Heyf” and “Yaraliam” differ in length on the original issues, and the singer’s two much shorter personal films have been retained separately. The Zurich, Tehran or other venues of her uncaptioned personal clips are unknown. The San Francisco chorus film of Sahba Aminikia's work explicitly credits Kronos Quartet and the San Francisco Girls Chorus, and the composer credits Pouri and Mina Momeni for the two recorded voices. The chamber project listing for the Brooklyn Youth Chorus uses a different performing lineup. The Toronto clarinetist Yalda Ehsani separately published a live rendition of “Neja Unudum Sani.” Radio Khiaban credits Pouri as the singer in its episode’s closing segment. The Banff recital programme lists “Kuçelere su sepisham” and “Hardasan yaar” without establishing an issued recording.

Ali Massoudi’s 227-upload channel contains five numbered Mahni concert edits in May–July 2023. One identifies Small World Music Center, the band and guest clarinetist Selcuk Suna. Four identifies composer Parviz Meshkatian. Five identifies the song “Se Godaar.” Two’s written description says “Ay Qiz,” but the film’s own title card says “Yalgizam Song.” The publisher’s numbering therefore identifies these five cuts without guessing the underlying songs. The singer’s independently edited March footage has different running times and framing. Her 2019 “Yalgizam yalgiz” is clearly a different performance, at Stallet. The 2024 Society Clubhouse trio programme has a different pianist and clarinetist and is distinct from the Mahni films.
