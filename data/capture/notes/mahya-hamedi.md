# Mahya Hamedi

The Iranian singer identifies her Tehran birthplace and describes composing a play with Diba Beihaghi, winning a scholarship to Berklee and working in music after graduation. Her original SoundCloud credits the independent recordings, and early Apple singles identify Hamedi herself as their rights holder. Her artist portrait on Spotify was visually compared to other named images. Her verified New York interview and the 2025 Persian account in Radio Zamaneh supply biographical context.

Four singles on the principal Spotify and Apple profiles, two guest singles and two further guest EPs were reconciled track by track. The EPs credit her on the first two movements of Ferran Rico’s Once bitten and only Solitude on Barış Kadem’s Aşk ile. A second distributor profile has the Bluebyrde single on YouTube and SoundCloud. The four uploads on Hamedi’s own SoundCloud and both personal YouTube videos were checked against those issued copies and the collaborator sources. The original Dariush Azar Telegram post remains on the surviving Spring’s Tear record. No personal Telegram or independently issued Bandcamp release could be identified. Fan uploads without evidence of a different performance were excluded. The own-site URL supplied in interviews now returns a domain landing placeholder from this location; the singer’s 2025 interview, original festival files, Berklee programmes, Radio Zamaneh and actual recording credits provide the biographical claims.

Two Track’s advance listing scheduled Lalaei but the issued episode has a timed Woman, Life, Freedom medley. The aired recording and research paper identify the Daughters of Cyrus introduction. The Berklee performance-centre headphone show, filmed artist performance of Florentine, original campus studio episode and separate Let Her Sing versions are distinguished. Source credits also establish her frame drum on both pieces in Bahar Badieitabar’s 2022 Two Track, the recorded two-song folk-festival set, the Memoried Quartet recording, the Azmand Quartet, the Shiraz Ensemble, Afarin Nazarijou and Rahovi Ensemble’s complete Damsaaz concert (including instrumental sections). Programme attribution distinguishes a stage concert from a separately issued song. The 2024 Emruz performance film identifies her as pianist on her own composition. The original publisher names Hamedi and pianist Ava Nazar at the January 2024 Brooklyn premiere of Dawn. Its embedded film is a later performance with a different vocalist. The Shiraz Ensemble archive contains three separately titled excerpts from March 2024 and a PhillyCAM broadcast. Dodicilune credits her in the main ensemble on When time slipped away. The press sheet does not individually identify wordless singing on its instrumental cuts. The original Berklee concert programme credits her as alto, section leader and score preparer on the Pletenitsa Balkan Choir programme, which lists Kondas and I Should Care. Its Vimeo page has become unavailable at the time of review.
