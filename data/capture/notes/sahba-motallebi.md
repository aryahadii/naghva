# Sahba Motallebi

Motallebi describes beginning as a setar student in Sari, the teacher who helped her gain admission to Tehran’s conservatory as a Baháʼí girl, and how teaching tar and setar paid her living expenses from sixteen. Her conservatory and national-orchestra years precede her composition studies in Russia, workshops in Turkey and California degree. The 2018 interview gives her national-orchestra year as 1376 (1997–98), whereas the Smithsonian and two festival programmes give 1999. Both dates are preserved here. The article leaves the year open. Her own account of composing “Birth” after the birth of her second child connects the Smithsonian performance with the specific work played at the White House. [Original interview](https://kayhan.london/1397/05/07/124707/) · [Smithsonian programme and audio](https://asia-archive.si.edu/podcast/persian-music-classical-and-new/) · [White House pool report](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/pool-reports-march-20-2023).

The native Apple artist listing, label editions and Spotify albums were compared at track level. Mahoor’s *Introducing Young Performers* identifies Motallebi on “Bayat Esfahan.” The 2018 *Fandango at the Wall* credits her on “Tabla Rasa” and the 2022 New York album on “Fly Away.” The original performer’s SoundCloud account has 76 uploads. The published track descriptions identify the instrumentalists, vocalists, engineers and poets. The multi-part Shahnazi recording was matched on part number, mode and duration, retaining spoken recollections and instrumental tracks separately. The acoustic *Solace of the Eyes* titles align in reverse upload order with the nine-track distributor release. *Tranquility* and *Conjuring* also have artist-issued SoundCloud sets. Some native track dates are earlier than the venue’s album date or the later digital issue. The venue names *Dashti-Nava* and the two *Ever Lasting Songs of Iran* volumes, whose complete original track lists have not been verified. Neither the label nor the running time repeated under every record on the present artist website was used. The artist-channel census contained 1,129 regular uploads, 115 shorts and 171 overlapping playlists. Her own concert, improvisation and composition playlists were opened as independent cross-checks. The catalogue identifies concert cuts, finished performances, the two *Secret Between Me and the Tar* volumes and the relevant masterclass, instructional repertoire and book playlists. Her recording of the radif of Mirza Abdollah has a full native playlist and shorter mode- and lesson-level subdivisions. The corresponding complete course is linked once. Its constituent fingering exercises, alternate speeds and fragments are available through that link. News interviews and instrument-maintenance instructions fall outside the recorded music catalogue. There were 934 retrievable posts in the artist’s Telegram archive. Identifiable otherwise unpublished concert excerpts, full demonstrations and exact duplicate streams were checked by original captions, edition, named musicians, place and duration. Several posts have only a mode or an unnamed part number, precluding a confident performance match. Bandcamp searches in both scripts returned no verified artist-issued release. [Artist YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@Sahbamusic) · [Artist Telegram](https://t.me/s/Sahbamusic).

Independent archives include the Kennedy Center’s Millennium Stage film, the expanded 2020 Kronos Quartet performance of “Salam Aleykom,” and the Smithsonian’s 2017 concert with Naghmeh Farahmand. The museum provides one continuous audio file and an ordered programme. Farahmand’s solo daf track is only hers. The museum’s notes describe both Motallebi’s and Darvish Khan’s chaharmezrabs; the programme explicitly assigns its opening track to Darvish Khan. The Smithsonian’s programme and technical credits both record the concert date as March 5, 2017, while its generic player displays March 11, 2018. The record does not infer an issue date from that conflicting player label.
