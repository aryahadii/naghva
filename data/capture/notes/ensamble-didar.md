# Ensamble Didar

The Mexican Fine Arts Institute dates Didar to 2011. It is distinct from Moshtagh’s ensemble of setar students, his chamber group Parizad, and recent Asatir concerts. His two early discs and Mexican solo album Tarde y Lejos are not Didar records. Checked the director’s 159 public videos, founding member Manuel Sauceverde’s 12 uploads, the festival and broadcaster, and digital store, SoundCloud and Bandcamp searches; no original commercial group album was located. The leader’s own photo from Reng Mahour shows the musicians on stage. The 13-minute group retrospective appears with an identical duration on Mona Art and the director’s channel. The festival’s minute-long introduction and the CENART teaser are promotional. Sauceverde’s six-minute concert recording resembles the director’s Shur film but has no named piece on its native page, so no composer or title was carried across.

Canal 23 registers its programme MW-20138 on 25 May 2017 and explicitly cites its own YouTube stream. The June concerts at the same hall are announced separately, and the advertised set list differs from the final broadcast cue sheet. Broadcast running time 71:49 versus YouTube archive 97:09 with the stream pre-roll. The original cue sheet identifies, in order, a setar prelude, setar/tombak duet, Tasnif Mashrute, Roya-e Shur, Harvayeh, Reng Afshari, Solo Tar Bayat e Esfahan, Raz o Niaz, Solo de setar, Reng-e Nava, Tasnif-Ey-Atash, Solo Tar Nava, Tasnif-e Raftam dare meykhaneh, another Reng-e Nava, and Delshodegan. Each of the fifteen cue-sheet pieces has its own recording entry, with the two Reng-e Nava cues distinguished by timing. Only the complete programme carries the full-stream URL.
