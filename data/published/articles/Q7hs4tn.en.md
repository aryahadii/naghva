Mahan Mirarab is an Iranian-Austrian guitarist, composer, arranger and producer based in Vienna and Berlin. Born and raised in Tehran, he began playing music at 15.[^identity][^tehran]

In Tehran, Mirarab learned with a small jazz community that pooled money to make its own conservatory and invited Armenian teacher Vahagn Hayrapetian to teach improvisation.[^community][^teacher] Cassette tapes were his teachers: he listened closely, slowed recordings and transcribed jazz solos.[^cassette][^transcription]

He moved to Europe in 2009. In Vienna, he sold his amplifier to fund a recording, then met bassist Robert Jukic and drummer Wolfi Rainer, who supported him and recorded his first album there. He later met [Golnar Shahyar](/en/golnar-shahyar-5j4/), and they began collaborating.[^europe][^amplifier][^vienna-contacts][^vienna-support][^first-album][^collaboration]

Mirarab describes his music as contemporary and rooted in the many cultures of Iran, and has spoken of microtonality as a different tuning system.[^roots][^microtonality] His fretless-guitar practice connects to Ney ornaments in classical Iranian music. He sought out musicians from different regions and tried to relearn classical Iranian repertoire; the double-neck guitar began as a way to travel more easily with both instruments.[^ney][^regional-music][^double-neck]

## Selected recordings

### Unspoken

ACT introduces [*Unspoken*](#unspoken-nm6) as an album of personal stories. Mirarab performs solo on the double-neck guitar, joined on some tracks by Kian Soltani, Lars Danielsson and Golnar Shahyar.[^unspoken-title][^unspoken-lineup]

### Say Your Most Beautiful Word

The interview around [*Say Your Most Beautiful Word*](#say-your-most-beautiful-word-hsc) describes the first time Mirarab had space to prepare emotionally as well as musically before recording. He calls hope a beautiful word and says risk is needed for development.[^say-title][^mica-prepare][^mica-hope][^mica-risk]

### Persian Side of Jazz

In his first year in Vienna, Mirarab focused on a trio called *Persian Side of Jazz*. [*Persian Side of Jazz, Vol. 2*](#persian-side-of-jazz-vol-2-7fw) carries that title forward in his catalogue.[^vienna-psj][^psj-vol2]

## Collaborations

Mirarab’s official discography places Sakina Teyna’s *Sûret - Faces and Images*, Mash’s *Naked 7*, and Sakina & Friends’ *Longing* in its “As Bandleader, Composer & Arranger” section; Apple Music gives the last album the full title *Bendewarî / İntizar / Longing*.[^discography-section][^discography-suret][^discography-naked][^discography-longing][^longing-title] The album billing remains Sakina Teyna, Mash and Sakina & Friends respectively.[^suret-artist][^naked-artist][^longing-artist]

Apple Music credits Mirarab as composer on “Neyare Bedewiye” from *Sûret - Faces and Images*. It lists him and András Dés as songwriters on Mash’s “Naked 7,” and Mirarab and Sakina Teyna as songwriters on “Migration” from *Bendewarî / İntizar / Longing*.[^neyare-title][^neyare-composer][^naked-title][^naked-mahan][^naked-andras][^migration-title][^migration-album][^migration-mahan][^migration-sakina]

## Guitar work

His official guitarist listing includes records by artists in his musical circle: [*Tear Drop*](/en/golnar-shahyar-5j4/) by Golnar Shahyar, [*Damahi*](/en/damahi-ep6/#damahi-d3a) by Damahi, and [*Avareh*](/en/hamzeh-yeganeh-373/#avareh-feat-kaveh-sarvarian-5bm) by Hamzeh Yeganeh.[^guitarist-section][^tear-drop][^damahi][^avareh]

## Derakht

His work with Golnar Shahyar also took a shared form in [*Derakht*](#derakht-m6d), released as Golnar & Mahan. Its Bandcamp description points to Persian music, American jazz, Latin styles and Afro-Cuban polyrhythms.[^derakht][^derakht-sound]
