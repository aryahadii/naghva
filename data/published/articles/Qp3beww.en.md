Salim Ghazi Saeedi is an Iranian composer and guitarist born in Tehran in 1981. He wrote three instrumental albums for the rock band Arashk before releasing records under his own name. He recorded much of this music in his home studio, playing the instrumental parts himself. He later moved to Australia while writing *United Ubiquity of Flesh*.[^birth][^band][^studio][^solos][^move]

## Guitar and the Tehran studio

Saeedi bought his first guitar in 1999 and taught himself to play. In 2004 he joined Arashk. He learnt recording alongside guitar, and his home studio eventually took on commercial work. On *Abrahadabra*, he used instrumental pieces to tell stories through a succession of mental pictures. For *Sovereign*, he turned to Ferdowsi’s *Shahnameh*. When his bandmates made the vocal album *Yell*, he played rhythm guitar and worked as its recording engineer.[^guitar][^band][^studio][^first][^epic][^yell]

He also edited sound and music for two plays directed by Amir Reza Koohestani, *Amid the Clouds* and *Recent Experiments*.[^theatre]

## One musician in several roles

In 2010 Saeedi released *Iconophobic*, an instrumental album about the fear of images. A collaboration with oud player Negar Bouban took a different path. She brought him a piece she had already composed, and he improvised against it on electric guitar. They released the result in 2011 as *When There Is More Beauty in the Contrary*.[^solos][^fear][^oud][^improv]

On *namoWoman* he set Persian quarter tones alongside Western intervals. He also wrote the booklet in a language he had constructed, which he called Babel. In 2017 he released *United Ubiquity of Flesh*. Written during his move to Australia, the album alternates passages of hard-rock guitar and drums with solo guitar played in free time.[^tone][^book][^year][^move][^space]
