The Casualty Process grew out of an electronic rock band in Tehran called The Plastic Wave. After an underground concert in 2007, guitarist Shayan Amini, singer Maral Afsharian and musician Saeid “Natch” Nadjafi spent fifteen days in prison. When they could make music again, Nadjafi had a computer to work with. He began building electronic tracks, and Afsharian sang on the album they gave away online, *[RE]action*.[^stanford-concert][^stanford-term][^stanford-computer][^stanford-maral][^archraw-album][^archraw-voc]

In March 2010 a Brooklyn audience heard The Plastic Wave’s songs live while the musicians themselves watched from Iran over Skype. The New York band Cruel Black Dove played the set. Afsharian subsequently left, and Nadjafi renamed the project The Casualty Process. He and Amini moved to New York in April 2011. That July they released *[UN]even*, with Natch, Setareh and Amini sharing the vocal credits.[^iran-cbd][^stanford-skype][^ny-newname][^ny-move][^bcun-even-issued][^bcun-even-credits]

Amini left in late 2012, and singer and guitarist Jordan Miller joined. In March 2015 Amini played alongside Nadjafi again at Spectrum in New York, with Austin Dacey on guitar and vocals.[^freedom-stage] On “Violet Machine,” from the 2015 EP *It Takes Time*, Miller wrote and sang the words to Nadjafi’s music. In 2020 Nadjafi returned to the streets of New York for “Night/Lust/Vacancy,” a song he traced to a time of vulnerability in the city at night.[^fly-left][^fly-miller][^bcit-takes-time-issued][^vefwxykqzhyw-lyrics][^vefwxykqzhyw-music][^vhonxtabu7cm-issued][^vhonxtabu7cm-night]
