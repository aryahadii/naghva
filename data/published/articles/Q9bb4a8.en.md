Niyaz is a musical project built around the voice of [Azam Ali](/en/azam-ali-m7d/) and the instruments of [Loga Ramin Torkian](/en/loga-ramin-torkian-ce3/). Founded in 2004, it brings Persian and neighboring musical traditions, Sufi poetry and electronic production into songs made for both listening and dancing.[^bio][^dance]

## The early trio

The first album, [*Niyaz*](#niyaz-m3n), appeared in 2005. Ali had sung with Vas, Torkian had played with Axiom of Choice, and producer and remixer Carmen Rizzo joined them as the third member.[^original-niyaz][^early-azam][^early-loga][^early-rizzo] Ali and Torkian wanted to move beyond the intimate acoustic setting of their traditional music toward a more outward-facing sound. Rizzo’s electronic production helped them set Rumi and Urdu mystical poetry in a contemporary dance setting.[^dance]

[*Nine Heavens*](#nine-heavens-epy), released in 2008, presented this approach across two discs, pairing electronic and acoustic versions of the material.[^original-nine-heavens][^double] It drew on Amir Khosrau’s poetry and older Iranian folk songs. Torkian saw the poet’s movement between Persian words and Indian melodies as an earlier example of the cultural exchange the group pursued.[^dehlavi]

[*Sumud*](#sumud-fzj) followed in 2012. The 2013 [*Sumud Acoustic EP*](#sumud-acoustic-ep-4kc) combined three acoustic versions of its songs with three new pieces. On “Naseem,” Ali sings alongside Habib Meftah Boucheri’s self-made flute.[^original-sumud][^acoustic-year][^acoustic-scope][^naseem]

## Building a stage work

Ali and Torkian moved from Los Angeles to Montreal in 2009 and began considering a multimedia project.[^montreal] Their 2015 album [*The Fourth Light*](#the-fourth-light-kzw), inspired by the poet and mystic Rabia al-Basri, became the basis of a stage production developed with visual artist Jérôme Delapierre. First performed in Montreal in 2016, it used projected imagery drawn from Persian calligraphy, arches and sacred geometry. Translucent curtains created layers of images, while the visuals responded to music and performers’ movement. The group continued revising the materials and technical design after the early shows.[^date][^stage-start]

In September 2026, [*Seven Realms of Attar*](#seven-realms-of-attar-jkk) extended their work into a studio album and theatrical project inspired by Attar’s *Conference of the Birds*. Sinan Cem Eroglu joined the recording on kopuz, kaval and voice.[^attar-date][^attar-work][^attar-credits] Visual artist Theron Pray and choreographer Jody Sperling collaborated on the stage production, which KCRW announced for October at Royce Hall in Los Angeles.[^attar-stage][^attar-royce]
