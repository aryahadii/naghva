Chaartaar is an Iranian band formed in 2011 by singer Arman Garshasbi, composer Arash Fathi, poet and lyricist [Ehsan Haeri](/en/ehsan-haeri-r62/) and arranger [Aeen Ahmadifar](/en/aeen-ahmadifar-zb5/). They bring Persian singing and newly written lyrics into electronic arrangements, with each member contributing to the songs as they take shape.[^formed][^members][^electronic][^method] They began by sharing songs online, made their debut album [*Baaraan Toee*](#baaraan-toee-9rf) in 2014, and followed it with two more albums and a series of singles.[^online][^debut][^second][^third][^adineh]

## Forming the group

The group grew out of friendships. Garshasbi knew Fathi and Haeri, while Fathi knew Ahmadifar. After they played and sang together, Fathi made a melody. Ahmadifar gave it an electronic arrangement, and Garshasbi introduced Haeri when they needed someone to write the words.[^friends][^heard][^melody][^haeri-intro]

Their first song was “Dar Hasrate Maah.” Haeri’s first lyric did not satisfy the others. After several hours of talking with them, he rewrote it to express the group’s sensibility rather than his own alone. All four had to agree before a song went out.[^first-song][^rewrite][^agreement]

## Making the first album

For their debut, Fathi wrote the melody before Haeri wrote the words. Haeri worked within the metre and feeling of the melody, while Garshasbi chose how to sing it, including its vocal ornamentation. The division of roles left room for changes: adding the words could alter a melody, and Garshasbi shaped the vocal line through his interpretation.[^method][^metre][^feeling][^voice]

Fathi had studied Western music and approached Persian modes through that training. He said that in “Baaraan Toee” he used Western harmony to move from Dashti to Esfahan. Ahmadifar’s interest in electronic music shaped the arrangements. He had initially been reluctant to introduce instruments into these arrangements, but used the bowed kamancheh in “Hendeseh,” where he felt its sound belonged. Haeri and the others conceived the album chiefly as a collection of love songs.[^harmony][^electronic][^reluctance][^kamancheh][^romantic]

*Baaraan Toee* came out in 2014. The band then began performing at Arikeh Iranian in Tehran and took its concerts to cities including Shiraz, Rasht, Kerman and Isfahan.[^debut][^stages]

## Further albums and singles

Chaartaar released its second album, [*Jaddeh Miraghsad*](#jaddeh-miraghsad-8d3), in 2015, with the same four members writing, arranging and singing. Its third album, [*Darya Kojaast*](#darya-kojaast-mck), followed in 2018. The group had already introduced “Benevis” as a single before including it on that album.[^second][^second-roles][^second-date][^third][^third-date]

The band continued making singles after the third album. In 2019, guitarist Navid Zomorodi composed and played on [“Sedayam Bezan”](#sedayam-bezan-single-ckv), and played guitar on [“Begoo Ke Zendeim”](#begoo-ke-zendeim-single-77t).[^navid][^sedayam-date][^begoo][^begoo-date] In 2020 Chaartaar dedicated [“Iran”](#iran-single-ehm) to broken hearts, and [“Gele Kon”](#gele-kon-single-34c) to the women of Iran.[^iran][^iran-date][^gele][^gele-date]

## Garshasbi’s solo work

In December 2023, Garshasbi said his forthcoming solo album, *Az To Goftam*, was a personal project that would run alongside his work with Chaartaar.[^solo][^solo-title] The following month, Haeri said some of his lyrics had been used on the album without his agreement. Garshasbi replied that Haeri had received payment and that his authorship had been acknowledged.[^rights-haeri][^rights-garshasbi]
