Golfam Khayam is an Iranian composer, guitarist and improviser who grew up in Tehran. Her classical guitar studies led her to the United States and Switzerland, where she studied the music of Dušan Bogdanović. She later developed her own compositions from Iranian instrumental techniques and improvisation, working both as a soloist and with other musicians.[^bio][^origin][^study][^i-geneva][^s-setar][^s-tanbur]

## Studying guitar

Khayam began learning piano as a child and soon turned to classical guitar. In 2004–05 she received a scholarship to study in the United States and earned a Master of Music at the University of Cincinnati. She continued her studies in Geneva, eventually earning a master’s degree in composition.[^i-childhood][^i-usa][^study][^geneva]

She was drawn to Bogdanović’s music before studying with him. When she released [*Poet Guitar*](#poet-guitar-489), she had not yet worked with him, but wanted to devote an album to his music. That wish took her to Geneva.[^i-first-album][^i-inspiration][^i-geneva] On [*Ravi*](#ravi-v2t), she interpreted and improvised on his compositions, playing solo guitar and duets with the oud player Siavash Roshan.[^ravi]

For his *Persian Miniatures*, Bogdanović asked her for Iranian musical sources. She supplied recordings and a book of the radif, the repertoire of Iranian classical music; the pieces took their titles from Persian miniatures.[^i-miniatures]

## Finding a musical language

Khayam also studied setar. Comparing its techniques with those of the guitar led her to explore how the sounds of Iranian plucked instruments could be transferred to her own instrument.[^s-setar][^s-tanbur]

With the clarinetist Mona Matbou Riahi she formed the Naqsh Duo. They recorded [*Narrante*](#narrante-efj) in Lugano in July 2015 with Manfred Eicher and Ramin Sadighi, and released it in May 2016.[^duo][^recording][^date] They treated the album as a continuous dialogue between guitar and clarinet, placing open passages for improvisation within composed structures.[^dialogue]

By 2018 Khayam was developing a solo project that used one guitar to create the different colours she had previously explored in the duo. Although improvisation remained part of her practice, this project was mostly written out, with little improvisation in performance.[^s-solo][^s-lily]

## Writing for other instruments

Khayam brought these interests into larger ensembles. In *I am not a tale to be told*, based on a poem by Ahmad Shamlou, she used controlled improvisation to give the soloist and instrumentalists room to shape the music.[^o-poem][^o-improvisation]

Her concerto for viola, santur and ensemble draws on javab avaz, the call-and-response of Iranian music.[^h-concerto][^h-response] She also linked its opening santur pulse to her own determination to continue composing through the exhaustion of early motherhood.[^h-pulse]

Other pieces changed as she wrote for new combinations of instruments. “Lullaby,” inspired by Attar’s *Conference of the Birds*, began for solo guitar, became a movement of the string-orchestra work *Seven Valleys of Love*, and was then arranged for four violins.[^lullaby]
