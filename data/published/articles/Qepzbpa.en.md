Karvan brought together two musicians who had known each other in Norway since 1987. Javid Afsari Rad played Persian santur, and Abdulrahman Surizehi played the Balochi benju and the bowed sorud. For their 1998 concert in Oslo they were joined by Jamil Ahmad Magsi on doholak and tabla, Ghulam Muhammad on tanburag, and Majid Hesabi on tombak. The five players came from Isfahan, Balochistan and Karachi.[^meet][^1998][^rad][^ben][^tab][^tan][^tom][^land]

Afsari Rad described the concert in two parts. First the musicians played Balochi and Persian repertoire separately. Then all five improvised together on melodies from Balochistan, Iran and India. The programme ranged from Balochi trance music to Persian dastgah-e Nava, with separate solos for tabla and tombak. Etnisk Musikklubb issued the ten pieces in 2001 as *Where the Music Traditions From Baluchistan, Persia and India Meet*. The group can also be heard on the label’s 2005 compilation, which includes “Sufi Dance” and a version of “Ghazal” described as an improvisation on a Balochi folk song.[^concert][^genre][^solo][^tabla][^label][^comp][^year][^sufi][^song]
