Saba Alizadeh is an Iranian composer and kamancheh player, born in Tehran in 1983 and based in the Netherlands. He makes electroacoustic music with instruments, field recordings and archival voices, often processing the kamancheh until it is difficult to recognize.[^identity][^birth][^home][^objects][^field][^processing] His work ranges from the composed pieces on [*Scattered Memories*](#scattered-memories-rxg) and [*I May Never See You Again*](#i-may-never-see-you-again-8mc) to the improvised duos on [*Rituals of the Last Dawn*](#rituals-of-the-last-dawn-ddp).[^scattered][^2021][^ritual-improv]

## Learning and experimenting

Saba grew up in a musical family. His father, Hossein Alizadeh, plays tar and setar and composes music. Saba joined his father's ensemble Hamavayan in 2007.[^birth][^hamavayan][^hamavayan-context] He studied kamancheh with Saeed Farajpoury, Kayhan Kalhor and Hamidreza Khalatbari. Through recordings, he also learned from Bahari's playing, particularly his ornamentation and use of silence in free-tempo improvisation.[^mentors][^bahari]

He traced his interest in electronics to older Iranian radio recordings in which musicians incorporated echo into their playing. Later, at the California Institute of the Arts, he studied experimental sound with Mark Trayle and explored effects, electronics and John Cage's ideas about composition. He began treating recorded sounds and instrumental sounds alike as material that he could reshape.[^echo][^teaching][^calarts][^objects]

In 2014, he founded Noiseworks to organize experimental performances and workshops on music technology. He financed the events himself with help from friends.[^noiseworks-date][^noiseworks] His own recordings brought everyday environments into music: on [“July 8th 2015”](#۱۷-تیر-۱۳۹۴-qnb), he combined kalimba and setar with a field recording he made in a forest in Sari, northern Iran.[^field]

## Composing and recording

In 2018, Saba released *Scattered Memories* on CD in Iran. Karlrecords released a reworked version internationally in 2019 as [*Scattered Memories*](#scattered-memories-rxg).[^scattered][^iran-launch-date][^iran-launch] He built its pieces in different ways: for “Ladan Dead End,” he made the electronic parts before recording kamancheh; for “Greetings to Earthfire,” he recorded kamancheh first and added electronics afterwards.[^construction]

On his 2021 album [*I May Never See You Again*](#i-may-never-see-you-again-8mc), he worked with both planned structures and improvisation.[^2021][^planned][^improvised-touch] For [“Silences Inbetween”](#silences-inbetween-s78), he said, he extracted pauses from speeches by leading figures of the Third Reich, played them back in a Berlin water reservoir and then composed with the resulting material.[^silence-name][^silence] By contrast, [“Touch”](#touch-with-andreas-spechtl-gf6) grew from an improvised studio session with Andreas Spechtl on voice and electronics and Saba on kamancheh. He planned almost everything in [“Only Hope Breaks the Dark Waves”](#only-hope-breaks-the-dark-waves-we9) in advance.[^improvised-touch][^planned]

In 2023, he composed [“Nafir (Clamour)”](#nafir-clamour-snc), playing kamancheh with violinist Aso Kohzadi. He described its sound as a collective outcry during the uprising in Iran.[^nafir][^outcry][^protest]

## In the Netherlands

Saba moved to the Netherlands around 2023.[^noiseworks] His 2025 album [*Temple of Hope*](#temple-of-hope-bdt) responds to the Woman, Life, Freedom movement and earlier struggles in Iran. He used kamancheh, modular synthesizer and a no-input mixer, working again with Spechtl and bringing in other voices.[^hope-context][^hope-movement][^hope][^hope-guests] [“Women of Fire”](#women-of-fire-feat-sanam-maroufkhani-8fy) features the singer [Sanam Maroufkhani](/en/sanam-maroufkhani-a84/).[^sanam]

For [*Rituals of the Last Dawn*](#rituals-of-the-last-dawn-ddp), released in 2026, he recorded improvised duos with Pietro Caramelli on guitar and electronics and Liew Niyomkarn on lap steel guitar and electronics.[^ritual] He described the sessions as listening and responding without discussing a structure in advance; he later shortened the duet with Caramelli for the vinyl release.[^ritual-improv][^ritual-context][^ritual-first][^ritual-edits][^ritual] Even when he processes the kamancheh until it becomes difficult to recognize, he aims to retain Iranian music's deeper use of time, silence and tonality.[^heritage]
