Mehdi Saki (born 1974 in Ahvaz) is a singer, actor and performer who founded [Kamakan](/en/kamakan-6vg/). He sings in Arabic and Persian, drawing on the poetry and folk songs of both languages. His work combines singing with storytelling and physical performance, from Kamakan concerts to theatre built around his childhood memories.[^origins][^bio][^gesture][^childhood]

## Ahvaz and Tehran

Mehdi grew up in an Arabic-speaking home in Ahvaz and studied in a Persian-speaking environment. He later explained that growing up with both cultures made his attachment to their poetry and music inseparable. Arabic lullabies were part of his childhood; he went on to study dance and theatre in Tehran before gathering musicians around Kamakan.[^bilingual][^bio]

By November 2012, he was appearing as a guest singer and guitarist with [Pallett](/en/pallett-q53/). He sang “[Triangle](/en/pallett-q53/#triangle-feat-mehdi-saki-g6f)” on their debut album, *Mr. Violet*. Kamakan’s first [album](/en/kamakan-6vg/#kamakan-mzz) followed in 2015, with arrangements by [Makan Ashgvari](/en/makan-ashgvari-n8j/).[^pallett][^pallett-date][^triangle][^debut]

## Poetry and performance

In January 2017, while preparing new material with Kamakan, Mehdi said he wanted each album to have a different character. He described arrangements built around oud, bass and percussion, with piano central to most pieces, and spoke of adding electronic elements. Choosing poetry remained important to him: he named Rumi, Aref Qazvini, Houshang Ebtehaj and folk poetry from southwestern Iran among the material they were working with.[^process]

At that month’s Fajr Music Festival, he opened with a southern song while plucking a guitar laid across his lap. He then told a story about the beginning of the Iran–Iraq War before singing in Arabic. Recitation, repeated vocal sounds and theatrical gestures were part of the performance.[^live][^gesture]

Later in 2017, his memories of wartime Ahvaz became the basis of *The Fifth Wing*. The director Ahmad Selgi adapted Mehdi’s text *Mahoud and Tales of War* for the stage. Selgi described theatre and music as intertwined in the work: Mehdi’s singing was its central musical element, accompanied by sparse live guitar and oud.[^childhood][^text][^theatre][^voice]

## Later collaborations

In later collaborations, Mehdi brought his voice to work outside Kamakan. He sang on “[Tamam](/en/mehdi-saki-3gk/#tamam-de8)” from *Jostojoo*, a collaboration between Arif Mirbaghi and Kaveh Ghaffari inspired by Iranian popular cinema. He also sang “[Butterflies hugging each other](/en/mehdi-saki-3gk/#butterflies-hugging-each-other-vtq)” for Ava Rasti’s music for a film by Saber Abar.[^jostojoo][^butterfly]

Following a November 2023 residency at Le Grand Mix in France, Kamakan’s trio brought Mehdi’s voice, guitar and percussion together with Rasti’s bass and electronic machines and Axel Moon’s saz, rabab and electronic machines.[^ensemble] His teaching also addressed the body and voice: in May 2026, VIVA Alliance in Vancouver offered a course with him in the Suzuki method, combining movement and rhythm with voice and breath exercises.[^teaching][^course]
