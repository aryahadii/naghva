O-Hum formed in Tehran in 1378 of the Solar Hijri calendar, with Shahram Sharbaf, Shahrokh Izadkhah and Babak Riahipour.[^origins] The band combines Western rock with Iranian music and instruments, setting Hafez’s poetry to music.[^approach]

## The first trio

The band’s first album was *[Nahal-e Heyrat](#nahal-e-heyrat-remastered-qqe)*.[^debut] Its early live activity included a private concert at Tehran’s Russian Orthodox church in winter 1380 SH, described as its first live performance.[^first-concert] Persian Wikipedia describes *[Hafez Ashegh Ast](#hafez-ashegh-ast-we9)* as a two-song collection and the group’s last work with its original line-up.[^hafez-trio]

The chronology of *Hafez Ashegh Ast* remains unresolved. An archived official discography gives March 2001, the current official catalogue gives 1380 SH, and Persian Wikipedia describes the two songs as being released through the band’s website before Nowruz 1382.[^hafez-archive][^hafez-catalogue][^hafez-online]

## Continuing with new musicians

In the band’s account, difficulties working in Iran led to changes in the original line-up, and Sharbaf continued O-Hum with different musicians from 1380 SH onward.[^continuity] Earlier songs also became material for *[E-HUM](#e-hum-o-hum-remixes-1999-2009-zz9)*, a collection of remixes drawn from the band’s work of 1378–1388 SH. Sharbaf is credited with the remixing, production and engineering.[^remixes][^remix-production]

*[In Kherghe Biandaz](#in-kherghe-biandaz-pg7)* was recorded with a younger line-up. O-Hum’s history describes it as the group’s first officially released album in Iran, recorded and released after the Ministry of Culture and Islamic Guidance granted permission for the band to work there after twelve years. That account places both the permission and the album in 1391 SH; the band’s catalogue dates the album to 1392 SH, while Spotify gives 2014. Its original release year remains unsettled.[^kherghe-history][^kherghe-catalogue][^kherghe-spotify]

The live album *[O Hum Dar Azadi](#o-hum-dar-azadi-wrk)* preserves the complete fifteen-song concert at Tehran’s Azadi Tower. The band’s history describes its release by نشر کوک as a double CD.[^azadi]

## The trio returns, and Version Two begins

In autumn 1400 SH, Sharbaf, Riahipour and Izadkhah began work on *[Shatt-e Sharab](#shatt-e-sharab-mqm)*. The band describes this as the original members’ first full-album collaboration in two decades. As on the debut, only the three of them made the recording; Izadkhah recorded his guitar parts in Vancouver and sent them to the others.[^reunion] They offered the album through O-Hum’s and Sharbaf’s websites, letting listeners choose the price, including free downloads, which remained available for six months. The group dates this direct release to 17 Esfand 1400; Apple Music separately gives 15 March 2022.[^shatt-direct][^shatt-apple]

In Shahrivar 1403 SH, O-Hum began Version Two, a strand of its work using poetry other than Hafez’s, different music and a distinct logo. The band’s history reports three released singles under this name.[^version-two] Its catalogue identifies *[My Story](#my-story-single-x2c)* (داستان من) and *[Missing EP](#missing-ep-single-6cc)* (گمشده) as second-generation O-Hum singles.[^version-two-songs]
