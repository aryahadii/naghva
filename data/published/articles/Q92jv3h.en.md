Mahdyar Aghajani is a Tehran-born musician, producer and film composer who records as Mahdyar.[^bio]

## Early music and hip-hop

As a child he wanted to play electric guitar, but, he recalls, chose violin because it looked more like a guitar than the piano. He studied classical violin for six years and then Middle Eastern music for four. He says he met rappers through graffiti and offered to make music with Iranian instruments, seeking a hip-hop sound of their own.[^study][^study2][^graffiti]

## Producing and collaborations

His collaborations include [Hichkas](/en/hichkas-gyv/)’s [Dastasho Mosht Karde](/en/hichkas-gyv/#dastasho-mosht-karde-vv4), for which Hichkas credits him with the music. [Bahram](/en/bahram-nouraei-k4w/)’s discography credits both musicians as producers of [Eshtebahe Khoob](/en/bahram-nouraei-k4w/#eshtebahe-khoob-dej), and Mahdyar with its sound design and mix.[^hichkas][^bahram]

## Europe and experimental work

In a 2009 interview, Mahdyar said the freedom to make music without fear of arrest was the main reason he left Iran.[^departure] He later recalled that discovering electronic musicians in Europe and hearing their music on loud venue speakers changed the way he imagined music.[^europe] He also described making experimental music alongside production and film scoring as a space to test ideas without boundaries.[^experimental]

## Seized

For his 2018 solo album [*Seized*](#seized-zvv), Mahdyar says he selected and retouched older experimental tracks and composed new ones.[^album][^assemble] He recalled writing “Seize” soon after arriving in Europe, when its electronic scene inspired his attention to sound textures.[^seize] On “Vow” he used recordings of the singer Bidad’s voice as an instrument, with quarter tones over a 6/8 meter. He connected “Vow” and the album more broadly to different forms of extremism.[^vow][^extremism] “Money Money” began with thoughts about class, status, luxury and elitism; he edited recordings of Ash Koosha’s voice to suggest the title words and described the repetition as a cycle reproducing itself.[^money-theme][^money] NTS listed a one-off Mahdyar guest show dated 26 January 2018 in its London schedule.[^nts][^ntsdate]

## Film work

His film work includes the soundtrack for *No One Knows About Persian Cats*, on which his site credits him as film composer, music producer and artist. For the documentary *Life Is Waiting*, it lists mixing and mastering.[^film][^film2]
