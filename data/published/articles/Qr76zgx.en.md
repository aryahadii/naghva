Majid Salari is a singer and composer from Hormozgan in southern Iran. He sang with [Damahi](/en/damahi-ep6/) before developing his own ensemble and independent recordings. His work draws on southern songs and poetry, often with new arrangements by the pianist [Hamzeh Yeganeh](/en/hamzeh-yeganeh-373/).[^origin][^development][^monsefi-jazz][^sham-practice]

## Damahi and Seleidon

Majid sang shorveh and backing vocals on “Sogand,” a song on Damahi’s first album, released in 2015. The words were by the southern songwriter [Ebrahim Monsefi](/en/ebrahim-monsefi-qjv/), whose work would recur in Majid’s own repertoire.[^sogand][^sogand-context][^sogand-year][^monsefi-jazz]

By 2016, he had released songs with the guitarist and oud player Ebrahim Alavi. In February 2017 he performed with the pianist Amir Darabi’s jazz trio at the first Southern Nights festival in Tehran. Yeganeh was arranging his independent songs by then; the festival performance combined Monsefi’s words and melodies with jazz elements.[^independent][^independent-date][^alavi-instruments][^trio][^trio-date][^darabi-piano][^arrangements][^monsefi-jazz]

He went on to sing with Seleidon, a group formed at Jazz Nights. At the third Southern Nights festival in January 2019, they played older songs in new arrangements and a new composition by Damahi’s singer [Reza Koolaghani](/en/reza-koolaghani-kwr/). Majid introduced the set with a text stressing the cultural and musical differences between regions of southern Iran.[^seleidon][^seleidon-date]

## Southern songs and songwriting

For [“Sham' o Shab”](#sham-o-shab-nt6), Majid traced a song’s history through the wedding celebrations of Tiab, near Minab. He explained that people had sung its poem during a romantic theatrical performance called *Khosrow Shirin*. The musician Qanbar Rastgoo later set it for the jofti and a local orchestra. Majid described Navid Najjar Gheidel’s new arrangement as an attempt to preserve its romantic mood with oud, guitar and simple percussion.[^sham-practice]

His voice also appears in Franak’s death scene in the animated film *The Last Fiction*. The scene’s Persian lullaby adapts a solo performance by Khalo Ghanbar, the singer and musician.[^film]

Majid wrote music of his own as well. In 2021 he sang [“Eshgh Hamin Bood”](#eshgh-hamin-bood-83b), his setting of words by Mahmoud Tolouei, with Yeganeh arranging and playing piano. Koolaghani joined him as a guest singer on [“Baron”](#baron-agf), which Majid composed to words by Amir Mehdi Asghari.[^eshgh][^eshgh-year][^baron]

## The Wanderer and later work

In 2023, Majid released *[The Wanderer](#the-wanderer-tm5)*. He worked with Yeganeh’s arrangements and an ensemble including Alavi and the drummer Shayan Fathi. He co-wrote “Booye Gol” with Yeganeh and the bassist [Dara Daraei](/en/dara-daraee-cm9/); for “Tark-e Diar,” he sang Monsefi’s words to music by Daraei.[^album][^album-date][^ensemble][^booye][^tark]

At his Bandar Abbas concerts later that year, he opened with the album’s title song and included earlier songs such as “Sham’ o Shab.” He closed with [“Khatereh”](#khatereh-ect), another song using Monsefi’s words.[^bandar][^bandar-date]

His work with Yeganeh continued through new interpretations of earlier compositions. On [“Ghazal-e-Khoon”](#ghazal-e-khoon-ehy), he sang Monsefi’s words to music by [Soheil Nafisi](/en/soheil-nafisi-hev/). For [“Ze Khiyalat”](#ze-khiyalat-49q), he took up Abdolwahab Shahidi’s “Ze Jahan Del Borkandam” with Yeganeh’s arrangement.[^ghazal][^ze]

Darabi, whose trio had accompanied him at Southern Nights, became a songwriting collaborator on [“Mashrouh’e Tanhaee”](#mashrouhe-tanhaee-ew4). Majid and Darabi composed it to Tolouei’s words; Darabi arranged the recording and played piano and bass.[^trio][^mashrouh]
