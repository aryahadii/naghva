Kiyarash Davoudi began playing the santur in Ahvaz when he was about twelve. As he advanced, he travelled between Ahvaz and Tehran for lessons, then gained admission to Tehran University of Art. On his first album, *Engare Feragh*, he and tombak player Karim Ebrahimi recorded seven duets. The record came out in Iran in spring 2018.[^post177-learn][^post177-travel][^post177-university][^crit-duet][^crit-performers][^post191-spring]

While studying the melodic style of Isfahan, Davoudi watched a recording of Mohammad Reza Lotfi playing solo at Tehran’s Milad Tower. Some of Lotfi’s phrases became the refrain of “Yademan 95.” Davoudi also drew on the playing of Jalil Shahnaz and recorded the piece with tombak player Morteza Zarei.[^post198-concert][^post198-refrain][^post198-shahnaz][^apple-1753748663-drummer][^post198-players]

For *Faryad-e Hejran* (2024), Davoudi composed for a larger group. The Vedad Ensemble accompanied singer Mansour Jomehzadeh, with tar, ney, oud, kamancheh and bass gheychak alongside percussion. He returned to the santur and tombak duet on *Jan-e Hazin*, recorded with Pedram Khavarzamini and released in 2025. In 2026 he paired the sound of the santur with Hadis Karami’s bendir on “Blood Drop.”[^apple-1762656412-heading][^v5amq9mffkbs-title][^v5amq9mffkbs-group][^v5amq9mffkbs-person5][^v5amq9mffkbs-person6][^v5amq9mffkbs-person7][^v5amq9mffkbs-person8][^v5amq9mffkbs-person9][^v5amq9mffkbs-person10][^v5amq9mffkbs-person11][^v5amq9mffkbs-person12][^apple-1858465428-heading][^apple-1858465428-date][^vpmlokpx44ei-title][^apple-6830335005-hadis][^apple-6830335005-date]
