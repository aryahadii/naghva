Bahar Movahed is a singer from Tehran whose recordings bring together Kurdish tanbur music and Persian classical singing. She studied tanbur and tar before taking up vocal music. A cassette of Parisa singing at the 1977 Shiraz Festival drew her to the voice, and in 1998 she began studying with Parisa. Movahed qualified as a dentist in Tehran in 2003 and continued recording alongside her practice.[^tehran][^tar][^shiraz][^voice][^dent]

In Iran she sang for Ali Samadpour’s film scores, including *Among the Clouds* and *Mandoo*. In the puppet opera *Ashura*, she sang the part of Shemr’s wife and was one of the voices of Gabriel.[^film][^mando][^cast] She recorded Kurdish songs with tanbur player Ali Akbar Moradi before moving to the United States in 2010. The recordings became her 2012 solo debut, *Goblet of Eternal Light*. The two musicians drew on several Kurdish dialects, setting Sorani poetry by Mahwi and Nali alongside Hawrami verse by Mawlawi Kurd.[^us][^solo][^record][^words]

Movahed continued to sing with Samadpour in the United States. With Hamsaz Ensemble she performed his “Baange Naay” for the *Neishapur Nights* programme at Town Hall Seattle in May 2017.[^seattle][^set][^ney]

During the pandemic, Movahed in California and composer Navid Dehghan in Iran exchanged melodies on WhatsApp. Working in the Persian mode Rast-Panjgah, they set poetry spanning Rumi and Saadi to Sayeh and Siavash Kasrai. The resulting album, *Together Yet Alone*, features the musicians of Ghamar Ensemble and was released in May 2026. Nilofar Zubihi made the animated film for its song “Crack of Dawn.”[^tehran][^contact][^mode][^poet][^group][^issue][^animation]
