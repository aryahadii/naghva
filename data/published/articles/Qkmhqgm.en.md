Ooldouz Pouri grew up speaking Azerbaijani in Tabriz. On her first solo album, [*Waiting for the Dawn*](#waiting-for-the-dawn-rr7), she returned to songs that women had recorded in Iran and Azerbaijan in the 1950s and 1960s. Pouri sang nine of the ten songs in her mother tongue. For the tenth, “Shabe Mahtab,” she switched to Persian.[^life][^tongue][^older][^languages][^songnames]

## From Tabriz to the recording studio

Born in 1980, Pouri was four when her father, the translator Ahmad Pouri, began playing her Indian, Pakistani and Arabic music alongside classical music. In 2015 she began studying Persian singing with Mahsa Vahdat. That year composer Sahba Aminikia used the recorded voices of Pouri and Mina Momeni in *Sound, Only Sound Remains*. They sang music Aminikia had found on an old recording by the Armenian-Iranian singer and actor Loreta Hairapetian. In the filmed performance, the voices on tape are heard alongside the Kronos Quartet and the San Francisco Girls Chorus.[^life][^father][^name][^study][^tape][^loreta][^form][^film]

In 2016 the Norwegian label Kirkelig Kulturverksted released *Songs in the Mist*, a collection of Iranian women’s voices. Pouri sang “Yaraliam” and “Heyf.” She recorded both songs again for *Waiting for the Dawn*, released in November 2018. Vahdat and Erik Hillestad produced the album. Olav Torget played guitar, Gjermund Silset bass, Jovan Pavlovic accordion and Ali Rahimi percussion.[^comp][^songs][^two][^again][^date][^prod][^players][^drum][^album]

## Singing in Toronto

Pouri told *Hafteh* that her own father and brother would have been unable to attend a women-only concert in Iran. She moved to Canada in February 2020. In Toronto she formed Ooldouzlar to sing in Azerbaijani and Persian. She also performed with Padideh Ahrarnejad and Ali Massoudi in the trio Mahni. For a later project she began comparing Canadian and Azerbaijani folk songs, listening for similarities in their scales and the stories people tell through them.[^family][^move][^group][^trio][^studycan]

At Toronto’s Small World Music Center, clarinetist Selcuk Suna joined Mahni. In June 2024 Pouri sang “Neja Unudum Sani” at the Society Clubhouse with another clarinetist, Yalda Ehsani, and pianist Farid Yousefi.[^small][^winds][^club][^partners][^piano]
