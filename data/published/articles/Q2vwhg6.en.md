Kaveh Salehi is an Iranian guitarist, songwriter and singer, born in Saqqez in 1985. He studied music at Tehran Art University and became known through his work with [Pallett](/en/pallett-q53/). His independent work includes original songs and adaptations drawing on his classical guitar training.[^bio][^pallett][^solo][^learn][^tarrega]

## Learning guitar

Salehi began learning guitar at fourteen in Sanandaj. At sixteen, with his family’s support, he began travelling twice a month by overnight bus to Tehran for lessons, returning to Sanandaj after class. He studied flamenco with Shahin Alavi for a year, then turned to classical guitar. Alireza Tafaghodi accepted him as a student and taught him for two years before university, continuing as his teacher through his bachelor’s degree. Salehi later dedicated his composition “[Mazurka](#mazurka-by-kaveh-salehi-xxb)” to him.[^learn][^journey][^teacher]

## Pallett

In Pallett, Salehi played guitar and helped develop the group’s music. During the making of its first album, *[Mr. Violet](/en/pallett-q53/#mr-violet-bv3)*, the musicians built pieces together: bassist Darius Azar recalled Salehi bringing a melody, or singer [Omid Nemati](/en/omid-nemati-nha/) bringing words, for the others to develop.[^pallett][^making]

Salehi also worked with composer Fardin Khalatbari on Homayoun Shajarian’s album *[Emshab Kenar-e Ghazalhaye Man Bekhab](#emshab-kenar-e-ghazalhaye-man-bekhab-gpt)*. Khalatbari had commissioned a modified instrument called the luthar while searching for a sound to accompany the voice and strings. Salehi played it; Khalatbari recalled that he helped bring its sound closer to what the composer had imagined.[^luthar]

## His own songs

By 2017 Salehi was composing and singing work outside Pallett’s repertoire. Nemati described these songs as experiments that did not fit the band, at a time when its members had agreed to make room for individual projects.[^solo-before]

Salehi later performed his own songs in a concert series called *Night Bus*. He performed the programme at Hilaj Film and Acting School in Tehran; “[Band Baz](#بند-باز-bph)” addresses indifference and self-deception.[^nightbus][^bandbaz] He was booked to bring the programme to Stockholm in February 2024.[^solo] He is a former member of Pallett.[^current]

## Classical guitar and song

In 2024 Salehi released a setting of his own Persian words to the opening melody of Francisco Tárrega’s “Adelita,” titled “[You Are So Beautiful](#you-are-so-beautiful-based-on-a-theme-by-francisco-tarrega-k97).”[^tarrega] In 2026 he published his reworking of Alinaghi Vaziri’s “[Dokhtarak-e Julideh](#دخترک-ژولیده-cvf)” for classical guitar. He selected passages from the longer original and added an introduction and ending to give his version a coherent shape.[^vaziri][^vaziri-sections][^vaziri-shape]
