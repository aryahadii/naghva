Padik takes its name from the sound of anklets in Balochistan. Two musicians from the region, singer Rostam Mirlashari and benju player Abdulrahman Surizehi, formed the core of the group in Scandinavia. Anne Hytta brought the Norwegian Hardanger fiddle, whose extra strings resonate beneath the bowed strings. Swedish percussionist Fredrik Gille joined them in the quartet that played WOMEX in Copenhagen in 2011.[^ankle][^singer][^fiddles][^concert]

The early quartet paired Mirlashari and Surizehi with fiddler Ellika Frisell and Sebastian Notini. They released a self-titled CD before Hytta and Gille joined. The Norwegian folk music association places the quartet’s formation in 2000; the Swedish magazine *Lira* gives 2001. Pakistani tabla player Nafees Irfan appears in the lineup reviewed for *Music from Balouchistan*.[^early][^irfan]

The surviving performances let listeners follow the same musicians from one stage to another. Padik’s own archive pairs the BBC London performance of “Nouruz” with “Kepout” in Norway and “Omit” in Dubai. The studio album, *Music from Balouchistan*, opens with “Jee Makoran.” Mirlashari’s edition lists Surizehi, Hytta and himself as the recording artists.[^shows][^norway][^pad]
