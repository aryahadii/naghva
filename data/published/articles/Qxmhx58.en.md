Marie Sahba is a songwriter and electronic musician born in Trondheim to an Azerbaijani-Iranian father and a Norwegian mother. She sings in English and Azerbaijani. Her father’s stories about Urmia and Istanbul became material for her songs, first on the EP *[Azeri Baby](#azeri-baby-cd7)* and then on her debut album, *[A Billion Years Too Soon](#a-billion-years-too-soon-83f)*.[^roots][^dj][^words][^story][^ded][^album][^questions]

## From the piano to Azeri Baby

Sahba remembers composing at about eight by humming tunes at the piano. In high school she studied classical piano before turning to voice. At home, she and her father watched Azerbaijani and Turkish singers on television. He would tell her about the places where he had lived.[^piano][^study][^home][^story]

Under the name Marie Denise, she filmed a live version of “Limbo” with her band and made a mixtape of Norwegian musicians for Trondheim Calling in 2015.[^live][^mix] “Black Veil” was the first single she released as Marie Sahba, in 2017. Two years later she produced a song herself for the first time, “Take Me Back”.[^early][^selfprod] Her first EP, *Out of Control*, came out that year on her own label, Sahba Records. She co-wrote “[1992 Denim/Baby](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1992-denim-baby/1530605469)” for King George VIII in 2020.[^king] She was working on *Azeri Baby* when her father died in 2021. She dedicated the record to him. “Sevirem”, its opening track, features the Iranian santur player Javid Afsari Rad.[^debut][^label][^own][^father][^ded][^guest]

## Istanbul and Lake Urmia

A year after her father died, Sahba was walking across Istanbul’s Galata Bridge. She wondered what the waterfront had looked like to him when he arrived from Iran at eighteen. That question became the starting point for “A Billion Years Too Soon”. She returned to Istanbul to film its video and produced it herself. The Bergen International Film Festival screened it in 2024 and credited Çağla Çağlar and Emre Köktaş as its directors.[^biff][^bridge][^questions][^fatherage][^shoot]

Sahba wrote and produced the album with Martin Vinje. For “[Orumiyeh](#orumiyeh-rrt)” she put a heartbeat into the recording to convey what it felt like to see the lake shrink. The album came out in February 2024. She toured Norway and Türkiye that year, playing Istanbul, Ankara, Adana, Batman and Diyarbakır.[^vinje][^lake][^beat][^album][^tour]

After her mother died in 2025, Sahba wrote “Nothing Lasts Forever”. She and Vinje produced the single, released in September 2026, with Afsari Rad again on santur.[^mother][^newguest][^production]
