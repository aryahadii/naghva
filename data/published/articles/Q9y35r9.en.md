Bahramji is a singer and santur player from Kermanshah. He grew up around Kurdish and Persian music and later worked with electronic musicians after years in India. The Dutch producer Maneesh de Moor joined him on *Call of the Mystic*, in which his voice and santur sit alongside keyboards and electronic rhythms.[^bhborn][^bhfam][^bhib][^bhmix]

## Kermanshah, India and Ibiza

He was born in 1952 and began playing the santur at thirteen, giving his first performances in Kermanshah. He remembers growing up among singers, poets and musicians in his family. In India in 1980 he began playing alongside musicians from other countries. His own songs continued to draw on the verse of Rumi and other mystical poets. He arrived in Ibiza in 2003 after spending years living and performing in India.[^bhborn][^bhage][^bhfam][^bhind][^bhrumi][^bhib]

## Making a record

Bahramji made the 1997 album *Sama* with his son Bashir, who plays tombak. They returned to much of that repertoire on *Master & Disciple*, adding new pieces for Blue Flame Records.[^bhbashir][^bhsama]

Bahramji begins by singing and playing the santur, with no fixed arrangement. A piece may take shape while he tries out sounds on setar, ney or percussion. He takes that material into the studio to arrange and mix it. He singled out *Call of the Mystic*, *Sufi Safir* and *Divaneh* as the three records that changed his life as a musician.[^bhlive][^bhinst][^bhtrio]
