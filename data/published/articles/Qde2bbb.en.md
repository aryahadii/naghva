Ali Azimi is an Iranian singer, songwriter and guitarist from Tehran, based in London. After studying and working in engineering, he made music with Radio Tehran and then formed a new band for his solo recordings. His songs combine Persian words with rock and draw on his experience of changing careers, migration and relationships.[^origin][^band-roles][^change][^songs][^solo-transition][^concept]

## Engineering and Radio Tehran

Azimi grew up in a musical family, taught himself piano and studied classical guitar with Bagher Moazen. He studied engineering at Tehran University and moved to Britain in 2004 to continue his studies. After several years working in London, he turned to music in his early thirties.[^training][^change][^britain]

During a visit to Tehran, he brought together old friends to record songs he had written over the years. They became [Radio Tehran](/en/radio-tehran-q8m/).[^visit] His change of career inspired “Tatilat” on the band’s *88* and “33” on his later solo album.[^songs]

He made *[Mr. Mean](#mr-mean-qxx)* in 2013 with a new band outside Iran.[^date0][^solo-transition] Distance between Radio Tehran’s members had made continued work difficult; Azimi began the new project with English musicians, including Tom Sullivan. He still regarded Radio Tehran as an open project in 2013.[^tom][^open-band]

## Writing songs

“[Pishdaramad](#pishdaramad-m38),” from *Mr. Mean*, joined his music to scenes from Iranian cinema. Azimi worked on the video with filmmaker Arash Ashtiani for about three months.[^video-person][^video-time] *[Ezzat Ziad](#ezzat-ziad-dp9)* followed in 2016.[^album1][^date1]

Azimi gave lyric-writing more time as his work developed. He recalled spending seven or eight months writing the words for *[Of Love and Other Evils](#of-love-and-other-evils-k6h)*. Each song explored a phase in a relationship, from its beginning to its breakdown, with nineteen musicians taking part in the album.[^writing-time][^concept]

Music and words sometimes came together much faster. He recalled writing “[Marze Por Gohar](#marze-por-gohar-d54)” in about an hour, after its concerns had occupied him for longer. He wanted two voices throughout the song, which he sang with Golshifteh Farahani; Peyman Salimi produced and arranged it.[^marze-time][^two-voices][^marze]

## Later songs

For “[Khooneye Bahar](#khooneye-bahar-dye),” Azimi set words by Omran Salahi to music and dedicated its video to the passengers of flight PS752.[^bahar][^bahar-dedication] His 2022 album *[Kahrobaye Arezou](#kahrobaye-arezou-5tk)* took its title from a song setting poetry by Houshang Ebtehaj.[^album3][^date3][^ebtehaj]

He continued responding to events in Iran with “[Dige Vaghtesh Resideh](#dige-vaghtesh-resideh-bvc),” writing its words and music. Its official release carried the Woman, Life, Freedom and Mahsa Amini hashtags.[^dige]


In 2026 Azimi wrote “[Khoda](#khoda-afx)” using a theme by Erik Satie, with an orchestral arrangement by Afshin Khaef.[^satie][^orchestra][^khoda-words]
