Sahba Aminikia is an Iranian American composer and pianist from Tehran. He trained in Iran, Russia and San Francisco and began performing his work in the 2000s. For the Kronos Quartet he wrote music that combines live strings with recorded voices, including the songs of carpet weavers in Iran. In 2018 he founded the Flying Carpet Festival, which brings musicians and circus artists to communities near the Turkish–Syrian border.[^bio][^pianist][^russia][^degrees][^early][^knots][^fest][^mardin]

## From Tehran to San Francisco

Aminikia was born in 1981. He grew up listening to Iranian classical music, rock and jazz, and reading Hafez, Rumi and Saadi. He studied piano with Nikan Milani, Safa Shahidi and Gagik Babayan. His first composition teacher was Mehran Rouhani. He went to the St. Petersburg State Conservatory to study with Boris Tishchenko, then moved to San Francisco in 2006. He finished his studies at the San Francisco Conservatory of Music in 2013.[^bio][^tastes][^rock][^piano][^rouhani][^russia][^sf][^degrees][^completed]

In 2009 he performed at a festival in support of the website Iranian.com. Around that time he was also sharing his own piano playing online. In [“Reng”](#reng-improvisation-sfcm-nb4), he improvised on “Mobarak Bad,” an Iranian tune. Hermes Records put out his chamber music album [*Miniatures*](#miniatures-vvp) in Tehran in 2012.[^early][^reng][^ap]

## Writing for Kronos

In 2009 Aminikia returned to Tehran after his father died. He wrote [*A Threnody for Those Who Remain*](#a-threnody-for-those-who-remain-h7c) for amplified string quartet and tape, drawing on the sounds he knew from the city. He brought recordings of the bazaar, prayers and streets into the piece. For [*Tar o Pood*](#tar-o-pood-warp-and-weft-switchboard-premiere-b9j), he listened again to a sound from childhood. His grandparents wove carpets, and he had spent hours watching them. He spent six months recording singers in different parts of Iran and used his grandmother’s voice in the last movement. Kronos premiered the piece at the 2014 Switchboard Music Festival.[^father][^third][^tape][^woven][^knots][^grandma][^premiere]

Aminikia put out a call on Facebook for Iranian women to send him recordings of their singing. From the responses and an old recording by Loreta Hairapetian, he developed [*Sound, Only Sound Remains*](#sound-only-sound-remains-6yy), named for words by Forough Farrokhzad. [Ouldooz Pouri](../ooldouz-pouri-kmh/) and Mina Momeni sang the song he had heard Hairapetian sing. Their recorded voices are heard with the Kronos Quartet and the San Francisco Girls Chorus in the 2016 performance at SFJAZZ.[^women][^loreta][^singers][^forough][^film]

As composer in residence at the 2017 Kronos Festival, he used three songs from Sima Bina’s collection of Iranian lullabies in [“Music of Spheres”](#music-of-spheres-jr4). The piece brought Kronos and the San Francisco Girls Chorus together with the Afghanistan National Institute of Music. Around this period, he began arranging songs for [Mahsa Vahdat](../mahsa-vahdat-p2m/) and Kronos. Six of his arrangements appear on the 2019 album [*Placeless*](#placeless-feat-mahsa-vahdat-marjan-vahdat-fmv), which also features [Marjan Vahdat](../marjan-vahdat-trh/).[^kronos][^lullabies][^spheres][^covers][^placeless]

## The Flying Carpet Festival and later work

In 2018 Aminikia and Pınar Demiral of the Sirkhane Social Circus School started working together on the Flying Carpet Festival. From Mardin, musicians and circus artists travel to nearby communities, where they give shows and hold workshops with children. Aminikia received a 2025 Aga Khan Music Award for his work on the festival.[^fest][^mardin][^workshops][^award]

Aminikia continued to write around particular voices. For the album [*Being*](#being-kqv), he arranged eight songs by singer Siavosh Rezvan Behbahani for strings and voice, with the Persian Gulf Quintet. In 2024 he set Hafez for the San Francisco Girls Chorus in [*Woman, Life, Freedom!*](#woman-life-freedom-e6p). During a residency at 836M in San Francisco he worked with writer Zara Houshmand on [*The Language of the Birds*](#the-language-of-the-birds-836m-performance-film-excerpt-mj8), an adaptation of Attar’s poem that premiered in May 2024. The work moves between Persian and English, with Houshmand playing the hoopoe who leads the birds.[^being][^eight][^woman][^wfall][^wdate][^birds][^prembirds][^libretto][^hoopoe]

For Kronos’s fiftieth anniversary, Aminikia paired music for strings with images by Kevork Mourad. His *Four Seasons* follows Lake Urmia as it shrinks over the course of a year and takes its seasonal form from Vivaldi. He worked again with Rezvan Behbahani for [*Bamdad-e Bazm*](#بامداد-بزم-638), released by Mahoor in early 2026. They drew on older and contemporary Persian verse. In one setting of Hafez, the singer moves through Rast-Panjgah while the orchestra answers his phrases.[^lake][^viv][^mourad][^kon50][^mah][^ret][^ghazal][^release]
