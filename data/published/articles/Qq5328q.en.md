Pallett is an Iranian band formed in Tehran in 2009 by singer [Omid Nemati](/en/omid-nemati-nha/) and clarinetist Rouzbeh Esfandarmaz.[^formed] Its songs draw on classical and contemporary Persian poetry, with music developed together by the musicians.[^music][^collective] After *[Mr. Violet](#mr-violet-bv3)* and *[Tehran, Smile!](#tehran-smile-sc5)*, the band began experimenting with electronics while keeping its acoustic instruments at the centre of its sound.[^records][^electronic][^later-sound][^later-date]

## Beginnings

The musicians knew one another before forming Pallett. Nemati, [Dariush Azar](/en/dariush-azar-fs9/) and Esfandarmaz had worked with Dang Show, but they had already been playing together informally. Nemati recalled that the Mahak charity asked them to fill in when Dang Show was unavailable. Esfandarmaz gathered the others, and they spent five days developing their ideas for the concert. Three of those pieces later appeared on the first album. After touring with Dang Show, Nemati returned to work with the others on Pallett.[^beginnings]

They built songs around ideas brought to rehearsals: a melody from guitarist [Kaveh Salehi](/en/kaveh-salehi-2vw/), for example, or a poem from Nemati. Nemati usually chose the poems, but the others had to agree with the choice. He explained the name Pallett as different colours coming together to make a new one.[^collective][^poetry][^name]

In November 2012 the group played three nights at Tehran’s Azadi Tower under Esfandarmaz’s direction. “Mr. Violet” was already in the set, months before it gave the debut album its name. Shaghayegh Sadeghian, who would later join the band, played flute as a guest at these concerts.[^azadi][^azadi-year][^set][^flute][^records][^roster]

## The first records

Pallett released its debut album, *[Mr. Violet](#mr-violet-bv3)*, in 2013 and *[Tehran, Smile!](#tehran-smile-sc5)* in 2015.[^records] While working towards another full album, the band released the EP *[Endless Ending](#endless-ending-ep-5ce)* in February 2017 with a DVD of live performances. Nemati said they needed more time for the next album and wanted to shorten the wait for listeners. Two of the EP’s five songs were new; the others had appeared online before, but were rearranged for the collection.[^records][^ep]

When journalists from *The World* heard Pallett at a packed Milad Tower concert in Tehran, they asked how that audience differed from one the band had played for at a New York jazz club. Nemati said that at home, songs about the city and the concerns of his generation needed little explanation. In New York, he felt that the musicians and listeners were strangers to one another.[^concert][^club][^home]

The band's clarinet also carried the sound of klezmer. Asked about playing this Jewish musical tradition in Tehran, Esfandarmaz spoke of the different peoples who shared its sounds, from Bulgaria to Poland. A klezmer tune had accompanied one of Pallett’s more unusual television appearances: the musicians mimed playing it, with no instruments in their hands.[^clarinet][^mime][^klezmer]

At that point Esfandarmaz described a plan to introduce electronics without losing the acoustic foundation of the music. In 2019 Nemati said the next album would use new instruments and players and explore a little electronic music, while retaining the band’s approach.[^electronic][^later-sound][^later-date]

## Prime Meridian and later work

Pallett released *[Prime Meridian](#prime-meridian-r88)* in March 2021. Salehi, cellist Mahyar Tahmasbi and Nemati made it with other musicians, including [Makan Ashgvari](/en/makan-ashgvari-n8j/), who sang on “In Majara”.[^prime][^prime-date][^prime-credits]

The line-up changed over time. Nemati, Tahmasbi and flautist Shaghayegh Sadeghian form the band’s current core; Esfandarmaz, Azar, Salehi and Hesam Mohammadianpour are former members.[^roster] In 2025 Nemati and [Monicki](/en/monicki-jfb/) sang together on “[It Won’t Stay the Same](#it-wont-stay-the-same-fgs)”.[^monicki][^monicki-omid][^monicki-year]

In October 2026 the band announced that it was cancelling its tour of Iran after being told that its woman instrumentalist could not appear on stage in some cities. It said it would perform when all seven musicians could take the stage on equal terms.[^cancel][^cancel-date]
