Siavash Amini is an Iranian composer and sound artist whose work develops musical spaces from dreams, literature and images. A self-taught rock and metal musician before studying music in Tehran, he moved from guitar towards computer composition. He co-founded and curates SET’s experimental art events and festival in Tehran.[^bio][^quietus-0][^questions-0][^tar][^site-0]

## From guitar to the computer

Amini received his first guitar at twelve or thirteen. Listening to jazz released by ECM opened up the expressive possibilities of instrumental music for him. He later studied music for a university degree in Tehran.[^fluid-0][^fluid-1][^fluid-2][^quietus-0] During his move towards electronic composition he put the electric guitar aside for a year and concentrated on music software, techniques and history. He released *[Storm Leaves Us Quietly](#storm-leaves-us-quietly-p5j)* in 2012.[^questions-0][^fluid-3][^fluid-4]

Literature soon became a recurring starting point. *[Till Human Voices Wake Us](#till-human-voices-wake-us-k28)*, based on poems by T. S. Eliot, appeared in 2014 on Umor Rex, his first album for a label outside Iran.[^till-human-voices-wake-us-0][^till-human-voices-wake-us-1][^till-human-voices-wake-us-date] His work in Tehran also included SET and SEDA, a space for sound art.[^site-0][^foxy-3][^foxy-4]

## Dreams and tuning

With *[TAR](#tar-akg)*, Amini explored how a person’s dreams and fears relate to a wider collective state of mind. He premiered it at CTM’s Fear Anger Love festival in February 2017.[^tar][^ctm]

In *[A Trail of Laughters](#a-trail-of-laughters-4my)* in 2021, recurring distressing dreams led him to read the *Book of Marvels*. His interest in tuning had begun much earlier, through reading *Mahoor Music Quarterly*; around 2005–06, software synthesizers finally let him hear and test different tuning systems.[^a-trail-of-laughters-0][^a-trail-of-laughters-1][^a-trail-of-laughters-date][^foxy-0][^foxy-1][^foxy-2] On *[Eidolon](#eidolon-var)* in 2023, he returned to Safi al-Din Urmavi’s theories. He approached the tunings as raw material for new electronic music, continuing experiments begun during *A Trail of Laughters*.[^eidolon-0][^eidolon-1][^eidolon-2][^eidolon-date]

## Images, poetry and recordings

Amini’s collaborations gave these ideas other forms. For *[A Mimesis of Nothingness](#a-mimesis-of-nothingness-kvf)* in 2020, he composed music from Nooshin Shafiee’s photographs of Tehran. With the writer Eugene Thacker, he made *[Songs for Sad Poets](#songs-for-sad-poets-hgf)* in 2022, composing and performing music around Thacker’s poems.[^a-mimesis-of-nothingness-credit][^a-mimesis-of-nothingness-date][^songs-for-sad-poets-2-credit][^songs-for-sad-poets-2-date] On the third volume in 2026, he programmed synthesized voices to perform the poems and layered as many as forty voices at once.[^songs-for-sad-poets-vol-iii-0][^songs-for-sad-poets-vol-iii-1][^songs-for-sad-poets-vol-iii-date]

For *[Caligo](#caligo-q7w)*, released in 2025, Amini worked with two early Iranian solo piano recordings and synthesized material from their transcriptions. He connected the project with the novella *Malakoot* and developed it in Tehran in autumn 2024.[^caligo-0][^caligo-1][^caligo-2][^caligo-3][^caligo-date]
