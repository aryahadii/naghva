Mohsen Sharifian plays two reed instruments from Bushehr, the ney-anban, which uses a bag, and the ney-jofti, a double pipe. Born in the port in 1976, he formed Lian there in 1993. Collecting local songs and dance tunes gave him material for his own compositions.[^birth][^instruments][^founded][^field]

Before a concert at Tehran’s Vahdat Hall, Sharifian described a distinction that mattered to him at home. In Bushehr a *mawlud*, a religious celebration of a birth, could have instruments and a pronounced beat. He wanted listeners in Tehran to hear how far its music was from the sound of mourning.[^mawlud]

The group’s own archive traces his recordings back to two Payam Cassette releases, *Liana* and *Shap*. By the time he made *Dingue Marrow*, he was arranging Bushehri music alongside sounds from India, Spain and Australia. Sharifian called the album the group's first independent experiment in combining these traditions.[^cassette][^second][^experiment][^countries]

Sharifian’s field recordings preserve other people’s voices. For his survey of Kish Island he gathered pearl-diving songs, wedding music and music associated with the zār ceremony. *Neymeh* gathers the work songs of Persian seafarers. On the Bushehr *shervah* discs the label credits him with making the recordings and writing the notes.[^kish][^types][^sail][^tape]
