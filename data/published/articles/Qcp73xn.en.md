Daftar Aval began with two instruments. In Melbourne during the 2020 lockdowns, Iranian qanun player Vahideh Eisaei and cellist Zoë Barry started playing together. They drew on Persian classical and folk music, improvising and adding their own voices. Eisaei founded the trio with Barry and oud player Reza Kashi. She continued to perform with Barry as Daftar Aval Duo.[^r1][^r2][^r3][^r4]

All three musicians brought pieces to the group. Kashi composed “Desert Walker,” which they played at their April 2022 concert. For the same concert Eisaei built a piece on an Afghan lullaby. Barry improvised on cello and guest Saghar Khadem played daf. Barry’s arrangement of an Azerbaijani piece reverses those roles, setting out parts for the qanun, oud and cello.[^r5][^r6][^r7][^r8][^r9][^r10]

Melbourne radio station PBS aired two of the trio’s early recordings, “Nava” and a live “Chahargah,” in 2024. In Adelaide in August 2025, Eisaei and Barry played as a duo with the resident Silk Strings Ensemble. The venue’s concert film brings their qanun, cello and voices together with the bowed strings of the Chinese huqin family.[^r11][^r12][^r13][^r14]
