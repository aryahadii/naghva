Bahar Choir is a Persian-language choir founded in Paris by composer and conductor Arash Fouladvand. Its first singers were seven or eight people taking private lessons in reading music. Fouladvand recalled that teacher and students decided together to form a choir. For their voices, he arranged Iranian folk melodies using Western harmonies.[^bio-formation][^press-lessons][^press-start][^voa-arrangements]

Fouladvand dates the first group to around 2007. The choir gives 2011 as its formal founding year. At its first *Voice of Peace* concert, [Darya Dadvar](/en/darya-dadvar-xyr/) sang the solos. Cellist Mehrdad Baran came backstage afterwards to propose working together. The singers went on to share the stage with the East Paris Philharmonic Orchestra for *Silk Road, Voice of Peace*. Fouladvand established a London branch in 2016. The choir now also rehearses in Amsterdam, and singers elsewhere can join classes online.[^press-early][^bio-formation][^press-voice][^press-cellist][^press-collab][^voa-silk][^voa-concert][^bio-london][^bio-cities][^own-online] In the documentary *Voices from a Spring Garden*, Fouladvand rehearses a poem by Rudaki with the London singers, more than 1,100 years after it was written.[^rudaki][^film]

In 2024, the choir and orchestra performed Fouladvand’s settings of Rumi in Paris and London. The live album *Asheghane Ha* opens with “Ney Name” and includes two settings of “Resid An Shah.” A year later they took a programme of Hafez to UNESCO in Paris and Central Hall Westminster in London. Another live album, *Baraye Zendegi*, pairs the choir with singer [Niaz Nawab](/en/niaz-nawab-b26/) on songs including “Hagh.”[^bio-rumi][^ap1778056291-edition][^ap1778056291-t0][^ap1778056291-t2][^ap1778056291-t3][^bio-hafez][^ap1831324055-edition][^ap1831324055-t2]
