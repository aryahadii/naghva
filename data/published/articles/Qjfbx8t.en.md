Monicki Firouzmand (born 2004), known as Monicki, is an Iranian singer, songwriter and tar player from Karaj. She trained in Persian classical music before turning to jazz, and became known for the covers she posted online, such as [“Belik”](#belik-6c5) with Dina.[^born][^harp][^practice][^belik-billing] Her own songs include [“Pumpkin Latte”](#pumpkin-latte-sh6) (2023) and [“Blue”](#blue-483) (2024), and she has written and sung songs with [Rian](/en/rian-vhg/) and with the group Pallett. By 2025 she was studying at Berklee College of Music in Boston.[^pumpkin-date][^blue][^rakhte][^pallett][^boston]

## Early life

Monicki was born on 2 June 2004 and grew up in Karaj, west of Tehran.[^born][^harp] She was immersed in music from the age of four. As a child she wanted to learn the harp, but Karaj had no harp teacher, so she chose the tar. She was so small that her tar had to be made for her, because her hand could not reach the frets of a standard one.[^childhood][^harp][^tar-size] In her teacher's traditional ensembles she sang as the female chorus voice, and the group was chosen more than once at the Navaye Khorram festival. At fifteen she began formal voice lessons.[^chorus][^fifteen]

She was a strong student at a school for gifted pupils and took the experimental sciences track.[^science] Restrictions on women singers in Iran, she later wrote, had kept her from thinking of music as a career. In her final year of school she gave up two years of preparation for the university entrance exam to follow music. The pressure had grown so heavy that a family counsellor spent a long time persuading her father to let her choose.[^restrictions][^choice][^counsellor]

## Jazz

At fourteen or fifteen, a blues guitarist and a jazz pianist among her friends drew her to jazz and blues and urged her to sing them.[^friends] Her main voice teacher, still in 2024, was Dariush Azar; she also studied briefly with Ms Karapetian, who teaches mostly classical singing, took piano lessons with Sardar Sarmast and studied theory with Bamdad Khoshghadami.[^azar][^karapetian][^sarmast][^bamdad] Seeing [Golsa](/en/golsa-h74/) sing jazz, she said, gave her the push to start.[^golsa] With the young group Sarko she sang in a series of seven themed concerts, as the singer of its jazz night.[^sarko][^sarko-jazz] The group's jazz and fusion nights in December 2021, and again in February 2022, were streamed live on Twitch.[^sarko-azar][^twitch][^sarko-bahman] She had gone by Nikki until then; on the night of her eighteenth birthday, in June 2022, she wrote, Monicki was born.[^name]

She chose to cover only songs that meant something to her. She could have grown faster with pop, she said in 2024, but preferred to build an audience that would follow her own jazz and blues later.[^covers][^audience][^pop-easy] When Cadence reposted one of her early covers, before anyone knew her, it made her take her Instagram seriously.[^cadence-repost]

## First songs

“Belik”, a cover of a song by Taha Omrani from Bushehr, she recorded with the ukulele player Dina, who lives on Kish island, in about half an hour one morning in Tehran. She posted it in August 2023.[^belik-cover][^dina-kish][^belik-half][^belik-date] With Dina she also covered a song by [Sara Naeini](/en/sara-naeini-b23/).[^sara-cover] In 2023 she was the featured singer on [“Dey”](#dey-ks4), a single by Nik.[^dey][^dey-year][^dey-artist]

Her first original song, “Pumpkin Latte”, produced by Hamzeh Yeganeh, came out in the spring of 2023.[^pumpkin][^pumpkin-date][^pumpkin-production][^first-original] In January 2024 she released [“Faryaade Entezaar”](#faryaade-entezaar-p9y) as a single, with an instrumental version.[^faryad][^faryad-date][^faryad-inst] By early 2024 she had released two of her own songs: the second was “Blue”, which Mona Davar had written and offered to her.[^two][^blue][^blue-credit][^blue-writer] A song with Pallett was finished, and the band treated her as a full member.[^pallett-member]

## Rian, Pallett and Berklee

She and Rian wrote and sang [“Rakhte Tane Delbar”](#rakhte-tane-delbar-myg), released in October 2024, and followed it with [“Maashoogh E Jan”](#maashoogh-e-jan-j55) in February 2025.[^rakhte][^rakhte-writing][^rakhte-date][^maashoogh][^maashoogh-date] In March 2025 Pallett released [“It Won't Stay the Same”](#it-wont-stay-the-same-fgs), with words she wrote and sang.[^pallett-ensemble][^pallett-date]

By then she had moved to Boston. After three semesters of psychology in Iran, she studies contemporary writing and production at Berklee, with psychology as a minor, and works as a research assistant in its BPM Research Lab.[^study][^study-2025][^boston][^research] In 2026 she was a coauthor of a conference poster on how improvising musicians learn patterns in sound.[^epa-project][^epa-year]

In September 2025 she sang at Let Her Sing in San Francisco, and that October she announced that she would sing several pieces at Martik's concert in Boston on 14 November.[^outcome][^martik] In November she released [“Ghamzeh”](#ghamzeh-xx4), with music by Mo Shariat, words by Mohammad Akhavan and Arsham Ghafouri on guitar.[^ghamzeh][^ghamzeh-credits] In 2026 she sang with Datis on his [“Sepehr Baba Kojayi”](#sepehr-baba-kojayi-xdv) in February and on Aria Rohani's [“Vatan”](#vatan-cz9) in March.[^sepehr][^sepehr-title][^sepehr-singers][^vatan][^vatan-date] She sang on five songs of Hossein Meimani's live album *Reng-e Farah (Live at the Warehouse)*, released in July 2026.[^warehouse-0][^warehouse-1][^warehouse-2][^warehouse-3][^warehouse-4][^warehouse-album][^warehouse-artist] At Takotook in Toronto she sang [“Bolandam Kon”](#bolandam-kon-dobare-monicki-cover-live-at-takotook-toronto-jt3), dedicated to the people killed in Iran in January 2026, their families and the survivors; the full video was posted in July 2026.[^live-title][^live-dedication][^live-posted]
