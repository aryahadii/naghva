Ensemble Chakâm began in 2014 as a trio of Iranian musicians who had been friends for years. Sogol Mirzaei founded the group to play Iranian instrumental music. She led from the tar, with Vahideh Eisaei on qanun and Saghar Khadem on tombak. In April 2016 they opened for Kayhan Kalhor and Ali Bahrami Fard in Lausanne.[^friends][^founder][^first][^trio]

By 2020 Mirzaei was playing in Chakâm with Palestinian qanun player and singer Christine Zayed and French viol player Marie-Suzanne de Loye. Each brought original pieces for the others to reshape. Mirzaei wanted the three instruments to tell a story on equal terms, in a repertoire that could hold their shared experience of displacement. The musicians won the Prix des Musiques d’Ici in 2023 and played at WOMEX in Manchester the following year.[^change][^origins][^writing][^award][^womex]

Their first album, Les vents brûlants, appeared in March 2025. It includes Mirzaei’s “Didâr” and Zayed’s “Olive”, which the trio had also recorded live at home. Two players could recast the same repertoire. That April, on France Culture, Mirzaei and de Loye performed “Tant qu’il y aura des vagues” and “Femme qui rougit comme l’univers” as a duo. Before the group’s first concert in Hungary in November, guest qanun player Farah Fersi joined them to record Mirzaei’s “Cavaliers Absents”.[^debut][^date][^didar][^olive][^radio][^hungary][^music]
