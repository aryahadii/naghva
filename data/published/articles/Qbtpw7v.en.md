The musicians who became Atine first played together in a smaller lineup. Aïda Nosrat and tar player Sogol Mirzaei were preparing for Morocco’s Mawazine festival. Mirzaei brought in percussionist Saghar Khadem and qanun player Christine Zayed. When their producer suggested keeping a group together, they wanted a bowed instrument in the lower register. After rehearsing with two cellists they chose Marie-Suzanne de Loye’s seven-string viola da gamba. Its movable frets let her follow the changes of mode.[^women][^celli][^strings][^tar][^qanun][^drum][^gamba] The name they chose can mean both “gathered together” and “unprecedented”.[^name]

The Paris quintet released *Persiennes d’Iran* in November 2020. Its opening number, “Ey Tir,” also survives in a separate performance filmed in Paris for the Farhang Foundation. The album places Nosrat’s voice alongside the plucked strings of the tar and qanun, with tombak and gamba beneath them. In 2021 the group took this music to Porto for a WOMEX showcase.[^disc][^sang][^tar][^qanun][^drum][^gamba][^concert][^porto] When the quintet brought the programme to the Opéra de Lille, it traced much of its repertoire to the late Qajar period, when Ali Akbar Sheida and Aref Qazvini helped bring the music of courts and salons to a wider audience. The players made their own arrangements of the songs.[^history][^arrange]
