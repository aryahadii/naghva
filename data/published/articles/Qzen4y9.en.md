Hamid Saeidi is an Iranian composer, producer and santoor player. His work has included music for television and theatre, multimedia performances and recordings with the Los Angeles group Opium Moon. With the group, he won the Grammy for Best New Age Album in 2019.[^bio][^voyage-0][^commission][^band-0][^academy-0][^academy-2]

## Training and work in Iran

Saeidi began studying Persian classical music at fifteen. He learned santoor with Madjid Kiani and later studied composition with Farhad Fakhredini and Vartan Sahakian.[^about-0][^about-1][^about-2] He worked for Iranian national television as a composer for eight years, and his stage music included Helena Waldmann’s *Letters from Tentland*.[^voyage-0][^about-3]

In 2006 he helped start Gabgoo. The group brought local tunes and instruments from different regions of Iran together with music from elsewhere in the world.[^gabgoo-0][^gabgoo-1][^gabgoo-2]

## Los Angeles

Saeidi moved to Los Angeles in 2009. He continued working across music and other media: the Farhang Foundation commissioned multimedia compositions for its Nowruz celebrations at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, including the project *[Imaginary Knots](#imaginary-knots-7zw)*.[^voyage-1][^commission][^about-6] His album *Imaginary Knots* came out in 2013; *[Mist Over](#mist-over-mfn)* followed in 2021.[^record-1][^record-2]

He also developed Hamid Saeidi & Ensemble, whose performances use call and response.[^about-4][^about-5]

## Opium Moon

In Opium Moon, Saeidi plays alongside violinist Lili Haydn, bassist Itai Disraeli and percussionist M.B. Gordy. He has described their collaboration as a way to practise tolerance and understanding.[^band-0][^band-1][^band-2][^band-3][^canvas-0] The quartet released its self-titled debut in 2018 and won the Grammy the following year. Its second album, *[Night + Day](#night-day-6jw)*, appeared in 2021 and received a nomination in the same category in 2022.[^record-3][^academy-0][^academy-2][^record-4][^academy-1][^academy-3]

Saeidi co-produced *Night + Day*. The group separated slower, hypnotic music on the night programme from more danceable pieces on the day programme. During that period, he also created and produced *Contestee*, a television talent show for the Persian diaspora.[^epk-0][^night-sound][^day-sound][^epk-1]

The group released *Where We Are Gathered* in September 2023. Saeidi traced its creation to the pandemic, when the musicians were physically separated; he described the record as an expression of their continued closeness. The quartet continued with *The Time Has Come* in 2026.[^record-5][^canvas-1][^canvas-2][^canvas-3][^close][^record-6]
