Farshid Arabi sings and writes Persian-language metal. With his own band, he set ghazals by Rumi to electric guitar. What drew him to those poems, he said, was the rhythm of the verse and the protest he heard in Rumi’s words.[^roles][^band][^ghazal]

## Waiting for Penhan

While O-Hum were circulating their music independently, friends urged Arabi to do the same with his first album. He wanted a public record of the work under his name and waited for a release permit. *Penhan* came out in 2003. Drummer Nima Navapour played in his band, and bassist Mohammad Nik later recalled hearing *Penhan* on cassette just as he was thinking of selling his instrument.[^penhan][^reason][^first][^drums][^cassette]

For *Marge Khamoosh* (2017), Arabi cut a much larger set of songs down to nine. One of them, “Beraghsa”, began as a possible standalone single. Arabi asked Ali Momenian to sing it. Momenian described a melody drawn from the Iranian mode Homayun that could still be sung on its own if the metal instruments fell silent.[^silent][^select][^singer][^mode]

## Film and stage music

Alongside the guitar records, Arabi wrote music for Kazem Masoumi’s film *Bitter Almonds* and the stage work *Garden Party in Snow*. Their scores reached streaming services in 2025 and 2026. His metal album *Faded Calendar* also appeared in 2025.[^scores][^theatre][^almonds][^garden][^calendar]
