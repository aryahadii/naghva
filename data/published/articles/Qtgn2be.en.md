      Kiosk is a pioneering Iranian rock band (The Berlin Daily Newspaper) founded by Arash Sobhani in Tehran in 2003. After censorship and restriction by Iranian authorities, its musicians immigrated to the U.S. and Canada. The band has since toured throughout the world and released 10 albums. Kiosk is known for its satirical lyrics, which provide a socio-political commentary of life in Iran, as well as its blend of several music styles, from Iranian folk to gypsy jazz to rock. Kiosk's music has been described by BBC World as "songs that speak to a generation...Kiosk's stinging political satire is hidden within its blues and folksy sound." Haaretz has referred to Kiosk as the most popular Iranian rock band in the Iranian diaspora, while Ahram Online describes how the band gives a voice to their generation. Kiosk's live album "Triple Distilled," recorded at Yoshi's Jazz Club, was chosen by presenter Mark Coles from BBC World's "World of Music" as one of the ten best world music albums of the year, alongside such artists as Paolo Conte and Ali Farka Touré. TIME Magazine has called Kiosk "a band that can criticize the Iranian government without retribution," while Frontline PBS has praised their "eclectic sound that incorporates a multitude of instruments and styles." Current Kiosk members include Ali Kamali (bass, recording engineer), Arash Sobhani (lyricist and songwriter, guitar, vocals) and Shahrouz Molaei (drum).

Kiosk is an Iranian rock band founded in Tehran by [Arash Sobhani](/en/arash-sobhani-eca/).[^history] Its music combines Iranian folk, gypsy jazz and rock with satirical lyrics about social and political life in Iran.[^profile] Sobhani explained the name through its Persian sense of any temporary structure: the band met wherever it could to rehearse, always somewhere new, and the word described its underground spirit.[^npr-name][^wkb-name] In 2009 Inter Press Service described Kiosk as one of the most influential underground rock bands to emerge since the 1979 revolution.[^ips-influential]

## Beginnings in Tehran

The band’s biography dates its formation to 2003, while its homepage and YouTube description give 2004.[^history][^homepage][^channel] Sobhani said Kiosk began in Iran as a side project to another band of his. Because of the government’s restrictions on lyrics, the musicians did not bother asking for permits and recorded a whole album with no hope of ever publishing it.[^ips-side] “We grew up listening to [Bob] Dylan, [Leonard] Cohen, Dire Straits and Pink Floyd,” he said, adding that a gypsy influence had grown on the band’s blues and rock roots.[^ips-roots]

That album became [*Ordinary Man (Adam e Mamooli)*](#ordinary-man-adam-e-mamooli-jh7), released in 2005 outside Iran by Bamahang Productions.[^first][^hj-first] Babak Khiavchi, a guitarist in Kiosk who founded the label, believed it was probably the first Persian underground album to be officially released and copyrighted in the United States, and he added it to the iTunes catalogue.[^hj-itunes] Most of the band’s members left Iran within three months of Mahmoud Ahmadinejad’s election in 2005. Sobhani said they were tired of authorities who cancelled their concerts or refused permits for an album.[^ips-left][^ips-tired]

## Albums from exile

Sobhani released [*Amor De La Velocidad (Eshgh e Sorat)*](#amor-de-la-velocidad-eshgh-e-sorat-fem) in May 2007, after leaving for the United States.[^hj-love][^love-name] Most of its social commentary had been written in Iran and only polished afterwards.[^hj-polish] Persian Wikipedia says the album reached the top of CD Baby’s sales ranking, and by early 2008 its video had been viewed almost 400,000 times on YouTube.[^fa-cdbaby][^hj-video] Some listeners on the underground scene dismissed Kiosk as an imitation of Dire Straits and Bob Dylan. Sobhani accepted that his music sounded like Dire Straits and said he was proud of it.[^hj-crit][^hj-proud] On tour, the band relied on Persian student organisations, whose volunteers sold its CDs and T-shirts.[^hj-students]

[*Global Zoo (Bagh e Vahsh e Jahaani)*](#global-zoo-bagh-e-vahsh-e-jahaani-f3z), from 2008, alluded to the idea of the global village and leaned further towards gypsy music.[^zoo][^fa-zoo] Kiosk performed at San Francisco’s Palace of Fine Arts and Yoshi’s Jazz Club.[^live] The Yoshi’s recording became [*Triple Distilled: Live at Yoshi’s*](#triple-distilled-live-at-yoshis-seh-taghtireh-89t), released in 2010.[^profile][^live-album] Alongside its albums, Kiosk released [*Ay Yarom Bia*](#ay-yarom-bia-svx) in 2008 and [*Morgh e Sahar (Bird of Dawn)*](#morgh-e-sahar-bird-of-dawn-gch) in 2009, both featuring [Mohsen Namjoo](/en/mohsen-namjoo-t6w/).[^single-1][^single-2]

The fifth album, [*Outcome of Negotiations (Natijeh e Mozakerat)*](#outcome-of-negotiations-natijeh-e-mozakerat-j67), came out in 2011.[^outcome][^npr-fifth] Introducing it in 2012, NPR noted that Iran’s censors reached everything from lyrics to instruments, which was why Kiosk had brought its work to the United States.[^npr-intro] Sobhani said its song “I’m Leaving” came from a feeling many Iranians know, leaving home with a mixture of anger and defeat that stays for a long time.[^npr-leaving] Another song addressed a friend, Morteza, who would listen to his complaints and keep pouring him drinks; Sobhani called it free therapy.[^npr-morteza] In the same interview he said the government was creating its own version of underground music, as it had done with independent cinema.[^npr-cinema]

[*Parallel Establishments (Tashkilat e Movazi)*](#parallel-establishments-tashkilat-e-movazi-e5m) followed in 2013. Persian Wikipedia describes it as a critique of Iran’s social and political situation from the 1360s to the 1390s.[^parallel][^fa-parallel] Sobhani said [*Call a Cab (Zang Bezan Azhans)*](#call-a-cab-zang-bezan-azhans-2ab), from 2014, dealt with Iran after 2009; its song “All Asleep” described a society that is asleep, its eyes open but its mind shut down. He had not been back to Iran since 2005.[^cab][^la-theme] Its [*Strange Days*](#strange-days-jj-cale-cover-jmf), released in 2014, is a JJ Cale cover.[^single-5]

On 22 March 2015 Kiosk gave a free Nowruz concert at LACMA in Los Angeles.[^la-lacma] Sobhani said then that he was singing mostly to himself, to vent his anger and frustration at his people’s situation. He saw the first role of art in such times as documenting what people were going through.[^la-self][^la-art] [*Condition Orange: Live in Toronto (Vaziyat Narenji)*](#condition-orange-live-in-toronto-vaziyat-narenji-m59) was released the same year.[^orange]

## An album as a chapter of life

Sobhani’s work in Kiosk includes songwriting and lyrics, as well as guitar and vocals.[^profile] In a 2016 interview, he explained why the album remained an important form for him: a period or chapter of life could have a beginning and an end within it. He compared making an album to writing a novel rather than a blog, preserving the feeling of a period rather than a single moment.[^album-form]

That interview accompanied [*Stereo Tull Presents*](#stereo-tull-presents-rty), released in 2016.[^stereo-date] Sobhani recalled Stereo Tull as an organization that circulated unauthorized music in Iran during the 1360s and 1370s of the Solar Hijri calendar. Before the internet and satellite television, with shortwave radio also less available, it brought music from around the world into Iran, copied it onto cassettes and passed it to listeners.[^stereo] He described the album as the story of Kamran Mellat, who led that network.[^stereo-story]

According to Persian Wikipedia, Ardalan Payvar, a member of the band, left Kiosk after Sobhani signed an open letter to Donald Trump in December 2016.[^fa-payvar]

[*Sweet Destiny*](#sweet-destiny-pjc), titled *پایان شیرین* in Persian, grew from Sobhani’s long-held wish to make a musical film.[^sweet-title][^sweet-work] In August 2021, he said the band had spent about four years working on the album with limited resources.[^sweet-work] During that period, they had gone without releasing an album while concentrating on the project.[^sweet-focus] Persian Wikipedia describes its story as the last moments of a man sent to his execution with a deaf and mute messenger, though no one knows his crime, and says the album is dedicated to the families of people who were executed.[^fa-sweet][^fa-dedic] *Sweet Destiny* was released in 2021; [*Desolateville*](#desolateville-cep) followed in 2023.[^recent][^latest]

## Singles and screen

Between albums Kiosk released singles, among them [“To Ro Mikham”](#to-ro-mikham-grr) in 2018, [“Michael Jackson Passed Away”](#michael-jackson-passed-away-rcy) and [“Mah”](#mah-74t) in 2020, [“Mottaham”](#mottaham-frc) with Houman Javid in December 2022 and [“Daro Va Kon”](#daro-va-kon-6fv) in November 2023.[^toro][^toro-name][^mj][^mj-name][^mah][^mah-name][^mottaham][^mottaham-name][^daro][^daro-name] During the 2022 protests, the band worked with about twenty singers on “Zan Zendegi Azadi,” a song built around the slogan “Woman, Life, Freedom.”[^yh]

Kiosk’s songs have been used in *A Girl Walks Home Alone at Night* and in the Iran episode of Anthony Bourdain’s *Parts Unknown*. The band is also the subject of the documentary *Kiosk, A Generation Destroyed By Madness*.[^wkb-girl][^wkb-bourdain][^wkb-doc] It has toured the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, several European countries, Lebanon and Australia.[^wkb-tour] Its members include Ali Kamali on bass, Sobhani and Shahrouz Molaei on drums.[^profile]
