Kiosk is an Iranian rock band founded in Tehran by [Arash Sobhani](/en/arash-sobhani-eca/).[^history] Its music combines Iranian folk, gypsy jazz and rock with satirical lyrics about social and political life in Iran.[^profile]

## From Tehran to San Francisco

The band’s biography dates its formation to 2003, while its homepage and YouTube description give 2004.[^history][^homepage][^channel] Its musicians emigrated to the United States and Canada after censorship and restrictions in Iran.[^profile] [*Ordinary Man (Adam e Mamooli)*](#ordinary-man-adam-e-mamooli-jh7) was released in 2005.[^first]

Kiosk performed at San Francisco’s Palace of Fine Arts and Yoshi’s Jazz Club.[^live] The Yoshi’s recording became [*Triple Distilled: Live at Yoshi’s*](#triple-distilled-live-at-yoshis-seh-taghtireh-89t), released in 2010.[^profile][^live-album] Alongside its albums, Kiosk released [*Ay Yarom Bia*](#ay-yarom-bia-svx) in 2008 and [*Morgh e Sahar (Bird of Dawn)*](#morgh-e-sahar-bird-of-dawn-gch) in 2009, both featuring [Mohsen Namjoo](/en/mohsen-namjoo-t6w/).[^single-1][^single-2] Its [*Strange Days*](#strange-days-jj-cale-cover-jmf), released in 2014, is a JJ Cale cover.[^single-5]

## An album as a chapter of life

Sobhani’s work in Kiosk includes songwriting and lyrics, as well as guitar and vocals.[^profile] In a 2016 interview, he explained why the album remained an important form for him: a period or chapter of life could have a beginning and an end within it. He compared making an album to writing a novel rather than a blog, preserving the feeling of a period rather than a single moment.[^album-form]

That interview accompanied [*Stereo Tull Presents*](#stereo-tull-presents-rty), released in 2016.[^stereo-date] Sobhani recalled Stereo Tull as an organization that circulated unauthorized music in Iran during the 1360s and 1370s of the Solar Hijri calendar. Before the internet and satellite television, with shortwave radio also less available, it brought music from around the world into Iran, copied it onto cassettes and passed it to listeners.[^stereo] He described the album as the story of Kamran Mellat, who led that network.[^stereo-story]

[*Sweet Destiny*](#sweet-destiny-pjc), titled *پایان شیرین* in Persian, grew from Sobhani’s long-held wish to make a musical film.[^sweet-title][^sweet-work] In August 2021, he said the band had spent about four years working on the album with limited resources.[^sweet-work] During that period, they had gone without releasing an album while concentrating on the project.[^sweet-focus] *Sweet Destiny* was released in 2021; [*Desolateville*](#desolateville-cep) followed in 2023.[^recent][^latest]
