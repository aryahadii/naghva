Marjan Farsad (مرجان فرساد), born in Tehran in 1983, is a singer, songwriter, animator and illustrator. She was based in New York at the time of her 2021 interview with the Center for Human Rights in Iran.[^bio]

## Building scenes in songs

Animation and design remained Farsad's livelihood as she developed her music. In a 2020 interview, she described her lyrics as personal, rooted in situations she had experienced. Asked whether animation influenced her songs, she said it might: she often tried to picture a setting and tell a story through the words.[^visual][^scenes]

Her official channel's [2014 upload of Lullaby for Bunnies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fuG1mhwpLzc) credits her illustration alongside music and lyrics.[^lullaby-visual][^lullaby-upload]

That connection also appears in the credits of her later recordings. On [Nameh Baroon](#nameh-baroon-yyf), she wrote the music and lyrics and supplied the illustration; on [Parie Gheseha](#parie-gheseha-mvb), she is credited for music, lyrics and artwork. Both recordings were produced by Shervin Najafian, who also played guitar; he additionally played piano on Nameh Baroon.[^rain-credits][^fairy-credits] Her website also carries Italian lyric translations credited to Solnaz Kheirieh.[^translations]

## From Blue Flowers to White Tree

[Blue Flowers](#blue-flowers-7f5), her debut album, appeared in 2014, followed by [White Tree](#white-tree-cx9) in 2020.[^albums] Making the second album involved returning to the recording process: Farsad said she had been dissatisfied with an earlier attempt and did not want to release it in that form. Audience fundraising made another production possible.[^funding]

She described the musicians' own ideas as a substantial part of the finished album: their contributions became part of the work itself.[^team]

The [Be Yadam EP](#be-yadam-ep-tkf) followed in 2022.[^ep][^ep-date]

## Mahtab

[Mahtab](#mahtab-7ka), produced by the Center for Human Rights in Iran in 2021, grew out of Farsad's response to the imprisonment of her friends Keyomars Marzban and Niloufar Bayani. She described the song as more personal than political and said their cases had compelled her to speak out.[^mahtab][^friends][^response][^personal] Its image of stars disappearing night after night represented, in her account, the feeling of events in Iran.[^mahtab-image]

Farsad wrote the music and lyrics. Alejandro Vega handled production, engineering and mixing, Steve Mayone played guitar, and Jose Blanco mastered the recording.[^mahtab-credits]
