Ava Arehsazan, an Iranian oud player and composer, was born in Tehran in 1997. Her mother, Azar Zargaryan, taught her the basics of music on the setar. When she took up the oud she began by working out music for herself, improvising and composing before studying the instrument with Ehsan Emami. At the University of Tehran she learned from oud players Negar Bouban and Yurdal Tokcan and from Dariush Talaei. In 2017 she won first prize on oud at Iran’s eleventh National Youth Music Festival.[^s1][^s2][^s3][^s4][^s5]

While studying, Arehsazan performed with Shahrazad at Tehran’s Niavaran Hall. She went on to study composition and recorded with both the Aan and Nowroud ensembles. Aan’s musicians had played with Mohammad Reza Lotfi. When Aria Mohafez composed a lament for their former teacher, Arehsazan played oud in its concert and on the album Bright Sun. She returned for Sanaz Sattarzadeh’s Neshan, on which the Iranian ensemble alternates full-group pieces with solos and duets.[^s6][^s7][^s8][^s9][^s10][^s11]

Arehsazan joined the Rokh Quartet in 2024. Both she and Yasamin Shahhosseini contributed oud to its studio album Bostântchi, and Arehsazan also played on the live Tehran recording. In Geneva she teaches oud through the dastgâh repertoire, asking students to learn melodies by listening and to use them as the basis for improvisation. She and the quartet’s singer Hossein Rad have also performed as an oud and voice duo in London.[^s12][^s13][^s14][^s15][^s16]
