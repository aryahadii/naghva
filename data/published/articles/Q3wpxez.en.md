Mamak Khadem is a singer whose work brings Persian poetry into conversation with regional melodies from Iran and beyond. Her solo debut, *Jostojoo: Forever Seeking*, drew on music from Iran, Greece, Turkey and Armenia, giving those melodies new contemporary arrangements.[^jostojoo]

## Learning across traditions

Khadem grew up in Tehran[^upbringing], sang in the Children’s Choir for National Radio and Television in Iran, and moved to the United States as a teenager in 1977. She later returned to Iran regularly to study with Persian vocalists. Alongside that training, she studied classical Indian singing at the Ali Akbar Khan College of Music in northern California and explored Balkan singing with the Los Angeles choir Nevenka.[^early][^training]

While studying for her master’s degree in applied mathematics at California State University, Long Beach, she began a teaching career that lasted eighteen years. During those years she became a founding member of Axiom of Choice. The group recorded *Beyond Denial*, *Niya Yesh* and *Unfolding*. She left full-time mathematics teaching in 2003, while her later work as an educator included Persian singing classes and workshops combining singing, meditation and movement.[^education][^group][^leave][^classes]

## Solo albums

Her first solo album, *[Jostojoo: Forever Seeking](#jostojoo-forever-seeking-qyf)* (2007), developed her search for connections among regional musical traditions. Khadem and Jamshied Sharifi arranged the album together; on “Heydar,” Omar Faruk Tekbilek joined them as an arranger.[^solo][^jostojoo][^arrangements]

*[A Window to Color](#a-window-to-color-r7c)* (2011) centered on the poetry and paintings of Sohrab Sepehri and their attention to the natural world. *[The Road](#the-road-vs7)* followed in 2015. On “A Thousand Strings,” she set Persian words by Rumi to the Serbian melody “Ajde Jano,” a concrete meeting of the traditions she had been exploring.[^solo][^window][^road]

## Remembrance

Her fourth solo album, *[Remembrance](#remembrance-8pt)*, was released by Six Degrees Records on 25 February 2022. Created during the pandemic, it was dedicated to her father, whose final days in Iran she could not share because of travel restrictions. Khadem co-wrote and co-produced the album with Jamshied Sharifi, Marc Copely and Chris Votek.[^date][^loss][^production]

The Persian lyrics draw on Rumi, Saied Soltanpour and Fatemeh Baraghani. On “Across the Oceans,” Chris Martin contributes vocals and piano, while Coleman Barks recites Rumi.[^poems][^guests][^loss]
