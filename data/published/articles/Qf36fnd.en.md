Sevdaliza, born Sevda Alizadeh in Tehran in 1987, is an Iranian-Dutch singer, composer and producer. She moved to the Netherlands as a child and taught herself to sing and make electronic music.[^identity][^birth][^migration][^teach] Working with the producer Mucky, she paired her songs with videos exploring the body and gender. Her debut album [*ISON*](#ison-e85) appeared in 2017; later she worked with Pabllo Vittar and Yseult on [“Alibi”](#alibi-ft-pabllo-vittar-yseult-4q9) and released [*HEROINA*](#heroina-7m4) in 2025.[^mucky][^human-visual][^ison-date][^alibi-artists][^heroina-date]

## Finding music

At sixteen, Sevdaliza left home on a basketball scholarship and went on to play for the Dutch national team. She completed a master’s degree in communication and began working; she later recalled that her first job, at MTV, introduced her to artists and made her want to make music herself.[^sport][^degree][^mtv] Without training in reading music or playing an instrument, she spent hours in the studio learning to sing and use Ableton.[^teach]

She began making music with the Rotterdam-based producer Mucky. She described their working relationship as a patient one: they spent long hours finding a creative connection, giving each other room rather than forcing a song.[^mucky] In 2015 she released the EPs [*The Suspended Kid*](#the-suspended-kid-4fy) and [*Children of Silk*](#children-of-silk-feat-rome-fortune-4ac).[^early-ep][^silk-ep]

## Songs and images

Her videos explored the body as well as the songs. In [“Human”](#human-8rs), she used an unsettling bodily transformation to confront the fetishization of women and the male gaze. She described performing in the costume as something that changed the energy of the work.[^human-visual] In January 2017 she also released [“Bebin”](#bebin-vmf), sung in Persian, in protest against the US entry ban affecting citizens of Iran and six other countries. She announced that donations from the song would support people facing racial exclusion.[^bebin-date][^bebin-context][^ban-scope][^bebin-own]

Sevdaliza released *ISON* through her own label, Twisted Elegance, in 2017. She thought of the album as an emotional diary, and said that writing worked best when she began without a preconceived idea and balanced control with letting go.[^own-label][^ison-intent][^subconscious]

## Shabrang and Dahlia

Making [*Shabrang*](#shabrang-d8b) in 2020 meant working in different circumstances. For *ISON*, Sevdaliza had spent at least two years in the studio; for the new album she created between stretches on the road.[^shabrang-date][^tour-process] Its title refers to Siyavash’s horse in Ferdowsi’s *Shahnameh*. She also covered Googoosh’s “Gole Bi Goldoon”, a song she had heard repeatedly at emotionally important moments in her life and felt drawn to reinterpret.[^shabrang-title][^googoosh-cover][^googoosh-memory]

In 2021 she introduced Dahlia, a robot-like double seen in “Homunculus – Oh My God”. The character grew out of her experience of an industry whose expectations she did not fit, in sound, appearance or behaviour.[^dahlia] It accompanied the project that became the 2022 EP [*Raving Dahlia*](#raving-dahlia-k44).[^dahlia][^raving-date]

## New collaborations

Sevdaliza began studying Spanish and formed an online friendship with the Puerto Rican rapper Villano Antillano. They released [“Ride or Die”](#ride-or-die-feat-villano-antillano-7g4) in 2023; the Dominican rapper Tokischa joined them for [the second part](#ride-or-die-pt-2-d3x) in 2024, which they recorded together in Paris.[^spanish][^ride-date][^ride2-date] She also worked with Grimes on [“Nothing Lasts Forever”](#nothing-lasts-forever-xkc).[^nlf-artists]

For “Alibi”, Sevdaliza joined Pabllo Vittar and Yseult. She began its demo with her six-month-old child on her lap, intending to make an anthem celebrating feminine power.[^alibi-artists][^alibi-context][^alibi-demo] The song appeared in June 2024, and that September the three performed it during Karol G’s set at Rock in Rio.[^alibi-date][^rio] Sevdaliza described this period as a move away from using music primarily as therapy towards sharing joy, power and wisdom, especially with women and queer listeners.[^joy]

*HEROINA* followed in 2025. On the album, she wrote “Strong Because You Are” for her mother; she explained “Messiah” as a fantasy of placing women at the centre of religion without shaming their sexuality.[^heroina-date][^mother-song][^messiah]
