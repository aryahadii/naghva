Cinna Peyghamy is a Paris-born electronic musician who plays the tombak, an Iranian hand drum, alongside a modular synthesizer. Born in 1995 to Iranian parents, he spoke Persian at home but grew up unable to visit Iran. Learning the drum gave him a way to explore a part of family life he knew at a distance.[^born][^origin][^travel][^start]
 
## From Cikkun to the tombak

Peyghamy studied engineering, musicology and music computing in Paris, Montreal and Saint-Étienne. Under the name Cikkun he made [*Gamut*](#gamut-5s7), released in 2017, and mixed and mastered it himself. The next year he began learning both tombak and modular synthesis. Research into contact microphones for his master’s thesis in 2019 gave him a way to bring the two instruments together.[^educ][^early][^gamut][^start][^contact]

In September 2022 Peyghamy recorded his improvised performances in Bourges, France. Back in the studio, he kept ideas from the concerts and reworked their structure and sound. The Iranian label Zabte Sote released those recordings as *The Skin In Between* in November 2023.[^concert][^combine][^label][^ep]

## Music from family memory

For *Zurkhaneh*, Peyghamy studied the Iranian training halls where athletes perform traditional exercises to music. The internet was his only way of seeing the practice. His piece received its French premiere in Lyon in February 2025.[^sport][^online][^first]

On [*Music for Tombak & Synth*](#music-for-tombak-synth-j6p), released in June 2026, Peyghamy’s father Khosrow, known as Payram, reads a poem by Ahmad Shamlou on “Dar Shab.” Another track draws on a recording of Mahmood Schricker’s setar, and clarinettist Quelque Bourdon joined Peyghamy to write and play on “Slow Eaters.”[^record][^recite][^father][^setar][^clar]

Peyghamy also played with Azu Tiwaline in Marseille in 2024. Their extended recording of “Magnetic Service” accompanies *Dancing Plague*, a work by the artist duo 2girls1comp that changes the way male characters dance in *Grand Theft Auto V*.[^marseille][^dancing][^artwork]
