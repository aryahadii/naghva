Singer Rostam Mirlashari founded Golbang as an octet in Stockholm in 1994. The name, as the musicians tell it, means “the song of the nightingale.” The ensemble grew around musicians with Balochi, Persian, Kurdish, Afghan and Swedish backgrounds. They brought different ways of phrasing the same song to rehearsal, and improvisation became part of how the group played.[^gol][^night]

The band released records under its own name. *Setareh* marked its fifteenth year, with songs for the new year as well as love songs. Its fourth album, *Morid*, arrived four years later than the group had planned. Loss and mourning run through it, but the arrangements vary: “Ruze Nou” uses a reggae rhythm, while “Jane Shouki” returns to a lilting 6/8.[^voice][^title][^morid][^reggae][^rhythm]

On the early Caprice CD, the eight musicians set Mirlashari’s voice and Abdul Rahman Surizahi’s benju against keyboards, guitar, bass and several kinds of percussion. Three guest singers supplied the chorus. After 2000 the band regrouped around folk musicians trained at Stockholm’s Royal College of Music. Years later it made *Shohaz*, a concert for families with children aged five and over.[^eight][^regroup][^sho][^fam]
