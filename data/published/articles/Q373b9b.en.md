Hamzeh Yeganeh is a composer and pianist whose work brings Iranian musical material into jazz, from his own recordings to ensemble projects. A founding member of [Damahi](/en/damahi-ep6/), he plays keyboards and banjo in the band's original line-up.[^study][^band]

## Iranian material, different ensembles

Yeganeh's engagement with Iranian music extends beyond Damahi's southern repertoire. He drew on Baluchi music for his music-degree thesis, and on Iranian material in Ghali Irani and [Kahgel](#kahgel-xvf). His ensemble work includes Abrang, followed by [Naima](/en/naima-8rj/), and collaborations with Peter Soleimanipour and Arian Keshishi.[^study][^groups] Kahgel brings him together with Kaveh Sarvarian and Mahan Mirarab.[^kahgel]

With Damahi and Majid Salari, the focus turns toward the music of Bandar Abbas and its meeting with jazz.[^south] His contribution to Damahi's [Neighborhood of the Sleepers](#neighborhood-of-the-sleepers-r9k) is piano, alongside vocalist [Reza Koolaghani](/en/reza-koolaghani-kwr/), with oud, bass, drums and percussion completing the ensemble.[^recording]

## From the studio to the trio

[Hand In Hand](#hand-in-hand-feat-idin-sadeghzadeh-ssw) combines Yeganeh's compositions and piano, keyboards and synthesizers with Idin Sadeghzadeh's electric bass. He also handles production, mixing and mastering. The guests have specific places in the ten-track studio album: Amin Taheri plays drums on the opening track, and Kaveh Sarvarian percussion and tombak on tracks two and nine.[^studio]

The separate [Hand In Hand (Live at Hatra 2024)](#hand-in-hand-live-at-hatra-2024-e2j) returns to that repertoire as a trio with Sadeghzadeh on bass and Taheri on drums. Recorded at Hatra Music Institute, it contains the studio album's ten pieces and two older works, “Mahabadi” and “Jaam-e Darvish.”[^live][^liveworks]

His collaborations also include the jointly billed album [Parvâz-e Lahzehâ](#parvaz-e-lahzeha-6n4) with Sadaf Naghavizadeh and production of [Monicki](/en/monicki-jfb/)'s [Pumpkin Latte](#pumpkin-latte-sh6).[^billing][^pumpkin]

## Piano as a teaching instrument

[Persian Tunes For Piano (Remastered)](#persian-tunes-for-piano-remastered-nmy) connects his arranging and performing to teaching. The pieces progress from beginner material toward more demanding playing: left-hand rhythmic patterns recur across the collection, while chord symbols invite students to improvise. Yeganeh is credited with the arrangements, piano, production, mixing and mastering.[^tunes][^tunescredits]

The edition's chronology remains unresolved: the label says it was rerecorded at Kargadan Studio on 13 July 2024, while the streaming catalogue dates the remastered album to 30 June 2023.[^tunescredits][^tunesdate]
