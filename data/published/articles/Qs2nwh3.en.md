Barad took its name from a book of Iranian names after Hermes Records offered to release the musicians’ first recordings. The four had been meeting to play together and, as they recalled later, had gone to the label with demos. Their album credits Pouya Mahmoodi on vocals and guitar, Kasra Saboktakin on bass and guitar, Aydin Naeeni on guitar and Arash Pajand Moghadam on drums. They recorded in Tehran in the summer of 2003, with Ramin Sadighi producing.[^book-rehearsal][^book-publisher][^book-label][^sleeve2-pouya][^sleeve2-kasra][^sleeve2-aydin][^sleeve2-arash][^sleeve2-session][^sleeve2-producer]

Mahmoodi brought regional Iranian melodies to the rehearsals. Saboktakin, Naeeni and Moghadam reworked his pieces until the quartet could play them as a rock band.[^book-arrange] The opening “Leyva” and the eleven-minute closing “Sheykh Shangar” draw on the zar ceremonies of Bandar Abbas. The sleeve credits Soheil Nafisi with collecting the material for the closing piece. “Guaty,” another of the album’s eight tracks, takes its name and music from a ceremony of Baluchistan. For the songs between them, Mahmoodi sang poems by Fereydoun Moshiri, Rumi and Hafez.[^sleeve1-zar][^sleeve1-nafisi][^sleeve1-guaty][^sleeve1-poet1][^sleeve1-poet2][^sleeve1-poet3]

Hermes documented four nights of Barad concerts at Tehran’s Farabi Hall in February 2004.[^oldba-dates][^oldba-bill] Barad also played Tehran’s Farabi Hall at a benefit for those affected by the Bam earthquake. The group disbanded about two years after its sole album came out. In 2006 “Within Each of My Veins,” sung to a poem by Mohammad Rafie, appeared on the international compilation *Rough Guide to Iran*.[^concert-bam][^honar-end][^sleeve1-poet4][^sprough-comp][^sprough-song][^wmc-year]
