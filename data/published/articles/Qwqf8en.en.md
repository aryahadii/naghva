At Berlin Atonal in 2018, twenty musicians walked through the hall playing shakers. Their distance from each listener kept changing, and so did the echo. The piece, *next time, die consciously*, called for 31 drummers, bass clarinet and electronics. It was the first work Farahnaz Hatam and Colin Hacklander presented as LABOUR. They had worked together in Berlin under other names since 2013. Hatam writes sound in SuperCollider. Hacklander plays drums and composes.[^space][^debut][^start][^hatam][^colin]

A year later they returned to the festival with *nine-sum sorcery*. Singer Hani Mojtahedy sang Kurdish and Persian verses. For the recording, Hatam built an instrument in software that could break the voice into tiny fragments and rearrange them. The duo issued the piece on vinyl in 2020, the first record on their own studio label.[^vocal][^grain][^imprint]

## From the drum circle to the airwaves

In Dakar, the two musicians recorded more than 500 drum patterns with the family of Senegalese drummer Doudou Ndiaye Rose. At the opening of the 2022 Dakar Biennial, thirty sabar players from the family joined them for portions of *Etu Keur Gui*. Listeners can hear the piece in two parts on the Honest Jon’s release. Musicians from Dakar also appeared as guests on the duo’s monthly NTS programme, which began in July 2020.[^archive][^dakar][^radio]

Another part of their recorded work came from writing for the Berlin fashion label GmbH. Its 2024 anthology collects *White Noise*, *Talisman* and *Welt am Draht*, the last made with Khyam Allami.[^clothes]
