Vazesh are a trio formed in 2018 by the Iranian tar player Hamed Sadeghi, saxophonist and bass clarinettist Jeremy Rose, and double bassist Lloyd Swanton. Based in Sydney, they improvise together, drawing on the Persian radif. Their debut album, [*The Sacred Key*](#the-sacred-key-qkr), grew out of a concert at the Sydney Opera House.[^sacred-year][^sacred-line][^tapestry-origin][^music-location][^artist-radif][^sacred-two]

## A call to the bassist

Rose and Sadeghi had been playing together when Rose called Swanton and asked him to join them. In his other group, Eishan Ensemble, Sadeghi usually takes the lead on tar. In Vazesh, the players exchange roles. A tar melody can give way to a rhythmic figure while another instrument takes up the tune.[^tapestry-call][^sacred-exchange][^sacred-tar]

In 2020 the trio played two sold-out concerts as the Sydney Opera House reopened its music programme. Matt Hampton recorded the December 12 performance. The following year the trio released the recording as *The Sacred Key*, with two pieces, “Hypolimnion” and “Thermocline”. It received an ARIA nomination for Best Jazz Album.[^sacred-two][^sacred-rec][^sacred-date][^track-hypolimnion-track][^track-thermocline-track][^aria-nominee][^sacred-aria] The concert was also a finalist for Jazz/Improvised Music Performance of the Year at the 2021 Art Music Awards.[^concert-award]

## A concert in fourteen parts

The three musicians also recorded their second album, [*Tapestry*](#tapestry-jcf), in concert. Peter Nelson recorded them at the Annandale Creative Arts Centre. The 2024 release divides the performance into fourteen tracks. Each has a one-word name, and all three musicians share the writing credits.[^label-live][^label-venue][^tapestry-date][^blue-sections]
