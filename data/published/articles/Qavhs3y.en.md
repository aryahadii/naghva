Shen Band was the name Iranian songwriter Kaveh Hashemi used for the rock music he recorded in Montreal. He taught himself guitar and wrote his first songs in English. He told the Montreal magazine Hafteh that he switched to Persian because he felt his singing voice did not suit the English songs.[^guitar][^english][^voice][^farsi] He wanted to play in a band and took the final syllable of “musician” for its name. He wrote the songs, arranged and played them, and enlisted help from other musicians during recording and production.[^help][^iran][^city][^guitar][^what][^word][^work][^solo]

Hashemi released seven albums under that name, from *Baavarhaa* in 2006 to *I’m Here* in 2019. The aftermath of the Iranian election inspired his 2010 album *Naamareehaa*.[^iranianvote] The 2011 collection *Songs of Love and Protest* included “Shansi Chand Taa Kish.” Its lyrics use the moves of chess and backgammon to ask who holds the power when the game is being played.[^first][^last][^comp][^chess][^theme]

In 2012 Hashemi said he had declined an approach from SXSW because he had no way to perform the songs live.[^no][^contact] By early 2016 he was recording in Montreal with other musicians. They filmed “Siyaah” and a new recording of “Donyaa Saaket Neshasteh.” That summer Hashemi, Saeed Moradzadeh, Babak Namvar and JY Andre played for a local audience as a rehearsal for the tenth anniversary of the first album.[^stage][^why][^titles][^remade]

In 2017 he named Babak Namvar on violin and piano, JY Andre on drums and Kian Farshadi on bass. That year he wrote “Recluse (Not a Terrorist)” in English in response to restrictions on travel from Iran to the United States. In October 2026 he announced he would record and perform as musiSHEN, a name he felt better described the way he had always worked.[^group][^single][^context][^change][^year][^solo]
