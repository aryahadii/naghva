Fared Shafinury is an Iranian-American setar player, singer and composer from Corpus Christi, Texas. At home he spoke Persian and heard Iranian classical music. Outside he heard country, rock and Tejano.[^id][^music] He remembers singing along to pop songs and wanting to know what the words meant. By the time he was reading classical Persian poetry, he sometimes found it easier to follow than everyday conversation.[^corpus][^pop][^poetry]

## Recording between Tehran and Austin

As a young musician Shafinury spent summers studying in Iran with teachers including the setar player Masoud Shaari. In Tehran he learned the radif, the repertoire at the heart of classical Persian music. He returned to Texas in 2008 and played with his Austin band, Tehranosaurus.[^study][^studies][^texas][^band] He recorded his first full-length album, *Behind the Seas*, in Tehran and Austin. On “Skies Within Your Eyes” he sang a poem by Forough Farrokhzad. For the title track he turned to Sohrab Sepehri. The digital edition came out in 2011.[^album][^forugh][^sepehri][^edition]

One of his compositions reached listeners a few bars at a time, as the opening and closing theme for the Persian-language learning podcast *Chai and Conversation*. Its host Leyla Shams recalled that he released his second album, *Into the Night*, late in 2016. Its Apple Music edition is dated 1 January 2017.[^podcast][^late][^digital]

Shafinury also brought his setar into a duet with live electronics. On “Quantum Shāhed: The Witness & the Waves,” released by Ravello Records, Jeff Morris played the electronics alongside him.[^live] Through the Radif Retreat Institute, Shafinury built a place online to teach and archive the classical repertoire he had learned in Iran.[^school]

At the Getty Villa in 2022 he performed new settings of Rumi’s poetry for the concert series *Persia After Hours*. The museum released the concert in two parts.[^getty]

## My Iran

In 2026 the Farhang Foundation commissioned Shafinury to write a piece around the idea of light overcoming darkness. For “My Iran” he chose a poem by Kourosh Samiei and worked with producer Babak Saeedi. Shafinury described the song as a meeting between the bent pitches he knew from Persian music and the five-note scale of the blues.[^commission][^poet][^producer][^blues][^nowdate]
