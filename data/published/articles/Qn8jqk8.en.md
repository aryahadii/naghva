Makan Ashgvari is an Iranian singer, songwriter and electronic musician from Tehran.[^origins][^profile][^electronic] He makes music from recordings gathered on journeys around Iran, works with guitarist Amir Saremi on acoustic albums, and founded Otaghkar to organize independent music, workshops and performances.[^journey][^duo][^otaghkar]

## Early work and Otaghkar

Makan studied music at Tehran University, but left without completing a degree.[^education] He began singing Deep Purple songs at eighteen. After studying classical music, guitar and piano and working on a succession of rock projects, he turned to jazz.[^beginnings]

He made and released [*Hejdah*](#hejdah-6g5) in Tehran in 2009.[^hejdah][^hejdah-year] In 2012, he sang on [*Sahel-e Tehran*](#sahel-e-tehran-persian-jazz-music-bdq) with the jazz group [Naima](/en/naima-8rj/). He also worked as the art director of [Kamakan](/en/kamakan-6vg/)’s first album.[^naima][^collaborations]

In 2013, he founded Otaghkar, a platform that organized events, workshops and music releases. Through it he worked with other independent musicians: more than a hundred people took part in its workshops, and in 2017 it organized the Bazshenid festival in Tehran, with 26 musicians performing.[^otaghkar]

## Far and To Trucks

In 2016, Makan and Amir Saremi released [*Far*](#far-gmg) through Noufe. Besides Makan’s voice and Saremi’s guitar, the album used electric piano, clarinet, violin and viola, with string arrangements by Christophe Rezaei.[^far][^far-year][^far-players]

For his next solo album, [*To Trucks*](#to-trucks-mmj), Makan collected sounds while travelling around Iran, often hitchhiking. He dedicated it to the truck drivers he met on those journeys.[^dedication] He cut, distorted and re-recorded the material for one side of the album; on the other, he reworked older poems and songs, changing words and combining lines. He thought of the two sides as journeys through geography and time.[^journey] He handled the recording and production himself, using existing songs and poetry rather than writing new songs for the album.[^recording]

## Then Go

Makan and Saremi returned to their duo on [*Then Go*](#then-go-g4t), released in 2019.[^far-year][^duo] This time they recorded only voice and guitar together at Saba Studio. Makan said its storytelling poems could draw listeners into the songs, while the arrangements had fewer changes than on *Far*. Kaveh Abedin recorded, mixed and mastered the album.[^duo-process] Makan and Saremi produced it themselves.[^duo-production]

## In Rage We Dance

In July 2025, Makan released [*In Rage We Dance*](#in-rage-we-dance-wcc), writing, playing and producing all its tracks.[^release][^credits] He described drawing on the darbuka, Kurdish singers, rhythms inspired by Dabke and the voice and groove of Cheikha Rimitti. Unable to travel to the places he wanted to record, as he had for *To Trucks*, he used his laptop to listen to and combine these sounds.[^rage-process] He wanted listeners dancing in Tehran to feel solidarity with people across the region.[^rage-solidarity]
