Aïda Nosrat chose the violin at ten and was playing in the Tehran Symphony Orchestra at seventeen. She also studied Persian singing with Gholamreza Reazayi. In Manushan, her group with guitarist Babak Amir Mobasher, the violin and singing became parts of the same performance. At Brecon she moved from a verse to a violin solo while Mobasher kept time on the body of his guitar.[^violin][^orchestra][^teacher][^duo][^stage] Nosrat and Mobasher left Tehran in November 2015 on a French skills-and-talents visa and settled in Ivry-sur-Seine.[^move][^ivry]

Nosrat’s other ensembles gave her the chance to write for very different voices. For the Sowal Diabi collective she sang and composed a setting of the opening words of Rumi’s reed poem. In Atine her voice sits alongside tar, qanun, tombak and viola da gamba. By her account the players tried two cellists before they found the sound they wanted in the gamba’s bass register and movable frets.[^nas][^sang][^tar][^qanun][^drum][^gamba][^celli][^strings] In 2020 she and Rhoda Scott sang on “Gold Timer” for French pianist Andy Emler’s album No Solo.[^emler][^gold]

For *Common Routes* Nosrat travelled with guitarist Olivier Kikteff and accordionist Antoine Girard. They worked with local musicians in six countries, from France and Belgium to Hungary and Spain. The musicians they met along the way became part of what the trio recorded.[^trip][^countries]
