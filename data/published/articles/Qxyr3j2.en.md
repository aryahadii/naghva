Darya Dadvar is an Iranian soprano and arranger based in Paris. She grew up around puppet theatre. Her mother sang and directed a marionette company, and Dadvar told Radio Farda that her mother was her first real teacher. In Toulouse she won a gold medal in singing in 1999 and went on to specialise in Baroque singing. She moved to Paris in 2001.[^home][^mother][^teacher][^toulouse][^medal][^baroque][^city]

## Tahmineh on the Tehran stage

In the summer of 2002 Dadvar returned to the Tehran stage as Tahmineh in *Rostam and Sohrab*. Composer Loris Tjeknavorian conducted the Armenian Symphony Orchestra. In her own concert arrangements she often worked with a much smaller group. She said she used the piano more than any other instrument because it could take the place of an entire orchestra.[^year][^role][^show][^piano]

## A song from one concert to the next

“Mah Pishanoo” appears on three of Dadvar’s concert records, made in Berlin in 2004, Toronto in 2008 and Malmö in 2014. Each album preserves its own performance. Dadvar also composes. For “Naz Parastoo” she set a poem by Mehdi Akhavan Sales to music.[^berlin][^berlinsong][^toronto][^torontosong][^malmo][^malmosong][^naz]

“Iran,” with words and music by Leila Azodi Ghajar, gives another example of how her arrangements change. In one recording she sings with guitarist and arranger Babak Amini. In another she performs it with the Bahar Choir and the Orchestre Philharmonique de Paris-Est.[^iran][^guitar][^choir][^ensemble]
