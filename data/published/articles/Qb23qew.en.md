Sara Naeini (born 1981), who signs herself Saranin, is an Iranian singer, born in Shiraz and raised in Tehran, who has lived in Los Angeles since 2012 or 2013.[^birthday][^origin][^shiraz][^migration][^migration-kl] In Iran, where women may not sing solo in public, she sang underground and as a backing singer until she was barred even from that. In California she became known for her songs with the pianist and composer Reza Rohani, and since the COVID years for the videos they record at the piano at home.[^backing][^ban][^clicked][^covid] She also teaches singing, and says she has coached the singer Ebi.[^teaching][^coach-rehearsals][^coach]

## Early life

Naeini was born in Shiraz on 23 June 1981 (2 Tir 1360) and grew up in Tehran.[^birthday][^born-zh][^shiraz] Her grandfather was a naqqal, a teller of the old epics, and her mother, Hourvash Khalili, took part in one of the first naqqali contests in Shiraz.[^naqqal][^naqqal-mother] Khalili is a singer of traditional music who has also worked on folk songs, and singing was part of Sara's everyday life from the age of five or six.[^mother][^mother-kl][^folk] Ebi was the singer who first made her love singing as a little girl. At thirteen or fourteen, before she knew any English, she learned Whitney Houston's “I Will Always Love You” by copying its sounds, and she practised Celine Dion songs so loudly that the neighbours complained. Celine Dion stayed her favourite.[^ebi-child][^whitney][^celine][^celine-most]

Both her parents had studied physical education, specialising in gymnastics, and she grew up in the gymnastics hall.[^parents-pe][^gym-hall] For years she was Iran's national champion, and she dreamed of becoming a world champion.[^champion-years][^gymnast][^champion] That ended at fourteen, when she tried to jump from the roof of their four-storey building to the family's balcony. She fell into the yard and broke vertebrae in her back.[^fourteen][^fall][^roof][^fall-rz] She had caused the accident herself, she says, and it was hard to bear because she had defined herself by what her body could do.[^caused][^define] For three months she could hardly move, and singing was the one thing left to her. She put the same drive into it that she had put into gymnastics.[^three-months][^switch]

Before anyone knew her, she chose the name Saranin for herself. At home she was called Sara Banoo, and nin, she explains, means banoo, “lady”. She still signs her name Saranin.[^saranin][^saranin-home][^saranin-nin][^signature] She went on to study graphic design, at art school and at university.[^graphics]

## Singing in Iran

After her mother, her first teacher was Hamid Panahi, who, she says, opened the door for her to sing professionally.[^panahi][^panahi-door] Music became serious for her at eighteen, with an underground concert in a house in Tehran. The host set out two hundred chairs, and they played two shows a night for three nights. Her next concert was on Kish island.[^house][^house-md][^house-seats][^kish] The composer Reza Tajbakhsh arranged and wrote many songs for her, and they performed together underground.[^tajbakhsh]

At licensed concerts she could sing only as a backing singer. She sang with the rock band Piccolo and for singers including Alireza Assar, Mohammad Esfahani and Homayoun Shajarian.[^backing][^piccolo-rock][^singers] The best known of these concerts was Assar's run at Milad hall in Tehran, eleven nights with two shows a night. The audience took to her short solo passages, and that was the trouble: from the piano Assar kept telling the choir not to sing, and on the last night she was told not to sing at all.[^milad][^milad-nights][^welcome][^nakhoonia][^nakhon][^stop] Among the audience were the composer Anoushiravan Rohani and his wife, who went home and told their son, Reza Rohani, about her.[^rohani-meet][^voa-mother]

Between 2003 and 2009 the director Torang Abedian followed her for the documentary *Not an Illusion*, about the ban on women's singing and about underground music.[^film][^film-dates][^film-tol] Naeini was the only woman in Piccolo. When the band finally got a permit to play, in a hospital hall, she was allowed to sing only together with two other women. In 2006 the band, renamed Kook, got a permit for a concert at the Saadabad palace, where she was one of four backing singers.[^piccolo][^hospital][^hospital-women][^saadabad][^saadabad-chorus] By then she was known for singing in English, and she remembers one newspaper headline: “Don't call me Iran's Celine Dion.”[^english][^headline] She recorded an album in 2005 that was never released, and she sang abroad, in the United States in 2007 and twice in Vienna in 2008.[^album-2005][^abroad]

Reza Rohani first rehearsed with her around 2005, he recalls, but their work stopped when he moved to the United States.[^rehearsal][^no-continue] Years later he called her from America about songs he had written. She sang a trial take in a studio, he liked it, and he came to Iran to record the songs with her.[^call][^etude][^recorded-iran] They recorded “Didaar-e Jodaaee” in 2012; another singer, Morvarid, had sung it first.[^didaar-2012][^morvarid] The only video they made in Iran, a simple one, was of “Emshab Be Yek Eshareh”.[^emshab]

In Iran she also recorded “Esharat-e Nazar”, a song by Milad Derakhshani that was never meant to be released. Someone leaked it on YouTube; its video was filmed in Iran the night before she left.[^esharat][^esharat-kl][^leak][^video][^video-night] She recorded “Del Yar” in Iran too, with music by Anoushiravan Rohani, words by Delshad Emami and an arrangement by Reza Rohani. It was never properly recorded in a studio, and it too leaked, at Yalda in December 2016.[^delyar][^delyar-year][^delyar-studio][^delyar-self][^yalda][^yalda-leak]

Shortly before she left, the Ministry of Culture, which approved every musician named for a concert, stopped allowing her to sing even backing vocals.[^ban][^ershad] She was called in and warned several times, though never arrested.[^arrested] Unable to perform, she taught singing to women.[^teach-women] Being told not to sing, she said later, is like being told to die.[^die] On the day of her flight to America officials questioned her about why she sang. One told her that God had forbidden women to sing; she was no political activist, he said, but people loved her.[^god][^love]

## Los Angeles and Reza Rohani

She had wanted to leave Iran since she was eighteen, and she left at thirty.[^leave-18][^thirty] Rohani planned a series of concerts for her in California. Her first application for an American visa was refused for lack of evidence that she was a singer, and they had to explain that a woman cannot be a lead singer in Iran.[^california][^visa][^visa-why][^visa-lead] She arrived in 2012 or 2013 to tour with him.[^migration][^migration-kl][^two-years][^voa-tour] Their first concert was in Sacramento, followed by San Francisco and Los Angeles, and all of them sold out. She cried before the first, she said, because she could not believe she would sing on stage without a hijab.[^sacramento][^first-three][^sold-out][^first-concert][^hijab] Rohani had never seen her on stage until that night. For their first show in Los Angeles she pushed him to add a third part to the song “Nanoto”.[^never-seen][^nanoto][^nanoto-la]

They clicked musically, she said: she falls for his melodies, and he likes the way she sings them. When they work on a song, she says, he works like a director.[^clicked][^director] In May 2015 they premiered a new song, “Ashegh-e Khoondan”, with music by Rohani and words by Sogol Gharaei, on Sina Valiollah's comedy show *Chandshanbeh*, whose theme song she had sung. It remains one of her favourites, and one of the hardest to sing.[^ashegh-music][^ashegh-new][^theme][^ashegh-love] She also sang pop and jazz with other composers, among them Reza Tajbakhsh, and from 2016 toured with Oceanic Blue.[^music][^oceanic] She released [“Kamoone Rangi”](#kamoone-rangi-n95) in 2018 and [“Raz-e Bahar”](#راز-بهار-cs4) with Aria Rohani in 2019.[^kamoone][^raz][^raz-date] In 2020 she said she had not seen her father and her brother for seven years, since she cannot go back to Iran.[^seven-years]

During the COVID pandemic, with no concerts to play, she and Reza Rohani began filming songs at the piano at home, each in ten or fifteen minutes. The videos drew a far larger audience than they expected.[^covid][^minutes] Singles followed, among them [“Yare Roozaye Roshan”](#yare-roozaye-roshan-d7s) (2021), [“Navaee”](#navaee-yqb) (2022) and [“Khandeh”](#khandeh-hkd) (2023).[^yare][^navaee][^khandeh] Rohani holds the rights to their work together.[^rights] In 2022 they shaped a concert around what was happening in Iran.[^show-2022] At the start of 2024 “Del Yar” was finally released officially, on a [single](#sara-naeini-reza-rohani-performing-anoushiravan-rohanis-compositions-vtt) of Anoushiravan Rohani's songs, and that summer “[Didaar-e Jodaaee](#didaar-e-jodaaee-67w)” appeared on YouTube.[^delyar-release][^delyar-apple][^didaar-yt]

## Teaching and later work

Naeini works as a vocal teacher in Los Angeles.[^teaching] Through Siavash, Ebi's production manager, she says, she became Ebi's vocal coach, helping him recover the technique he once had.[^siavash][^coach-rehearsals][^coach-dream][^coach] She spent two weeks on one of his tours and sang in two of his shows, among other songs “Oon Ke Ashkato Pak Mikoneh” with him; her latest stage appearance with him was in Istanbul.[^ebi-tour][^ebi-song][^istanbul] She calls herself a pop singer and prefers the stage to the studio.[^pop][^stage] Her old wish was to be an international singer, which for her meant singing in English.[^wish] In October 2026 she released [“Lie to Me”](#lie-to-me-hx9), an English song and the first she wrote herself, words and melody.[^lie][^lie-title][^lie-date]
