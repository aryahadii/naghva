Hani Mojtahedy is a Kurdish singer from Sanandaj, Iran. Trained in Persian classical singing, she sings Kurdish poetry and has worked with musicians ranging from the Czech National Symphony Orchestra to the electronic duo LABOUR. In Berlin she began recording with producer Andi Toma, with whom she made *[Hjirok](#hjirok-25b)*.[^roots][^language][^czech][^lab][^duo]

Mojtahedy began singing professionally in 2000 and founded Vian, an ensemble of women. They could obtain permission to give concerts in Iran if they played for women alone. Even photographs of the musicians and their performances were forbidden. Mojtahedy says these restrictions shaped her decision to move to Berlin in 2004.[^year][^audience][^photo][^berlin]

Her album *[Dayk](#dayk-sk6)* came out in 2010. She later sang in Dilshad Said’s Kurdish symphony “Peshmerga” with the Czech National Symphony Orchestra. In January 2018 she spoke about “Azadi”, a song she had released in support of protesters in Iran. She set words by the Kurdish poet Hazhar Mukryani to her own melody.[^dayk][^czech][^azadi][^hazhar][^protest]

For LABOUR’s *nine-sum sorcery*, released in 2020, she sang interpretations of Kurdish and Persian verse. The rhythms she remembered from her grandfather’s house in Sanandaj were the starting point for another collaboration with Toma. Their album *Hjirok* received a German Record Critics’ Award in 2024.[^lab][^labyear][^field][^duo][^prize]

On a trip to Iraqi Kurdistan, Mojtahedy sang into a gorge along the Iranian border and heard her voice return from the other side. Toma recorded the echoes. Those recordings became the starting point for *Forbidden Echoes*, which they brought to the Holland Festival in 2025 with the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra.[^voice][^origin][^festival]
