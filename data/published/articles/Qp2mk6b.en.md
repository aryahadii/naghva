Mahsa Vahdat is an Iranian singer and composer, born in Tehran in 1973. Her music draws on Iranian classical and regional singing, and she composes settings of both classical and contemporary Persian poetry. Since the mid-1990s she has worked independently, often with her sister Marjan, and developed lasting collaborations with musicians in Norway and the United States.[^birth][^independent][^style][^poetry][^garden][^duet]

## An independent singer

Vahdat studied traditional Persian music with several teachers and holds a music degree from Tehran University of Arts.[^education] She began performing independently in 1995. She developed a contemporary style from Iranian vocal traditions and wrote most of her own songs, using poems by Hafez and Rumi as well as Forough Farrokhzad and Mohammad Ebrahim Jafari.[^independent][^style][^poetry]

Her participation in *[Lullabies from the Axis of Evil](#lullabies-from-the-axis-of-evil-g32)*, released in 2004, began a long collaboration with the Norwegian label Kirkelig Kulturverksted and producer Erik Hillestad.[^lullabies] On 22 May 2007 she and Marjan sang at a concert in a garden in Tehran, despite restrictions on women singing in public. The performance became *[Songs from a Persian Garden](#songs-from-a-persian-garden-yh5)*.[^garden][^garden-date][^garden-year]

## Collaborations and a solo album

In 2009 Vahdat made *[Scent of Reunion](#scent-of-reunion-love-duets-across-civilizations-b27)* with the American blues singer Mighty Sam McClain. The project brought their voices together in love duets, with recording sessions in Oslo, Tehran and New Hampshire.[^duet][^date][^recording]

Working with Norwegian pianist Tord Gustavsen on an album with the choir SKRUK led to further concerts and then to her solo project *[Traces of an Old Vineyard](#traces-of-an-old-vineyard-rhj)*. For its opening song, “Vanishing Lines,” Vahdat set a poem by Hafez. She recalled that the melody came to her while she was in Wrocław, Poland, working on another project.[^tord][^wroclaw]

In 2017 the composer Sahba Aminikia introduced Vahdat to Kronos Quartet. She and Marjan made their first album with the quartet, *[Placeless](#placeless-feat-mahsa-vahdat-marjan-vahdat-fmv)*, released in 2019.[^kronos-meeting][^placeless][^placeless-date]

## Songs and memory

Vahdat later settled in the San Francisco Bay Area. She recalled going to Berkeley’s Rose Garden at sunset, thinking of the sun rising in Iran. This experience led her to write her own words for “[Vaya Vaya](#vaya-vaya-single-y5k).”[^bay][^sun][^song]

For “[Braids of Innocence](#braids-of-innocence-b38),” she returned to a childhood memory. She recalled visiting an uncle imprisoned at Evin, when her mother briefly lifted her headscarf to show him her braids. Vahdat asked the composer and poet Atabak Elyasi, her husband, to write a text about the visit, then composed its melody. The song gave its name to her album with SKRUK.[^braids][^melody-braids]
