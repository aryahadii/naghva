Born in Sanandaj, Zanyar Hesami studied architecture and became a musician working with the daf, a frame drum, and the divan, a long-necked lute also known as the saz. In Iran he taught group daf classes and founded the Sejar Percussion Band. He also played as a soloist with the Tehran Symphony Orchestra, performing music by Ahmad Pejman under conductor Shahrdad Rouhani.[^born][^arch][^special][^teach][^sejar][^tehran][^rouhani][^pejman] He played divan on singer Mohammad Motamedi’s *Sarmast* and daf on Farid Isaei’s album of tanbur music, *Tanbur-e Dalahu*.[^sarm][^dalah][^tan]

For Sejar he wrote “Dlovan,” which the ensemble played at the closing ceremony of a festival for daf soloists in Tehran in 2021. His last concert in Iran was with Sejar in 2022 at Tehran’s Vahdat Hall. That December he released “Jin Jiyan Azadi” in support of the Woman, Life, Freedom protests.[^dul][^concert][^final][^protest]

In London, he and [Sara Fotros](/en/sara-fotros-vp2/) founded the [Zagros Daf Ensemble](/en/zagros-daf-ensemble-h6s/). Hesami wanted to find a place for the daf in contemporary ensembles as well as in the traditions in which he had learned it. In 2025 he played saz with Naser Razazi and Faraj Alipour in London and Manchester, and performed a saz solo at the Hay Music Festival. In “Danamirkê,” he set Kurdish poet Jalal Malekshah’s words to music and sang his own arrangement.[^zag][^sara][^new][^naser][^cities][^hay][^words][^sound]
