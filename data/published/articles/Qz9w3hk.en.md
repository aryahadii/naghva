King Raam is the stage name of Ramin Seyed-Emami, an Iranian rock singer and songwriter who began his career with the post-punk group [Hypernova](/en/hypernova-53g/) in the early 2000s. He turned to Persian solo songs in 2011 and later extended his storytelling into theatre and the Persian podcast *Masty o Rasty*.[^identity][^iranian][^career][^departure][^podcast]

## Hypernova and Persian songs

Raam taught himself guitar in Iran. Friends who were looking for a singer invited him to join them because he spoke English well.[^guitar][^recruitment]

Raam sang with Hypernova in Tehran's underground music scene, where the band played in basements without official permission. He sang only in English with the group, then decided to try singing in Persian.[^basements][^english][^persian]

He launched his solo career in Toronto in 2011 with [*Songs of the Wolves*](#songs-of-the-wolves-jb6), setting lyrics by Tara Aghdashloo to music. For this album, he moved from Hypernova's post-punk toward softer acoustic rock.[^solo][^lyrics][^career]

## Back in Tehran

Raam returned to Iran in 2014 and continued recording. During this period he performed in theatre but, by his account, could not hold an officially permitted concert.[^return][^theatre-iran][^permission] He recorded [*A Day & a Year*](#a-day-a-year-247) in Tehran, then released [*Until a Thousand and ...*](#until-a-thousand-and-3gm) in 2017, his last album made while based there.[^tehran-recording][^last-tehran]

Writing for beehype, Ali Eshqi described “A Day Will Come,” from *A Day & a Year*, as a nostalgic song about once-close friends now living in different parts of the world.[^song][^song-album]

## Storytelling on stage

His father, the environmentalist Kavous Seyed-Emami, died in Evin prison after being detained. In his solo performance *Departure*, Raam combined his music with the story of his father's death.[^identity][^father][^departure][^music]

In 2020, he began *Masty o Rasty*, a podcast in Persian.[^podcast][^podcast-start] In January 2024, he appeared as a musician and storyteller in Javaad Alipoor's *Things Hidden Since the Foundation of the World* at the Sydney Opera House, as part of Sydney Festival.[^sydney-role][^sydney-venue][^sydney-date]
