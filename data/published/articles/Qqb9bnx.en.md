Mehdi Moshtagh is a composer and setar player from Isfahan who has lived in Mexico since 2011. He studied Persian classical music with Mohammad Reza Lotfi and Dariush Talai. At twenty-three he recorded *On the Way*, a duet for setar and tombak. That music took him to Mexico for the first time, to play at the Ollin Kan festival. For his next record, *Roozgar*, he composed for two setars, oud, tombak and voice.[^birth][^mexico][^teachers][^onway][^visit][^roozgar]

In Mexico City he led Ensamble Didar and a group of setar players, Setaristas Moshtagh. He also recorded with the ensemble Egiptanos on its 2013 album *Quitapenas*. In 2015 he held a series of concerts in his home, devoting one evening to each of the thirteen modes he distinguished in the Persian radif. One listener told him Bayat-e Tork gave her hope. He remembered the remark because he played in that mode himself when he felt discouraged.[^groups][^egip][^dateap676974657][^house][^modes][^hope][^feel]

His 2016 album *Tarde y Lejos* gives three of those modes a sustained solo treatment. He brought the two countries’ music together on *Perxico, Persia y México* in 2020. For the Mexican song “La Morena” he had already filmed a performance in Mexico City with jarana player Claudio Naranjos Vega and frame drummer Francisco Bringas. His music also accompanied Miguel Nájera’s film *Tetlalli: The Place of Stones*, which won the audience award at the Ann Arbor Film Festival in 2020.[^albumap1500954006][^dateap1500954006][^albumap1503709581][^dateap1503709581][^morena][^film][^award][^audience]

On *Asatir* (2023) he drew on Mexican and Iranian myths in eight compositions and arrangements. Alongside tar, setar and shurangiz, he used Mexican drums and whistles of pre-Hispanic origin, electronic sounds and recorded soundscapes. In 2026 the Centro Nacional de las Artes programmed the work in a concert marking twenty-five years of his career.[^asatir][^eight][^sound][^celebrate]
