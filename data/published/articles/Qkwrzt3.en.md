Reza Koolaghani (born 1986) is a singer, songwriter and actor from Bandar Abbas, on Iran's southern coast, and the singer of the band Damahi, which he formed with the bassist Dara Daraee in 2014.[^birth][^band-en][^formation-cadence][^decade] With Damahi he released the albums [*Damahi*](#damahi-tv5) in 2015 and [*Dar Man Boro Shekar*](#dar-man-boro-shekar-fdd) in 2019 and a run of singles after them, singing mostly in the dialect of the south to music that joins the sound of Iran's southern shores with jazz and other genres.[^album-1][^album-2][^singles][^dialect][^efg-style] In 2023 the band made its London debut at the EFG London Jazz Festival, and in 2024 it took its music to Canada.[^efg-1][^canada][^warehouse-place] He has also acted in films and sung with the Hormozgani singer Majid Salari.[^acting][^baron]

## Early life

Koolaghani was born in the autumn of 1986 in Nayband Jonoubi, a seaside neighbourhood of Bandar Abbas, and grew up in the city.[^birth][^grew-up] As a teenager he played in the group Honar No Hormoz and learned music theory from Ahmad Tohidi.[^training] The songs of Ebrahim Monsefi reached him first; the other masters of Hormozgan's music, such as Ali Khan Habibzadeh, he came to know later through his own work.[^monsefi] He has called Monsefi first of all a teacher, and says Monsefi is still what he hums to himself.[^monsefi-teacher][^monsefi-hum] Nasser Abdollahi shaped him from childhood. Before Abdollahi was famous, Koolaghani remembered, he was the best singer at gatherings in Bandar Abbas.[^nasser][^nasser-majles] Among the musicians of his own generation in the city he counts Ebrahim Alavi, later Damahi's guitarist.[^generation]

## Tehran and the islands

To be seen and recorded, Koolaghani left Bandar Abbas for Tehran.[^tehran] Around 2009 he spent long stretches on the islands of Hormuz, Qeshm and Hengam, playing with friends. His creativity took shape there, he said; in Tehran he has to force it.[^islands][^creativity]

## Damahi

Damahi takes its name from a giant mythical fish. In the legend of the people of Hormozgan it lived in the Persian Gulf and held in its belly a utopia where there is never war. Damahi is also the name of a neighbourhood of Bandar Abbas.[^name-fish][^name-utopia][^name-place]

Koolaghani remembers first coming across Daraee on the beach.[^beach] The band began in 2014 with five members: Koolaghani, Daraee, the keyboardist Hamzeh Yeganeh, the guitarist and oud player Ebrahim Alavi and the drummer Shayan Fathi.[^band-en][^decade] That summer, Daraee later told Songlines, Damahi took part in Do Re Mi Fair Play, a film project in Brazil during the FIFA World Cup. It played in several Brazilian cities and on FIFA's official stage in Curitiba before Iran's match with Nigeria.[^brazil][^curitiba] In September 2014 the band put out [“Mahaleye Khamooshan”](#neighborhood-of-the-sleepers-r9k) (Neighbourhood of the Sleepers), with melody and words by Nasrok.[^first-single][^first-single-credits]

Of five producers the band met, only Ehsan Rasoulof asked what the band itself wanted to do. They chose him although his offer was the lowest, because he left their work alone. Some friends who had written for Koolaghani stopped working with him over that choice, and only later came back.[^producer][^producer-friends] The band counted its producer as its fifth member.[^fifth] Its first album, *Damahi*, came out in September 2015 and included “Mahaleye Khamooshan”.[^album-1][^album-1-track]

Koolaghani later called seeking an official permit the biggest mistake of his life: until then he had sung in the mountains and the desert, and with the permit he built a frame of censorship for himself. Even so, the Ministry of Culture began rejecting Damahi's songs.[^permit][^censorship][^rejected]

## “Divaneh” and the second album

In March 2016 Damahi released [“Kheyli Saadeh”](#kheyli-saadeh-m4d), the title song of the television series *Bimar-e Standard*, with music by Daraee and words by Kian Iraji.[^kheyli-date][^kheyli-tv][^kheyli-music][^kheyli-words] In May came [“Divaneh”](#divaneh-5et), with Koolaghani's music and words by Ehsan Goudarzi.[^divaneh][^divaneh-lyric] Goudarzi had sat down and spoken a couplet, and Koolaghani made the song from it there and then.[^divaneh-couplet][^divaneh-made] The producer stepped in only once, to say the song needed a second verse, and Goudarzi wrote it.[^second-verse][^second-verse-goudarzi] It is the only song Koolaghani made in the easy, accented southern style that he criticises in others; it came together in the moment, when the band added the drawn-out vowels and broader dialect words.[^divaneh-style][^divaneh-moment] It became the band's biggest hit, partly because of that dialect, and with that one song, the band said, it fills halls in Shiraz, Bushehr and Bandar Abbas.[^divaneh-hit][^divaneh-halls]

In June 2018 the band released [“Dard-e Bi Dardi”](#dard-e-bi-dardi-say) as a single.[^dard] That September it played at Niavaran Palace in Tehran, and from the concert it published “Mordan-e Mardaneh” (Dying Like a Man), to words by Monsefi.[^niavaran][^mordan-title][^mordan] In January 2019 (Dey 1397) Damahi and Khaloo Ghanbar shared a night of the festival Shabhaye Jonoob (Nights of the South) at the Niavaran cultural centre.[^jonoob]

On the second album, the band said, the words came first.[^words-first] Its name was changed on the day of a video shoot, after Daraee raised the matter, and together the band settled on *Dar Man Boro Shekar*, "Go Hunting in Me".[^rename][^title][^title-en] The album came out in June 2019 with “Divaneh” on it, and with [“Sooper Estar-e Man”](#sooper-estar-e-man-stf), whose words Mahmoud Tolouei offered to write when he heard its music.[^album-2][^album-2-track][^sooper][^sooper-words]

## Singles and tours

The pandemic stopped the band's concerts. Two shows planned for Esfand 1398 (February and March 2020) were cancelled, and the band lost, Koolaghani said, in spirit and in money. Its members regrouped to record a third album.[^covid][^covid-loss][^third] The singles kept coming: [“Barekat”](#barekat-single-snm) by Ebrahim Shahdousti in 2020; [“Rafic”](#rafic-rp3) (The Dude) in July 2021, to words by Ali Rezaie Sharif, recorded at the Kargadan studio in Tehran while Shayan Fathi played and recorded the drums in Madrid; and [“Parizad”](#parizad-3nz) in April 2022, with Koolaghani's melody to words by Monsefi.[^barekat][^rafic-date][^rafic-words][^rafic-tehran][^rafic-madrid][^parizad-date][^parizad-words][^parizad-melody] [“Shahrivar”](#shahrivar-38n), to words Ehsan Haeri wrote in 1394 (2015), followed in October 2022. On 17 October the band dedicated it to Mahsa Amini and to all the children of the land who had fallen in their blood.[^shahrivar-date][^shahrivar-words][^shahrivar-dedication]

In May 2023 a video report showed Koolaghani and Damahi in concert at the cafe-restaurant Khaneh-ye Doost, with the singer Shervin Hajipour there, his first appearance at a concert. Koolaghani asked the audience for a silence in memory of everyone who had died in the past months.[^doost-date][^doost][^doost-first][^doost-silence] The same month Amir Darabi's remix of [“Divaneh”](#divaneh-amir-darabi-remix-kd7) came out.[^remix]

In November 2023 Damahi made its debut at the EFG London Jazz Festival. The first show, at Rich Mix on 17 November, sold out, and a second was added on 20 November at World Heart Beat Embassy Gardens.[^efg-1][^efg-2] Reviewing the debut, Kiana Kardooni wrote that the sold-out night showed the band's appeal reached the Iranian diaspora too; the band came back for an encore of “Beman” and “Divaneh”.[^strand-sold][^strand-encore] On stage Koolaghani introduced a new song, “Gorbeh Hameleh”, to words by Goudarzi, for the album on its way.[^gorbeh]

By early 2024, Koolaghani told the Cadence podcast, Damahi had made two albums of thirteen songs each and had given fewer than forty concerts in seven years.[^two-albums][^concerts] The band had five members at first and four later.[^four] In April 2024 it released [“Hayamleh (Mihan)”](#hayamleh-mihan-wcq), a song by Ali Khan Habibzadeh dedicated to the homeland, with Ghanbar Rastgoo as guest singer and on the double ney.[^hayamleh-date][^hayamleh-dedication][^hayamleh-music][^hayamleh-guests] In June came [“Mahi”](#mahi-single-qyy), for the band's tenth anniversary.[^mahi] That month the promoter Homa Entertainment announced a Damahi tour across Canada, and on 27 November 2024 the band played a live session at the Warehouse Studio in Vancouver.[^canada-date][^canada-promoter][^canada][^warehouse][^warehouse-place]

In February 2025 Damahi released [“Octavio Paz”](#octavio-paz-single-j8k), a song with Koolaghani's melody dedicated to the islands of Hormozgan.[^octavio] That month Songlines reported that the band was working on its third album and would tour Europe in the spring.[^singles][^europe]

## Other work

Koolaghani has acted alongside his music, in films such as *Bandar Band* and *I'm Not Angry!*.[^acting] He wrote and sang the original song of Houman Seyedi's 2018 film *Sheeple* (*Mazhaye Koochake Zang Zadeh*), to words by Goudarzi, and his voice carries the music video made from scenes of the film *Khashm o Hayahoo*, which stars Navid Mohammadzadeh.[^sheeple-film][^sheeple][^khashm]

He sang as a guest on Majid Salari's [“Baron”](#baron-agf) in 2022 and wrote the music of a song on Salari's album [*The Wanderer*](#the-wanderer-tm5) in 2023. In July 2023 the two performed together at the Shahr-e Ketab bookshop in Fereshteh to mark the album's release.[^baron][^baron-date][^wanderer][^wanderer-date][^dooregard-launch][^dooregard-date]

In early 2024 he told the Cadence podcast that he was recording an album of his own with Amir Darabi.[^new-album][^darabi] He was living from private concerts and from tourism work in the south, and hoped in time to work with women musicians.[^private][^tourism][^women]
