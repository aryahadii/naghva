Sanam Maroufkhani is an Iranian singer-songwriter, composer and pianist, born in Tehran in 1996–97 and based in Amsterdam.[^origin][^profile] She writes Persian-language songs that blend influences from Southwest Asia and North Africa with Western alternative pop. Repeating piano patterns are central to her compositions.[^profile][^piano] In 2026 she performed with her own quartet and sang lead with the band Mahala.[^nachtzon-performed][^mahala-lead]

## Early years

As a small child, Sanam sat on the steps at home and practised songs with her mother. Her family encouraged her interest in music.[^mother-songs][^family-support] She began pursuing it seriously at sixteen, spending several years playing classical music and studying harmony and musical analysis. She then began searching for a musical language of her own beyond the boundaries of a particular style.[^sixteen][^classical][^artistic-direction]

At seventeen, she sang with a friend for their classmates on a school trip, which she later called the first professional performance of her life.[^school-singing][^school-age][^school-first] At twenty, she performed her own pieces and covers with her band. She recalled that both nights sold out and the group extended the run.[^early-age][^early-songs][^early-sold][^early-extension] As she grew older, she said, she became more conscious of the restrictions on performing and made less effort to perform live, but continued making music.[^live-work]

In 2018, Sanam and Maya Novaa released “[Smoky Bird](#smoky-bird-tvm).” They composed the song together, and Sanam wrote its lyrics.[^smoky-date][^smoky-artists][^smoky-writing] On “[Nahan](#nahan-hqx),” she sang and played piano with the singer and guitarist Peyman Salimi. Salimi rearranged the song, whose original music and words were by Ahmad Wali.[^nahan]

## Songs in Amsterdam

In June 2023, Sanam released “[When You Left Home](#when-you-left-home-4yc),” which she wrote, arranged and produced herself.[^when][^when-date] She dedicated it to Iranians who had sacrificed their youth for freedom, with the words “Woman. Life. Freedom.”[^dedication] That September, she [performed it](#vaghty-rafty-az-khooneh-let-her-sing-2023-live-ms6) at Let Her Sing at San Francisco’s Yerba Buena Center for the Arts.[^when-live][^sf-date][^sf-place] In the performance she told the story of a young girl imprisoned for taking part in freedom protests.[^when-story] She also sang “White Oaks,” a love story between two women inspired by Amsterdam’s canals, and “[The Poem From This Port](#the-poem-from-this-port-let-her-sing-2023-live-ecx)” with the singer Emma.[^white-oaks][^poem-live]

Sanam released the studio version of “[A Poem from This Port](#a-poem-from-this-port-7h9)” in 2024. She wrote the music and lyrics and worked with the producer Marc Sarrià.[^poem-year][^poem-credits][^poem-composition] The critic Kaveh Roshangar described the piano opening the song, then building to two climaxes with drums, strings and brass.[^poem-opening][^poem-arrangement] In May that year, Sanam was studying at the Conservatorium van Amsterdam.[^study]

## From trio to quartet

For the Houbara festival in Cologne in June 2025, Sanam expanded her trio into a quartet, with viola, bass guitar and drums accompanying her piano and voice.[^quartet-transition][^quartet-date][^quartet-viola][^quartet-bass][^quartet-drums][^quartet-piano]

Her composition “Indigo Valley” is about an inner refuge and was inspired by kintsugi, the Japanese art of repairing broken ceramics with gold.[^indigo][^kintsugi] In September 2025 she performed it at Paradisodebat, accompanying herself on electric piano.[^indigo-solo][^indigo-solo-date] A year later she sang it in *Female Voices of Resilience* at the Kölner Philharmonie, in an arrangement by Justus Berger.[^cologne][^cologne-date][^cologne-outcome]

In December 2025, Sanam released “[Mirror for the Sun](#mirror-for-the-sun-atj),” continuing her work with Sarrià. She sang and played guitar and piano; he produced the recording and joined her on piano.[^mirror][^mirror-date]

In May 2026, Sanam performed with her quartet at the Zonnehuis in Amsterdam for *Nachtzon / Night Sun*.[^nachtzon-date][^nachtzon-performed] Her set included an instrumental piece about her first bicycle in Amsterdam and a song about her mother.[^bicycle-instrumental][^bicycle][^mother-live] She sang lead with Mahala that evening too.[^mahala-lead] The band’s members met at the Conservatorium van Amsterdam. They combine music from Southwest Asia, North Africa and the Balkans with Western fusion pop.[^mahala-origin][^mahala-influences][^mahala-pop] In October she sang and played piano with Mahala at Popronde in Tilburg.[^tilburg-date][^mahala-tilburg-voice][^mahala-tilburg-piano]

That month she also released “[Beneath the Wooden Roof](#beneath-the-wooden-roof-live-recorded-at-sargfabrik-vienna-bmp),” a live recording from Vienna’s Sargfabrik, with her own lyrics and music composed with Avin Ahmadi and Reza Tavakoli.[^wooden-title][^wooden][^wooden-date]
