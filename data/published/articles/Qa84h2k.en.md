Sanam Maroufkhani is an Iranian singer-songwriter, composer and pianist, born in Tehran in 1996–97 and based in Amsterdam.[^origin][^profile] She began writing songs in English as a teenager in Tehran and turned to Persian around 2020, with “Noghteh-ha” for the band Jetpack and a reworking of the Afghan song “Nahan” with Peyman Salimi.[^english][^jetpack-persian][^nahan-afghan] In Amsterdam, where she studied at the Conservatorium, she leads her own quartet and sings with the band Mahala. Repeating piano patterns are central to her songs, which draw on Southwest Asian and North African music and Western alternative pop.[^study][^quartet-transition][^mahala-lead][^profile][^piano]

## Early years in Tehran

As a small child, Sanam sat on the steps at home and practised songs with her mother, and her family supported her interest in music.[^mother-songs][^family-support] She began studying music seriously at sixteen or seventeen, spending several years on classical piano, harmony and analysis.[^sixteen][^majara-start][^classical] She enjoyed it, but felt that it was not her path. She began searching for a musical language of her own and performing in cafés, to her family's surprise.[^rebellion][^artistic-direction][^cafes]

At seventeen she sang with a friend for the whole school on a school trip, which she later called the first professional performance of her life.[^school-singing][^school-age][^school-first] Around the same time she began writing songs, the first of them to reach someone who would not talk to her.[^first-song] On the day of the university entrance exam in the visual arts, friends took her to a café near Ferdowsi Square, where she played her one verse at the piano and the room applauded.[^konkur][^applause] At twenty she performed her own songs and covers with her band; both nights sold out and the group extended the run.[^early-age][^early-songs][^early-sold][^early-extension] As she grew older, she said, she became more aware of the restrictions on performing and made less effort to play live, but kept making music.[^live-work]

## From English to Persian

For a long time Sanam wrote and sang only in English, and putting Persian on the music she loved felt hard.[^english] In 2018 she and Maya Novaa released “[Smoky Bird](#smoky-bird-tvm)”, which they composed together to her lyrics.[^smoky-date][^smoky-artists][^smoky-writing] Before 2020, during a stay in Turkey without her piano, she wrote a song on the three notes she could play on a borrowed guitar. She was earning money by singing part-time in a bar, and she named the song after it: “Red Red Wine House”.[^turkey][^bar][^red-wine]

In 2020 she moved to Persian. She sang on Jetpack's synth-pop song [“Noghteh-ha”](#noghteh-ha-feat-sanam-maroufkhani-single-z2e), released in June; she had made demos of it in Persian and in English, and the band chose the Persian one.[^noghteh-date][^jetpack-persian] In November she released [“Nahan”](#nahan-hqx), an Afghan song by Ahmad Wali that she sang and played on piano with the singer and guitarist Peyman Salimi, who rearranged and produced it.[^nahan][^nahan-afghan] In an interview broadcast that month on the programme Ma'jara, she said she had released little for a year or two. She described how she writes: the melody first, then the vowels that fit it, then the words, which she keeps simple in either language.[^quiet][^melody-first][^simplicity] Some listeners asked her, she said, why she sang Persian with a foreign accent.[^accent]

In 2021 she was one of three musicians who provided music for *Arousak* (*The Doll*), a short documentary by Elahe Esmaili.[^arousak][^arousak-film][^arousak-director] That September she released [“Havapeyma-ha”](#havapeyma-ha-airplanes-gkb), which she wrote and sang, with Mohammad Shariat producing.[^airplanes-date][^airplanes-credits]

## Amsterdam

In June 2023 Sanam released “[When You Left Home](#when-you-left-home-4yc)”, which she wrote, arranged and produced herself. She dedicated it to Iranians who had sacrificed their youth for freedom, with the words “Woman. Life. Freedom.”[^when][^when-date][^dedication] That September she [sang it](#vaghty-rafty-az-khooneh-let-her-sing-2023-live-ms6) at Let Her Sing at San Francisco's Yerba Buena Center for the Arts, telling the story of a young girl imprisoned for joining freedom protests.[^when-live][^sf-date][^sf-place][^ybca][^when-story] She also sang “White Oaks”, a love story between two women inspired by Amsterdam's canals, and “[The Poem From This Port](#the-poem-from-this-port-let-her-sing-2023-live-ecx)” with the singer Emma.[^white-oaks][^poem-live]

She released the studio version of “[A Poem from This Port](#a-poem-from-this-port-7h9)” in January 2024, with her own music and lyrics and production by Marc Sarrià. The critic Kaveh Roshangar heard the piano open the song before drums, strings and brass carry it to two climaxes.[^poem-year][^poem-credits][^poem-composition][^poem-opening][^poem-arrangement] By May 2024 she was studying at the Conservatorium van Amsterdam.[^study] In January 2025 she sang on “[Women of Fire](#women-of-fire-feat-sanam-maroufkhani-8fy)”, from the kamancheh player Saba Alizadeh's album *Temple of Hope*.[^women-of-fire][^women-of-fire-date][^temple-kamancheh]

## The quartet

For the Houbara festival in Cologne in June 2025, Sanam expanded her trio into a quartet, with viola, bass guitar and drums around her piano and voice.[^quartet-transition][^quartet-date][^quartet-viola][^quartet-bass][^quartet-drums][^quartet-piano] Later that month she was booked to share a double bill with Makan Ashgvari, *Together We Fall*, at the Kantine am Berghain in Berlin, and in August the quartet was booked at Studio dB.[^twf][^studio-db][^studio-db-date]

Her song “Indigo Valley” is about an inner refuge and was inspired by kintsugi, the Japanese art of mending broken ceramics with gold.[^indigo][^kintsugi] She sang it at Paradisodebat in Amsterdam in September 2025, accompanying herself on electric piano.[^indigo-solo][^indigo-solo-date] In October and December she released two more songs with Sarrià, “[My Comrade Sings](#my-comrade-sings-mjp)” and “[Mirror for the Sun](#mirror-for-the-sun-atj)”; on the second she sang and played guitar and piano.[^comrade-date][^comrade-credits][^mirror][^mirror-date]

## Mahala and 2026

Mahala's members met at the Conservatorium van Amsterdam. The band combines music from Southwest Asia, North Africa and the Balkans with Western fusion pop, and in November 2025 it opened the final of the Amsterdamse Popprijs at the Melkweg.[^mahala-origin][^mahala-influences][^mahala-pop][^popprijs][^popprijs-venue] On 11 May 2026 Sanam played with her quartet at the Conservatorium's Maydays concert *Nachtzon* at the Zonnehuis in Amsterdam. Her set included an instrumental piece about her first bicycle in Amsterdam and a song about her mother, and she sang lead with Mahala the same evening.[^maydays][^nachtzon-date][^nachtzon-performed][^bicycle-instrumental][^bicycle][^mother-live][^mahala-lead]

That summer she was one of the singers on video in *Amsterdam Rainbow Dress – Unfolded Voices*, an installation by the Dutch National Opera & Ballet.[^opera][^opera-name][^opera-date] In September she sang “Indigo Valley” in an arrangement by Justus Berger at *Female Voices of Resilience* in the Kölner Philharmonie, accompanying herself on piano.[^cologne][^cologne-date][^cologne-review][^cologne-outcome] In October she sang and played piano with Mahala at Popronde in Tilburg, and released “[Beneath the Wooden Roof](#beneath-the-wooden-roof-live-recorded-at-sargfabrik-vienna-bmp)”, recorded live at Vienna's Sargfabrik with Avin Ahmadi and Reza Tavakoli, with whom she composed it.[^tilburg-date][^mahala-tilburg-voice][^mahala-tilburg-piano][^wooden-title][^wooden][^wooden-date]
