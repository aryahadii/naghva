Shahin Najafi (born 1980 in Bandar-e Anzali) is an Iranian singer, songwriter and guitarist. He began with poetry and classical and flamenco guitar, played in Iran's underground rock scene, and moved to Germany in 2005. His work has ranged from rap and rock to Gilaki jazz fusion and ensemble composition; he also makes electronic music as The Shah.[^beginnings][^germany][^meta][^sacred][^electronic]

## Early music and Germany

Shahin began writing poetry and learning guitar as a teenager. He later recalled giving private guitar lessons at night and spending the earnings on sight-singing and harmony classes the next day. In Iran he worked with underground groups playing pop, Spanish music and rock.[^beginnings][^study][^bands]

By 2008 he was singing and playing guitar with the Iranian-German group Tapesh 2012. For “[Ma Mard Nistim](#ma-mard-nistim-cms)”, he wrote and sang the words while the group supplied the music. He valued rap's capacity to turn verbal images into scenes: listeners could imagine social circumstances unfolding before them, rather than only hear an account of them.[^tapesh][^rap]

In September 2009 he released *[Illusion](#illusion-bgg)* through Pamas-Verlag. He sang and wrote the words; MEHZO Sound Studio composed and arranged the music. By February 2010 he was assembling the rock group Antikarisma with Babak Khazaei, Armin Mostaed and Pejman Afshari in Germany.[^illusion][^antikarisma]

## Songs and musical form

His 2012 song “[Naghi](#naghi-gez)” brought threats: the website Shia-Online offered a reward for his killing. Shahin denied intending an insult and said the song had begun after he encountered a satirical Facebook campaign about Imam Ali al-Naqi.[^naghi][^threatreport][^naghiorigin]

For “[Sha'aban](#shaaban-5p5)”, released in 2013 and arranged by Majid Kazemi, Shahin created an imagined worker. He explained that Sha'aban was not an actual individual but represented the poverty experienced by workers in Iran and elsewhere.[^shaabancredits][^shaabanarrangement][^character][^subject]

In 2017, when he released *[Radikal](#radikal-gkk)* and “[Blue](#blue-z27)”, Shahin described a shift in his priorities: he wanted music to move beyond spoken language, with words no longer taking precedence. Gilaki and flamenco had interested him since his early work in Iran. He still heard lullabies in Gilaki and was concerned about the language's disappearance. In Blue he brought together his own text, Hafez and Quranic material. He said he wanted to keep changing, and felt that controversy around his social and political subjects had obscured his experiments with form.[^radikaldate][^bluedate][^form][^gilaki][^lullaby][^blue][^change]

That December, *[Meta Phrygian](#meta-phrygian-live-gbx)* presented a live jazz-fusion ensemble with Gilaki material. Shahin sang and played trumpet; the group combined piano, bass and drums with a second trumpet.[^meta][^players][^metadate]

## Collaborative ensembles and electronic music

Shahin described *[Jens Sevom](#jens-sevom-zqw)*, released in January 2019, as the result of two years' work on jazz and ideas drawn from folk music. Guitarist Pouya Mahmoodi, percussionist [Habib Meftah](/en/habib-meftah-49p/) and pianist Adrian Wachowiak joined him. He compared the small ensemble to chamber music and stressed the freedom it gave singers and players to experiment with performance and form. Although he had written and directed the project, he considered all four musicians responsible for its central creative roles.[^jazz][^jens][^jensdate][^freedom][^shared]

Later in 2019, he composed *[The Sacred and the Violence](#the-sacred-and-the-violence-cgj)* for an ensemble of flute, strings, piano and percussion. Wachowiak arranged the music, and Simon Rummel conducted and played piano.[^sacred][^sacreddate]

By 2024 he was releasing electronic music under the name The Shah, describing an approach that combined Persian sounds with Middle Eastern and Afrobeat influences. That September he released *[Semen](#semen-4bz)* with Hand.[^electronic][^semen][^semendate]
