Pedram Khavarzamini is a tombak player and composer from Tehran. He wrote music for an ensemble of the Iranian goblet drums on [*Koutah*](#koutah-bpp). On [*Zikr*](#zikr-4ww) he is one of three players, alongside Mohammad Reza Ebrahimi on Kurdish divan and Kjetil Selvik on clarinets.[^school-birth][^school-debut][^zikr-mix][^zikr-names][^zikr-selvik]

As a boy, Khavarzamini tapped rhythms on benches and doors. He liked hearing his uncle play the melodica and a clay darbuka, and began studying the tombak at sixteen. His teachers were Kamyar Mohabbat and Bahman Rajabi. He later joined Rajabi onstage for a tombak duet.[^ethnotes-child][^ethnotes-uncle][^ethnotes-age][^tirgan-teacher][^tirgan-duet]

Khavarzamini founded the percussion group Varashan. He and fellow drummer Hamid Ghambary made *Koutah* together. In 1999 they also edited a collection of works by tombak player Amir Nasser Eftetah. Khavarzamini taught at the Labyrinth workshop in Houdetsi on Crete between 2005 and 2014. He moved to Canada in 2014, having already made regular trips there to play and record.[^tirgan-var][^school-debut][^school-hamid][^school-bookyear][^school-book][^school-greece][^tirgan-move][^ethnotes-travel]

Khavarzamini played on three albums by Jon Balke’s Siwan ensemble. For [*Hafla*](#siwan-hafla-d7p), travel restrictions meant he recorded his part after the other musicians had finished. He also worked with the Toronto–Crete quartet [This Tale of Ours](/en/this-tale-of-ours-rtf/), where his tombak accompanied the tar of Araz Salek and the bowed lyra of Kelly Thoma.[^ecmsiwan-player][^ecmnahnou-player][^ecmhafla-player][^ecmhafla-overdub][^quartet]
