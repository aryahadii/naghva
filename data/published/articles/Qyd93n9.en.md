In Saeid Shanbehzadeh’s performances the audience has a part to play. In Bushehr, he told Radio Farda, a wedding guest can join the singing and anyone can take up the cymbals or drum at a religious gathering. He brings that invitation onstage through clapping, dancing and ululation. At New York’s globalFEST in 2009 he sang, played flute and danced around his goatskin bagpipe while his son Naghib played percussion.[^public][^prague][^global][^bag]

Shanbehzadeh formed his ensemble at twenty and won first prize at Tehran’s Fajr Festival in 1990. In parallel with performing, he collected recordings of southern Iranian music and taught on Kish. In 2001 he joined the French dance company Montalvo/Hervieu for *Babelle Heureuse*. For the later concert *Sound: The Encounter*, he wrote “Mina” with Naghib and Syrian saxophonist Basel Rajoub. Its title means “harbor,” and its gestures evoke the fishermen of the Gulf.[^troupe][^archive][^teaching][^dance][^enc][^mina]

For *Tehran Nights*, Shanbehzadeh and Pedram Derakhshani recorded in Tehran and had the music mixed by Dominique Blanc-Francard in Paris. On “Bada Mobarak” Saeid arranged the music and played saxophone with Brazilian group Funk Como le Gusta, with Naghib on percussion. Father and son also performed together for the French radio programme *Couleurs du monde*.[^tehran][^sax][^radio]
