Hossein Rad is a singer, percussionist and producer from Tehran who lives in Geneva. He first learned singing from his father. He remembers walking to his music school at Chaharrah-e Sepah in central Tehran when he was nearly seven. Learning the radif, the repertoire of Persian classical music, meant learning an instrument too. Rad studied it on the setar with Ali Bayani, and studied singing and the tombak and dayereh drums with Farid Kheradmand.[^s1][^s2][^s3][^s4][^s5]

After moving to Europe in 2005, Rad worked on tombak technique with Djamchid Chemirani and studied Western classical singing with Muriel Ferraro in Paris. Through the ethnomusicologist Jean During he came to explore Central Asian repertoires. In a recording with rubab player Mathieu Clavel, he sings a Mavregi song from Bukhara and accompanies himself on the dayereh. He also founded Horan Sounds to help musicians working in Iran present their music abroad.[^s6][^s7][^s8][^s9][^s10][^s11]

In February 2021 Rad and tar player Sogol Mirzaei recorded a radio concert for Swiss listeners in an empty Geneva venue. Their duo grew into the Rokh Quartet. Looking back on the quartet’s first recording session in Tehran, Rad singled out the song “Bostântchi” as the clearest expression of what they played together. In London he also sang with the quartet’s oud player, Ava Arehsazan, in a programme built around the interplay of her instrument, his voice and percussion.[^s12][^s13][^s14][^s15][^s16][^s17]
