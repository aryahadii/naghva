Roham Sobhani formed the Zhouan Music Ensemble to play Persian classical repertoire and Kurdish melodies. When the musicians toured the Netherlands they traced the group to autumn 2004, though a later history of its protest songs gives 1385 (2006–07). Its name comes from a Sorani Kurdish word for a meeting place for lovers. The musicians wanted an ensemble where the instrumental players had as much say as the singer.[^found][^other][^name][^idea][^roham]

The ensemble won a prize at the Fajr Music Festival in 2008. A concert series in Sanandaj had a different fate. As Sobhani recalled in 2015, the first performance went ahead and the remaining two were cancelled.[^award][^cancel]

Zhouan made protest songs in the years around the 2009 election, including “A Wave of Blood” and “Waiting,” the latter dedicated to Neda Agha-Soltan. In 2011, it performed Kurdish music with singer Hossein Safamanesh. The group later played in Germany, and [Arezoo Banipour](/en/arezoo-banipour-pzd/) sang on its 2018 tour of Germany and the Netherlands. That tour coincided with the release of *Desire to Fly*, which set poetry by Forugh Farrokhzad alongside Kurdish folk songs.[^history][^neda][^march][^german][^tour][^year][^players][^record][^sound][^kurd]

After the November 2019 protests, the ensemble recorded “The Cage Which Traps You” in memory of the victims of the November protests and the downing of the Ukrainian airliner that winter,[^flight752] using Farrokhi Yazdi’s words. On “The Banner of Tresses,” dedicated to Mahsa Amini, Banipour sang words written by Sobhani himself.[^prison][^lyrics][^aman][^banner]
