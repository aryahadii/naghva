Mohsen Namjoo is an Iranian singer-songwriter, setar player and music scholar, born in 1976 in Torbat-e Jam in Khorasan.[^bio][^born-wk] He sets classical Persian poetry and singing against electric guitar and the vocal techniques of rock and blues.[^sound] His first official album, [*Toranj*](#toranj-b8x), came out in 2007, and The New York Times called him “the Bob Dylan of Iran.”[^toranj-poems][^dylan-nyt] He left Iran that year, was sentenced in absentia in 2009 over a song that used verses of the Quran, and spent nearly two decades in the United States before returning to Tehran in August 2026.[^left-2007][^sentence][^flew]

## Early life

Namjoo was born in Torbat-e Jam and grew up in Mashhad.[^born-wk] He began lessons in classical Persian music at twelve, and as a young man also listened to Western musicians such as Jim Morrison, Eric Clapton, Chris de Burgh and Bob Dylan.[^training][^western] He learned the setar eight months before entering university, then studied theatre and music at the University of Tehran, where he fell out with the music department over what he called its “rigid and backward” views.[^setar][^university][^fell-out] He gave his first two concerts, setting Iranian poetry to modern music, in late 1997 and early 1998.[^first-concerts] He wondered why intellectuals kept calling Iranian music behind the times: “The question for me is, where is the art happening in all of this?”[^criticized][^question]

During his military service a guitarist known as Abdi introduced him to the blues, and he began reading the history of blues and rock.[^blues][^military] The music of Robert Johnson and John Lee Hooker reminded him of Iranian village music.[^hooker] In 2002 he and Abdi started a rock band, Maad, but the authorities shut it down.[^maad]

## Underground fame and *Toranj*

His first recordings set a poem by Ahmad Shamlu to the tonbak and a guitar. He made two albums in this style, but they did not pass the censors.[^shamlu] Then the music began to circulate on its own: CDs were copied and passed around among young people, and while he was still a student in Tehran he became an underground star.[^circulate][^tehran]

*Toranj* came out in 2007, made with his friends from Mashhad in Maad, from songs he had written in the year or two before recording.[^toranj-process][^toranj-date] It was his first official album and, by one account, the last to receive a licence. It set poems by Hafez and Rumi to the drive of blues-rock.[^toranj-poems][^last-licence][^toranj-st] In the title song, a classical melody gives way to percussion and piano, and Namjoo almost shouts Hafez’s words.[^toranj-sound] Another song, “Dah e Shast,” looks back at the 1980s, when the hope of the 1979 revolution turned to executions.[^dahe] The same year the government invited him to play at an official event for Imam Ali.[^imam-ali]

## Exile and the sentence

Namjoo left Iran in 2007.[^left-2007] The trouble came from a song built on verses of the Quran, which he called “Chance” in one interview.[^chance][^shams] In 2008 he wrote an apology to his mother, to the clergy and to the Iranian people, saying the recording had never been meant for release.[^sentence] On 9 June 2009 a court sentenced him in absentia to five years in prison for ridiculing the Quran; he was on tour in Italy when he learned he could not go home.[^sentence][^italy] “I’m not religious,” he said later. “But religious people were insulted.”[^judge] That year he also sang in solidarity with the protesters who took to the streets after the disputed presidential election.[^solidarity] His third album, [*Oy*](#oy-jhm), released in 2009, was much more direct than *Toranj*.[^oy][^oy-date]

## Life in the United States

Namjoo settled in the United States in 2008 or 2009 and has toured widely since.[^us-earlier][^us] In 2010 he began his “A Minor” tour, which reached the Walt Disney Concert Hall in Los Angeles, and released “Such Strange Times,” his first song in English, to words by Ahmad Shamlou.[^a-minor][^strange] In February 2011 the Malaysian government cancelled one of his concerts a few days before the show, calling him unsuitable in terms of religion and culture.[^malaysia] “I miss my family,” he said at the time. “Most important thing is my mother.”[^miss]

He released [*Useless Kisses*](#useless-kisses-p5g) in 2011, and two live albums from university concerts: [*Alaki*](#alaki-rdm), from a February 2011 performance at Stanford, and [*13/8*](#13-8-bme), recorded at Berkeley.[^useless][^alaki-performance][^berkeley] From late 2013 he was artist-in-residence at Brown University, where in 2014 he taught “Tradition and Protest: Iranian and Persian Music.”[^residency][^teaching] Living in Brooklyn, he told NPR that he missed his mother, family and friends, but that the idea of Iran was “dead for me.”[^brooklyn][^exile-dead] He described his music as a way of bringing tradition to a generation that was forgetting it.[^mixing] He has also acted in films, including Babak Jalali’s *Radio Dreams* (2016).[^films]

His studio work continued with [*Trust the Tangerine Peel*](#trust-the-tangerine-peel-8s2) (2014) and [*Personal Cipher*](#personal-cipher-4d3) (2016). In 2017 he released [*Voices From the East*](#voices-from-the-east-live-ss3), recorded live with the Nederlands Blazers Ensemble, and [*Axis of Solitude*](#axis-of-solitude-live-85t).[^tangerine][^cipher][^voices][^voices-name][^axis] In 2019 he sang music by the santur player and composer Ehsan Matoori on [*Phantasm*](#phantasm-g9n).[^phantasm][^matoori][^phantasm-roles] For [*Motantan*](#motantan-ang) (2020) he went back to older songs, among them “Khashm Kardam,” which he had played on setar as a student in his early twenties. He added instruments to his usual guitar, bass, drums and setar, and wanted some words to carry a feeling through their sound rather than a fixed meaning.[^motantan-date][^khashm][^ensemble][^words] Later albums include [*Symphonic Odyssey 2020*](#symphonic-odyssey-2020-9yn), [*Odd Time Rock*](#odd-time-rock-d5y) (2022), [*Restless*](#restless-7gh) (2023) and [*Oula*](#oula-s6x) (2024).[^symphonic][^oddtime][^restless][^oula] In December 2025 he released [*Magnetism*](#magnetism-yvf) with [Kābin](/en/kabin-fwf/); by then he had released 19 albums and more than 20 singles.[^magnetism][^magnetism-date][^albums-count]

In 2020 several anonymous allegations of sexual assault were made against him; he denied them and was never charged.[^allegations]

## Return to Iran

On 27 August 2026 Namjoo released a video announcing that he was returning to Iran; it was to be made public once he was on his flight or had landed. He said that at this stage of his life, being home with his family and friends mattered more than whether he could work as a musician.[^video] He flew into Tehran that week, and official media welcomed him.[^flew] He said he had decided “a few months ago” and kept it quiet: “I have come to see my homeland and my family.”[^under-wraps][^homeland] The authorities did not say what had become of his 2009 sentence, or whether he would be allowed to perform or release music in Iran.[^no-explanation][^no-perform]
