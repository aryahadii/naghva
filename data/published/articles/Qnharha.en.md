Omid Nemati is a singer, composer and founding member of the Tehran band [Pallett](/en/pallett-q53/). He sang with [Dang Show](/en/dang-show-8cs/) before Pallett. In his work outside the band, choosing poetry and trying different approaches to singing have been central to his collaborations with composers.[^band][^tehran][^composition][^departure][^poem-first][^voice]

## Learning and voice

Omid grew up hearing Mohammadreza Shajarian and Shahram Nazeri at home. He recalled imitating them from the age of four or five, even when he did not understand the poems. His father took him to music classes in Vanak twice a week, and he learned the daf. He also studied graphic design and made illustrations for children. At sixteen, he wanted to form a band.[^childhood][^learning][^graphics]

He studied classical singing and then sought a way of singing that would incorporate Iranian vocal techniques. That search continued in his work outside Pallett: he drew on what he had learned from classical and modern music, and tried songs with different instruments while working with Sadegh Tasbihi.[^voice][^vocal-choice][^instrumentation]

## Dang Show and Pallett

The friendships and informal music-making that led to Pallett began before its members worked with Dang Show. Omid joined Dang Show with Dariush Azar and Rouzbeh Esfandarmaz, while they continued to share ideas. When the Mahak institute called about an event and Dang Show was unavailable, Omid contacted Esfandarmaz. They brought their ideas together in five days for the performance; three of those songs later appeared on Pallett's debut album. After returning from a Dang Show tour, Omid and his friends decided to pursue Pallett more seriously.[^formation]

Omid described his departure from Dang Show as a disagreement over musical taste, what it meant to work as a group, and the relationship with an audience. In Pallett, members developed ideas together. The bassist Dariush Azar explained that Kaveh Salehi might bring a melody and Omid a poem, which they would then work on as a group.[^departure][^collective]

## Deprivation

His first independent album, *[Deprivation](#deprivation-ca3)*, made with the composer Sadegh Tasbihi, was released in 2017. He described it as a different project from Pallett in both its idea and its form. Tasbihi had not begun with a fixed instrumental lineup in mind; they played and listened to the songs with different instruments as they worked out their shape.[^independent][^deprivation-date][^instrumentation]

Tasbihi described poetry as the starting point. He and Omid read poems, listened to music and talked about them during evenings together and on journeys. They also listened to poets reading their own work, attending to their phrasing, stresses and pauses. Omid recalled their shared wish to sing more substantial poetry. After recording a long piece using a poem by Mehdi Akhavan Sales, they tried to balance fidelity to the poem with the need to draw listeners in.[^poem-first][^poetry-balance]

Reworking older music was part of the project: the title track used the melody of Iraj Amirnezami's earlier song “Maryam, Chera?” Omid found the album demanding to sing. He said that he sometimes felt sad and did not want to carry a social responsibility, but felt that this mood had no place in Pallett. His independent work gave him room to express that part of his experience, while he continued to regard Pallett as the members' priority.[^old-melody][^solo-motive][^pallett-priority]

## Other collaborations

In 2020 Omid worked with Mahyar Alizadeh on *[Istgah](#istgah-tbz)*. Alizadeh sent him possible poems, then composed the music after they had settled on the choices. Omid described the album's sound as emerging through trial and error.[^istgah-date][^istgah-poetry][^istgah-trial]

That year he sang Amin Homaei's compositions on *[Before Autumn After Autumn](#before-autumn-after-autumn-9zj)*, with words by Ehsan Afshari. The poet distinguished two ways of working: he wrote “Nayab” and “Chizi Be Man Begoo” to existing music, while the other poems were selected through discussion with the composer and singer. He saw their connection as a shared atmosphere rather than a common meaning.[^afshari-credits][^before-date][^afshari]

Omid and the composer Ramin Behna released “[Manam](#manam-jcq)” in 2021. Behna composed and arranged the song, and said the recording revealed qualities in Omid's voice that had not appeared in his earlier work. Omid continued working with Homaei, and both sang on *[Eskeleye Chashmanat](#eskeleye-chashmanat-dk8)* in 2024. Homaei wrote the music, with words by Ahura Iman and Omid.[^manam-joint][^manam][^manam-voice][^manam-date][^eskeleye][^eskeleye-date]
