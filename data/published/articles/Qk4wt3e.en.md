Bahram Nouraei, known as Bahram, is an Iranian rapper born in Tehran in 1988. He began making music in Iran’s underground hip-hop scene, became known for the political song “Nameyee be Rayees Jomhoor,” and later settled in Stockholm. His work developed from social criticism toward concept albums about decisions, identity and personal change. In 2019 he helped establish the artist collective Peeleh.[^bio][^nameyee-letter][^move][^good-inspiration][^khodha-inner][^peeleh-network]

## Beginning in Tehran

Bahram grew up with his father’s cassette collection and singing at family gatherings, and his mother’s poetry and novels. He recalled reading Forough Farrokhzad and Sadegh Hedayat as a child.[^family][^singing][^mother][^reading] He and friends made music with downloaded beats in the basement of a friend’s grandmother’s house, using its computer and dial-up internet connection.[^basement][^early-beats]

In 2007 he released “[Nameyee be Rayees Jomhoor](#nameyee-be-rayees-jomhoor-hfq).” He later explained that Ahmadinejad’s promises to leave people’s clothing alone, followed by morality-police arrests, prompted him to write the open letter. He wanted serious social criticism rather than an angry outburst.[^nameyee-priority][^nameyee-subject][^nameyee-promise][^nameyee-arrests][^nameyee-letter][^nameyee-tone] He followed it with *[24 Sa’at](#24-saat-8bh)* in 2008, produced by Atour.[^early-card]

In 2009 he was held briefly in Evin Prison. He subsequently moved to Stockholm.[^prison][^move] His next album, *[Sokoot](#sokoot-e9x)*, appeared in 2011. Looking back, he described a move towards abstraction after the everyday world of *24 Sa’at*.[^silence]

## Sound and storytelling

Many early beats Bahram chose used samples of existing music. He felt familiar sounds helped connect with Iranian listeners, although uncleared samples also caused copyright problems.[^beats][^picked][^familiar][^clearance]

For *[Eshtebahe Khoob](#eshtebahe-khoob-dej)* in 2015, he worked with producer [Mahdyar Aghajani](/en/mahdyar-aghajani-92j/).[^good-card] Watching films including Christopher Nolan’s *Memento* helped him think about its reverse chronology. He used that structure to explore difficult decisions and personal growth.[^nolan][^good-question][^good-mistake][^good-inspiration]

## Peeleh and later work

In 2019 Bahram and the rapper Ghogha founded Peeleh, seeking a network in which musicians across genres and genders could share experience and resources.[^peeleh-cofounder][^peeleh-network] For *[Khodha](#khodha-34h)* in 2023, he wanted listeners to confront suppressed parts of themselves and turn anger into constructive action.[^khodha-intent][^khodha-inner][^khodha-card] The following year he worked with Ali Sorena on *[Khoone Khorshid](#khoone-khorshid-ra4)*, produced by Peymandegar and Ashkan Mousavi.[^collab-card]


He continued this exploration on *[Heech](#heech-vh2)* in 2025. He described *Khodha* as a crowd of conscious and unconscious voices, and *Heech* as an emptied frame, leaving that examination of the self open.[^selves][^nothing]
