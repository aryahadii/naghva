Arsames is a death metal band formed by Ali Madarshahi in Mashhad in 2002. The group draws on ancient Persian history and mythology, combining death metal's distorted guitars, growling vocals and blast beats with Persian scales. Madarshahi began as its drummer and became its vocalist in 2008.[^formation][^basements][^history][^mythology][^sound][^drums][^voice]

The band released its debut album, *[Immortal Identity](#immortal-identity-gk4)*, in 2010 and performed outside Iran. In 2020 its members said they had left the country after a prison sentence. They continued recording, later describing a way of developing riffs on setar before adapting them to electric guitar.[^album][^unirock][^departure][^setar]

## Early years

Arsames began rehearsing in underground basements in Mashhad. Madarshahi later recalled that finding a metal drummer in Iran had been difficult, so he had learned drums before founding the band. In an early lineup he played behind singer Hamed Azizi. Vahid Zehi helped write the lyrics, translated them into English and arranged them with the music.[^basements][^learning][^early-lineup][^zehi]

Madarshahi continued drumming until 2008. He later said that managing the band while playing drums had become difficult, and he passed the instrument to a younger drummer. When the vocalist left around the same time, he took up singing. The band continued to rehearse at home: in 2010 its members said they could prepare for concerts and work on recordings there, although they could not perform live in Iran.[^drums][^voice][^practice]

## Immortal Identity and concerts abroad

The band made Persian history the subject of its songs. Its members described looking to that history for an epic character in their music, while retaining the heavily distorted guitars, deep vocals and fast drumming of death metal. In 2011 they explained that Persian scales and lyrics distinguished their approach; they did not consider it a separate genre.[^history][^sound][^mythology]

Their first album, *[Immortal Identity](#immortal-identity-gk4)*, appeared in 2010. They dedicated it to the heavy metal singer Ronnie James Dio, expressing sympathy for his family and listeners after his death. They also recorded covers by Amon Amarth, Kreator, Carcass and Sepultura on *[Tribute To Warriors of Metal](#tribute-to-warriors-of-metal-tr4)*, and of Slayer's [“Raining Blood”](#raining-blood-rer).[^album][^dio][^covers][^slayer]

Arsames performed at Unirock in Istanbul in 2009, opening for Amon Amarth. In March 2012 they played Metal Mayhem IV at Jawalakhel Ground in Lalitpur, Nepal, where Napalm Death headlined.[^unirock][^nepal][^nepal-date][^nepal-venue][^nepal-headliner]

## Leaving Iran

In August 2020, Arsames told Loudwire that its members had been arrested during a studio rehearsal in 2017. They said they spent nearly a month in detention before being released on bail, with orders to stop working, releasing music and selling merchandise. They denied being a Satanic band.[^arrest][^bail][^denial]

The band said it had resumed activity after its website and Instagram account were shut down. According to the same statement, a later court summons and prison sentence prompted its members to leave Iran.[^departure]

## Later recordings

Arsames returned to Cyrus in its 2024 singles [“My Name Is Cyrus”](#my-name-is-cyrus-q72) and [“Name Man Koroush Ast (Persian)”](#name-man-koroush-ast-persian-k6p). Its later work also brought traditional instruments into the making of the music. In 2026 the band described developing and recording riffs on setar, then adapting those ideas for electric guitar.[^cyrus][^persian-cyrus][^setar]

For [“The Trooper (Traditional)”](#the-trooper-traditional-cf8), released in 2026, Arsames reinterpreted Iron Maiden's song with setar and daf. In its explanation of the recording, the band presented these instruments as a way of reshaping the song through Iranian musical traditions.[^trooper][^trooper-date]
