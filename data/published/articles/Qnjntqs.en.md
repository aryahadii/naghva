Milad Derakhshani is a composer, tar player and singer from Isfahan. He began with instrumental albums and later sang his own settings of Persian poetry, while continuing to write and perform instrumental music. His collaborations range from the Afsharestan ensemble to a duo with Mesbah Ghamsari and improvised concerts with pianist Amir Darabi.[^birth][^first][^first-voice][^group][^duo][^piano]

## Early instrumental work

Milad was born in Isfahan on 15 January 1985. His older brother, the santur player and composer Pedram Derakhshani, helped shape his musical ideas. Tar became his principal instrument; he also plays setar, oud and guitar. He released his first album, *[Afsharestan](#afsharestan-2w4)*, in 2007–08.[^birth][^family][^instruments][^first]

His early instrumental work took several forms. On *[Spree](#spree-mpe)*, he worked within the Persian modes Segah and Afshari, with kamancheh, oud and tombak. For *[Hicham Khabar Az Hich](#hicham-khabar-az-hich-42y)*, he combined tar with didgeridoo and percussion from different regions of Iran. In 2011–12 he founded the Afsharestan ensemble and performed with it.[^spree][^hicham][^group]

## Singing and the stage

In September 2014 Milad released his first vocal album, *[Esharate Nazar](#esharate-nazar-p4x)*. He sang and played acoustic guitar and oud, with a band that included Pedram on santur, Dara Daraee on bass and guest singer Kian Pourtorab on “Beshno.”[^first-voice][^esharat-players][^esharat-instruments]

The title song grew from his attachment to Houshang Ebtehaj’s poetry. He later recalled that the first poetry book he bought was a selection of Ebtehaj’s poems, and that he kept humming “Zaban-e Negah” before composing its setting. At his first concert as a singer, in Tehran in January 2015, he gave some album songs new arrangements for a different instrumental line-up. He told the audience that instrumental music remained his main priority.[^poetry][^concert-date][^concert-arrangement][^concert-priority]

That year he introduced another instrumental album, *[Ta Rahaee](#ta-rahaee-vt6)*, also known as *Afsharestan 2*. He continued working with producer Amirhossein Tabatabaei, who had accompanied him on the preceding album, and included a composition by Pedram.[^afsharestan2][^afsharestan2-year][^continuity][^pedram-composer]

## New collaborations

In 2016 Milad described trying different kinds of composition as he worked towards another vocal album. Sometimes he used tar, sometimes guitar; what mattered to him was the musical thinking rather than the instrument. By the following year he was preparing *[Harf Bezan](#harf-bezan-xhh)*, which he composed and arranged himself, again with Tabatabaei producing.[^writing][^harf]

He was also developing a partnership with the arranger and instrumentalist Mesbah Ghamsari. Their first collaboration brought together tar, bamkaman and harmonica. Milad said that their shared ideas encouraged them to make an album together. On *[Lost in Circles](#lost-in-circles-sr4)*, or *Noghteh Pargar*, he composed and played tar while Ghamsari arranged and played bamkaman and percussion. He won Barbad awards for *Harf Bezan* in vocal fusion and *Lost in Circles* in contemporary instrumental music.[^duo-origin][^duo][^awards]

In 2018 he composed “[Faryad](#faryad-xtt),” a setting of poetry by Shafiei Kadkani, and sang it with [Haleh Seyfizadeh](/en/haleh-seyfizadeh-wdt/).[^faryad]

## Pandemic recordings and improvisation

Discussing “Sayeh Rokh” in 2021, Milad described tar as a source of relief. He said that each piece needed its own approach to technique, depending on its genre and atmosphere. As a left-handed player, he had to think differently about phrasing and expression on the instrument.[^practice]

He began recording *[Mobham e Door](#mobham-e-door-g4k)* during the pandemic. He described solitude, silence, shock and uncertainty about the future as experiences that had changed both his music and his outlook on life. He released the album digitally in 2021.[^pandemic][^mobham-date]

In 2023 he and Seyfizadeh released “[Ayeneh](#ayeneh-jxp).” Later that year he and Amir Darabi performed *Abi Khayal*, a programme of tar and piano improvisation, at Tehran’s Roudaki Hall. Milad traced the project to their long friendship and renewed contact after Darabi returned to Iran. Darabi compared the improvisations to sitting together and telling stories.[^ayeneh][^piano][^piano-origin][^piano-process]

Milad revisited his vocal repertoire on the 2025 EP *[Milad Derakhshani (live)](#milad-derakhshani-live-e2h)*, with live versions of “Beshno,” “Harf Bezan” and “Esharate Nazar.”[^live-date][^live-beshno][^live-harf][^live-esharat]
