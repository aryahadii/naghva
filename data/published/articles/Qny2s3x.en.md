Abjeez began with two sisters writing songs in their bedroom. Safoura Safavi would come up with a melody and Melody Safavi would fit words to it. Born in Iran, they formed the band in Sweden in 2005 and named it after *abjee*, a familiar Persian word for a sister.[^write][^birth][^sister]

## Writing across borders

Persian is the language the sisters sing in most often. Their arrangements draw on ska and reggae as well as flamenco and rock. They chose to run the band themselves so that decisions about the music would stay in their hands.[^language][^style][^diy] Living on different continents also shaped the way they made their videos. A tour or a family visit gave them time together to shoot footage, often with friends behind the camera.[^distance][^video]

The American travel ban brought back memories for Melody of what it had taken to cross borders as an Iranian. She turned those memories into the words for “[Crossing Borders](#crossing-borders-csw).”[^border]

## From the team to the stands

Two of the sisters’ songs, “Hameh Baayad Bedoonan” and “Merci Team Melli,” cheered on Iran’s national football team. After hearing them, women’s rights activists approached Abjeez with a different request. Would they write about the women who wanted to watch their team from the stands?[^football2][^football] In the video for “Stadium,” a woman and a man wear the national team’s shirts and dance an acrobatic duet. The song asks male fans to back women’s campaign to enter the stadium.[^dance][^football]
