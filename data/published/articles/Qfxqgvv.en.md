Shadi Fathi is an Iranian setar player from a Kurdish family in Tehran. She began performing as a soloist at fifteen. After moving to France in 2002, she worked with musicians, dancers and theatre companies. With the percussionist Bijan Chemirani she recorded *[Delâshena](#delashena-9wn)* and *[Âwât](#awat-dxd)*, two albums in which she also recites Persian poetry.[^born][^solo][^france][^meet][^poems][^recite][^date-del][^date-awat]

## The setar and the drum

Fathi studied setar with Dariush Talai in Tehran. She went on to learn two drums, the zarb with Arash Farhangfar and the daf with Mehrdad Karim-Khavari. She met Chemirani in Marseille in 2016. In January 2018 they recorded *Delâshena* in studios in Château-Arnoux and Salon-de-Provence. Hossein Cheraghi made both of her string instruments for the record, the setar and the shurangiz. Fathi’s voice opens and closes the album. She plays daf on two pieces in between.[^teacher][^daf][^meet][^studios][^studios-two][^strings][^voice][^date-del]

## Compagnie Persiana

In 2018 Compagnie Persiana formed in Marseille with Fathi as its artistic director. The company released *Âwât* under licence to Buda Musique in 2022. The Kurdish title means “great desire”. The duo invited Redi Hasa to play cello and Shervin Mohajer to play kamancheh. On five of the pieces Fathi reads poetry, including verses by Khayyam and Rumi.[^org][^direct][^license][^date-awat][^kurat][^guests][^recite]

For her stage work *Azadi*, Fathi drew on a visit to Salento in southern Italy. She heard an echo of her own experience of surviving war in the persistence of the local Griko language. In the performance Ninfa Giannuzzi sings in Griko while Egidio Marullo paints in watercolours onstage.[^north][^legacy][^griko][^paint]
